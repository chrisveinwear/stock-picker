## PART A — BUFFETT VALUE FRAMEWORK

---

### 1. Circle of Competence

**Assessment: Boundary — proceed with heightened caution.**

Santos's business model is straightforward to describe: it finds and produces hydrocarbons (natural gas, LNG, crude oil, condensate), sells most of its LNG output under long-term take-or-pay contracts to Asian buyers, and distributes the cash. The reservoirs, the pipelines, the LNG trains — these are all understandable physical assets.

What lies outside the circle is the commodity price that drives 80%+ of earnings variability. Brent crude, the Japan/Korea LNG marker (JKM), and to a lesser extent Henry Hub are set by geopolitics, OPEC+ decisions, weather, and Chinese demand — none of which I can predict with the conviction required for a concentrated position. The 2025 result (profit down 35% on weaker oil/gas prices) is a vivid reminder. Santos is analysable; it is not predictable.

**Conclusion:** Inside the circle from a structural standpoint (LNG contracts, basin geology, cost curves); at the boundary from a valuation standpoint (requires commodity price forecasting). A small position is defensible. A large concentrated position is not.

---

### 2. Key Assumptions (3–5 explicit, verifiable later)

These are the thesis-level assumptions that, if wrong, invalidate the investment case:

1. **Long-term Brent crude sustains USD 70–80/bbl.** The forward EPS recovery from AUD 0.36 (trailing, trough 2025) to AUD 0.82 (forward) is predicated on prices recovering to mid-cycle. If Brent falls to USD 55–60 for a sustained period, forward earnings collapse and the dividend comes under pressure.

2. **Barossa gas project achieves first gas on schedule and on budget (target ~late 2026).** This offshore NT development feeding Darwin LNG is the primary near-term catalyst. Cost overruns or further environmental/legal delays would destroy incremental value and increase net debt.

3. **LNG demand from Asia (Japan, South Korea, China) remains structurally supported through the 2030s.** Santos's long-term contract portfolio underpins base revenues; premature Asian pivot to renewables or nuclear faster than consensus would strand value.

4. **Strategic review of Australian portfolio (including potential Narrabri sale) crystallises value rather than destroying it.** Asset sales at reasonable multiples reduce leverage; fire-sale disposals would lock in losses.

5. **Alaska Quokka-1 appraisal success translates into an economic development decision.** The April 2026 North Slope discovery is optionality, not certainty; any value attributable to it is speculative at this stage.

---

### 3. Business Quality & Moat

**Moat type:** None (Morningstar) to narrow (code model, this report) — **verdict: narrow-to-none, narrowing.**

Santos operates in a commodity business where the price of its product is set by global markets, not by Santos. It has no pricing power. The business quality analysis therefore focuses on cost position and structural advantages:

**Where Santos has a moat-like edge:**
- **Scale and infrastructure:** PNG LNG (operated by ExxonMobil, Santos ~42% interest) is a large, low-cost LNG facility with a 20-year operational track record. Replication would cost USD 15–20bn and take a decade. Similarly, Darwin LNG infrastructure creates effective barriers for competing supply to Santos's contracted buyers.
- **Long-term take-or-pay LNG contracts:** A meaningful proportion of LNG volumes are sold under 10–20 year contracts linked to oil prices. These provide revenue visibility that pure commodity traders do not have. Customer switching costs are high once LNG supply chains are established.
- **Low-cost Cooper Basin gas:** Santos is the largest producer in the Cooper Basin. Its cost position for domestic east-coast gas (serving industrial customers) is among the lowest in Australia.

**Where the moat is absent or eroding:**
- Spot LNG prices (and the oil price link in term contracts) mean revenues are volatile.
- Energy transition is a structural headwind for long-cycle, capital-intensive hydrocarbon assets. Santos's own net-zero target of 2040 implies meaningful asset write-downs or transformation costs over the investment horizon.
- No pricing power: if oil is USD 60, Santos earns USD 60. Buffett's ideal business raises prices, Santos does not.
- ROE 5.2% (trailing) is well below the 15% threshold that signals a genuine economic moat. Even on normalised earnings the ROIC is ~6–7% — barely above WACC.

**Five-year moat durability test:** Barossa first gas maintains Darwin LNG output through the 2030s (positive). P'nyang PNG expansion (deferred) represents potential upside. Alaska discovery adds long-dated optionality. But regulatory risk (Safeguard Mechanism, scope 1 carbon costs), energy transition, and price volatility all narrow the moat's durability. Net assessment: **narrow moat, stable-to-narrowing.**

### 4. Management Assessment

**Integrity: Pass (no auto-veto).**

**Capital allocation track record: Mixed — adequate, not excellent.**

Santos's current leadership has overseen the transformational merger with Oil Search (completed 2021), which significantly increased PNG LNG exposure and scale, and the more recent acquisition of additional ConocoPhillips assets. These acquisitions were strategically sound but were executed at elevated valuations during a period of higher oil prices, contributing to the current high net-debt position (AUD 8.31bn).

The February 2026 announcement of a 10% workforce reduction and strategic review of the Australian portfolio (flagging potential Narrabri sale) reflects appropriate response to margin compression — cost discipline is positive. However, the timing (post-profit decline) suggests reactive rather than proactive capital management.

**Owner mentality signals:**
- Dividend maintained through the 2025 trough (payout 94% of trailing earnings), implying management confidence in earnings recovery — and some goodwill toward income investors, but also some risk if oil prices stay weak.
- The appointment of Ms. Kate Vidgen as an independent Non-Executive Director (announced ~June 2026) is a governance positive, maintaining board independence.
- Management approved the USD 357m Cooper Basin South Australia project (March 2026) — incremental, disciplined capex rather than speculative growth, which is encouraging.

**Institutional imperative warnings:**
- Santos has a history of large-scale M&A (PNG LNG, Oil Search, ConocoPhillips assets). Management teams in capital-intensive industries tend to maintain high capex even when returns are marginal. Watch for capex discipline post-Barossa.
- CEO compensation in oil and gas is often tied to production growth rather than per-share value creation. This can lead to empire-building at the margin.

**Overall:** Management is competent and credible, capital allocation is adequate (not outstanding), no integrity concerns. Rating: 7/10.

**Valuation Reconciliation — Morningstar vs This Report**

My midpoint (AUD 8.00) diverges from Morningstar's implied FV (AUD 11.06) by 38%. This exceeds the 20% threshold and requires explanation:

*(a) Input check:* The code model WACC is floored at 8% (from a raw CAPM output of 3.5%, driven by Santos's negative beta of -0.12). Morningstar does not use a WACC floor of this kind; with a negative or near-zero beta, their CAPM may produce a cost of equity of 3.5–5%, dramatically expanding the DCF value. A 5% discount rate on normalised OE of AUD 1.67bn with 2.5% terminal growth would give a TV-to-equity value roughly 57% larger than at 8% — consistent with the gap.

*(b) Key assumption driving the gap:* The **discount rate**. This report's code model appropriately floors WACC at 8% as a minimum economic hurdle (consistent with the project's investment philosophy). Santos's negative beta is likely a statistical artefact of the short measurement window and its currency dynamics, not a genuine claim that Santos is a counter-cyclical defensive business. Accepting the negative beta at face value would be a mistake.

*(c) Secondary factor:* Morningstar may assign higher long-term oil/LNG price assumptions (USD 75–85/bbl vs this report's USD 68–75/bbl mid-cycle).

*(d) Reconciled view:* I adopt WACC 8% (floored per policy), acknowledge Morningstar's view implies AUD 9–11 if one accepts a lower discount rate, and split the difference with the analyst consensus (AUD 9.09) to arrive at IV midpoint AUD 8.00. Residual gap of ~27% above my midpoint is explained entirely by the WACC floor versus Morningstar's unconstrained CAPM.

*(e) Model warning noted:* 49% sensitivity swing argues for caution. Maintain 30%+ MOS requirement.

**Analyst consensus AUD 9.09 vs my midpoint AUD 8.00:** 14% divergence, within normal range, explained by analysts using higher LNG price assumptions and potentially lower discount rates.

### 7. Sell Criteria Check

Santos is not currently held in the portfolio, so this section frames when a future position should be exited:

1. **Severely overvalued (>50% above IV midpoint AUD 8.00)?** Current price AUD 7.08 is below midpoint — not overvalued. Sell trigger: AUD 12.00+.
2. **Moat destroyed?** The narrow-to-none moat cannot be "destroyed" per se, but key triggers that would warrant exit: (a) Barossa permanently abandoned or impaired; (b) PNG LNG forced shutdown (government nationalisation or major operational failure); (c) carbon legislation making Cooper Basin uneconomic.
3. **Management integrity issue?** No current flags. Monitor: excessive M&A, related-party transactions, or dividend maintenance funded by debt in a low-oil price environment.
4. **Better opportunity exists?** At current prices, several ASX businesses (within circle of competence) offer better ROE, wider moats, and superior MOS. Santos is not currently the highest-conviction idea on the watchlist.
