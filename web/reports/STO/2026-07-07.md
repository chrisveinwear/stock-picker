## PART A — BUFFETT VALUE FRAMEWORK

### 1. Circle of Competence

Santos sits **at the boundary** of the circle of competence. The core business model is understandable in outline: extract natural gas from large, long-life reservoirs, liquefy it at coastal facilities, and sell it to Asian utilities under 10–20-year take-or-pay contracts indexed to oil prices. Infrastructure is expensive to replicate, customers are locked in, and the assets have multi-decade operating lives.

Where the boundary is reached: the underlying LNG and crude oil commodity prices resist precise forecasting, project-level execution risk in frontier environments (Papua New Guinea, Alaska North Slope) can destroy capital silently, and the energy transition introduces a structural demand headwind that becomes increasingly material beyond 2030. The newly acquired Pikka Alaska crude oil position adds a separate commodity exposure and frontier operating risk that is further from a domestic investor's circle.

**Assessment:** Boundary. Proceed with full analysis, but apply a wider margin of safety (minimum 30%, prefer 35%) to compensate for commodity-price and execution uncertainty.

### 2. Key Assumptions (3–5)

The thesis depends on the following verifiable assumptions. Each should be checked at every quarterly production report and H1/FY result:

1. **Pikka Alaska ramps successfully to design production.** First oil at Pikka was achieved in mid-2026. The thesis requires Phase 1 to reach approximately 80,000 bopd by FY2027-2028. A delay of 12 or more months pushes the earnings and FCF recovery narrative materially back and is a potential SELL trigger.

2. **Brent crude and Asian LNG prices remain above Santos's all-in cost floor.** Santos requires roughly USD 45–50/bbl Brent equivalent to sustain the current dividend and fund essential capex. A sustained decline below USD 50/bbl would force either a dividend cut or accelerated debt growth — both are share-price-negative events.

3. **Capex normalises post-major-project phase.** FY2024-2025 capex averaged USD 2.2bn per year. The investment thesis requires capex to decline toward USD 1.4–1.6bn by FY2027 as Pikka transitions from construction to production, generating the step-change FCF improvement embedded in analyst forward estimates.

4. **PNG LNG continues to operate reliably.** PNG LNG is Santos's highest-margin asset and the primary engine of current free cash flow. PNG sovereign and political risk is perpetual and manageable, but a prolonged operational disruption would materially impair near-term cash generation.

5. **The strategic review of the Australian portfolio produces value-accretive outcomes.** The February 2026 review announcement signals a potential portfolio rationalisation. Value is only created if asset disposals occur at fair or above-fair prices and proceeds are applied to debt reduction. A fire-sale or a prolonged strategic distraction would be a negative.

### 3. Business Quality & Moat

**Moat type:** Infrastructure / switching costs
**Moat strength:** Narrow
**Moat trend:** Stable, structural headwind beyond 2030

**Assessment:** Santos's competitive position rests on two interlocking advantages. First, its portfolio of long-term LNG offtake contracts with Japanese, Korean, and Chinese utilities. These are decade-long take-or-pay agreements; buyers have co-invested in regasification infrastructure calibrated to Santos's LNG specifications, making mid-contract switching costly. Second, Santos owns stakes in large-scale LNG liquefaction trains — Darwin LNG, GLNG (Gladstone), and PNG LNG — that represent infrastructure with replacement costs measured in the tens of billions of dollars and permitting timelines measured in decades. New entrants cannot easily replicate these positions.

These two factors combine for a narrow moat on a 5-year horizon. However, Morningstar's independent assessment is **No Moat**, and this view deserves serious weight. Santos is ultimately a price-taking commodity producer: LNG pricing is indexed to JCC (Japanese Crude Cocktail), and Santos cannot sustainably sell above commodity benchmarks regardless of its infrastructure. Expanding global LNG supply from the US Gulf Coast, Qatar's North Field expansion, and East African projects continuously erodes Santos's pricing leverage at contract renewals.

**Five-year moat durability test:** Most of Santos's key long-term LNG contracts run through the late 2030s and 2040s, providing revenue visibility within the investment horizon. The moat is durable at the asset level (you cannot replace Darwin LNG or PNG LNG) but contestable at the contract-renewal level as the energy transition reduces buyers' appetite for multi-decade fossil fuel commitments. The net assessment is that the narrow moat persists within a 5-year horizon but is unlikely to be wider at the end of that period than it is today.

### 4. Management Assessment

**Integrity threshold:** Pass — no material governance red flags identified.

**Leadership context:** Santos's CEO search has been a visible distraction; a permanent appointment to replace the departed CEO would reduce the management discount embedded in the current share price. The board's decision to maintain the dividend despite a 35% FY25 profit decline signals income-investor prioritisation, which may be strategically correct as a signal of confidence in the Pikka recovery but reduces near-term financial flexibility.

**Capital allocation track record:** Mixed and under scrutiny. The 2021 Oil Search acquisition was transformational, adding PNG and Alaska exposure at a price that now looks reasonable in hindsight. However, the subsequent multi-year capital programme — USD 2.0–2.4bn per year for three consecutive years — has been executed while revenues and earnings fell sharply. Total debt grew from USD 5,519m (FY2022) to USD 7,540m (FY2025), a 37% increase over a period when net income declined 61%. Capital was being invested aggressively precisely when the commodity cycle turned against Santos. That sequencing is concerning, even if the underlying projects (particularly Pikka) are sound.

The failed ADNOC/XRG takeover bid (announced 2024, withdrawn 2025) is an ambiguous signal. Management engaged with the offer, which was appropriate fiduciary behaviour. The bidder's withdrawal suggests the process did not produce a price that reflected intrinsic value. The aftermath — a sharp share price decline — has reset the entry point but also confirmed the share remains unloved.

**Workforce reduction:** The February 2026 announcement of a 10% workforce cut and strategic portfolio review is the right structural response to the earnings compression. These actions would have been more confidence-inspiring three years ago; at this point they represent catching up to necessity rather than proactive leadership.

**Owner mentality score:** Moderate. Dividend maintained, share buyback activity limited in available data, capex discipline improving but late.

**Institutional imperative warnings:** Pikka is the latest in a series of large growth projects. The pattern of pursuing ambitious growth capital programmes during benign commodity cycles (when capital is cheap) is a classic institutional imperative risk. Watch for any new growth project announcement before Pikka's FCF is proven.
