## PART A — BUFFETT VALUE FRAMEWORK

### 1. Circle of Competence

**Inside.** Telecommunications is a business model Warren Buffett would recognise: a regulated, infrastructure-like utility with recurring revenue, high barriers to entry, and predictable cash flows. Spark is New Zealand's largest telco (~40% mobile revenue share, ~35% broadband), owning the critical "last mile" copper/fibre network and spectrum assets. The economics are transparent — revenue per user, churn, capex intensity, and regulatory regime are all analysable. The NZ market is a rational three-player oligopoly (Spark, One NZ [formerly Vodafone], 2degrees) with limited threat of a fourth entrant. This sits comfortably within the circle of competence for any investor who understands utilities, railways, or pipelines.

### 2. Key Assumptions (3–5)

The investment thesis rests on these explicit, testable assumptions:

1. **SPK30 strategy execution:** The "simplify, modernise, grow" plan (announced Aug 2025) delivers NZD 100m+ annualised cost savings by FY27 without impairing network quality or customer experience. *Verification: track opex/revenue ratio and NPS scores quarterly.*
2. **Dividend sustainability:** The FY25 DPS of NZD 0.17 (implied ~10% yield at AUD 1.68) is maintained, supported by FCF conversion >70% of net income. *Verification: monitor FCF/net income ratio and payout ratio vs. 80% ceiling.*
3. **Leverage de-risking:** Net debt/EBITDA trends below 3.0x by FY27 (currently ~2.8x on trailing EBITDA NZD 840m vs net debt NZD 2.26bn) via asset sales (towers, data centres) or retained cash flow. *Verification: quarterly debt trajectory and asset monetisation announcements.*
4. **Regulatory stability:** No adverse shift in Commerce Commission fibre pricing (current UFB anchor prices locked until 2030) or mobile termination rates. *Verification: monitor Commerce Commission determinations.*
5. **AUD/NZD stability:** FX rate holds near 0.91 AUD/NZD (implied from market cap vs. NZD equity). A 5% NZD depreciation reduces AUD-equivalent FCF by ~NZD 18m. *Verification: track FX and hedge ratio.*

### 3. Business Quality & Moat

**Moat Type:** **Narrow** (Morningstar concurs). **Strength:** Narrow. **Trend:** **Stable to Narrowing.**

| Moat Source | Assessment |
|-------------|------------|
| **Switching Costs** | Moderate — mobile number portability takes hours; broadband switching 1-2 days. Enterprise contracts 2-3 years. But low differentiation on commodity plans. |
| **Intangible Assets** | Spectrum holdings (700/900/1800/2100/2300/2600 MHz + 3.5 GHz 5G) are scarce, government-granted licences. Brand value high in NZ consumer/enterprise. |
| **Cost Advantage** | Scale in procurement, network ops, and marketing spend. But wholesale fibre input costs (Chorus/UFB) are regulated and identical for all RSPs — no structural cost edge on ~40% of broadband COGS. |
| **Network Effect** | Weak — value of Spark's network to a user doesn't increase materially with more Spark users (unlike social platforms). |
| **Efficient Scale** | **Strongest source.** NZ population 5.2m supports ~3 viable national mobile networks. High fixed costs (spectrum, towers, core) deter new entrants. Three-player oligopoly with rational pricing discipline (no price wars since 2degrees entry 2009). |

**Five-Year Moat Durability Test:**
- **Threat 1: Fixed-wireless access (FWA) / LEO satellite (Starlink).** Starlink NZ retail ~NZD 159/mo vs. Spark fibre ~NZD 90-110. Niche for rural/remote; not a mass-market substitute at current pricing. *Moat impact: Low.*
- **Threat 2: Wholesale fibre price increases.** Commerce Commission's final pricing principle (FPP) review could raise UFB input costs. Spark passes through ~70-80% to retail. *Moat impact: Moderate — compresses broadband margin.*
- **Threat 3: Mobile margin erosion from MVNOs.** One NZ and 2degrees host MVNOs; Spark has resisted. If Spark opens wholesale, mobile ARPU pressure. *Moat impact: Moderate — watch for strategic shift.*
- **Threat 4: Technology disruption (network slicing, neutral host).** Neutral host models (e.g., TowerCo spinouts) could commoditise passive infrastructure. Spark already sold 70% of towers to Infratil/CPPIB (2021). *Moat impact: Low-moderate — already partially crystallised.*

**Conclusion:** A narrow moat anchored in efficient scale and spectrum scarcity, but vulnerable to regulatory wholesale pricing and the slow commoditisation of connectivity. Not widening.
