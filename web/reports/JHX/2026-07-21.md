## PART A — BUFFETT VALUE FRAMEWORK

### 1. Circle of Competence

**Boundary.** The core business — fibre-cement exterior siding, trim and backer-board sold into US, Australian and NZ new-construction and repair-and-remodel (R&R) markets — is simple to explain: a manufacturing-scale, building-code-embedded product with genuine switching and cost advantages over wood and vinyl. That part is squarely inside the circle.

What pushes this to the boundary is FY2026: total debt jumped from USD 1,208m to USD 4,786m and equity from USD 2,162m to USD 6,426m in a single year, while revenue rose 24.7% (USD 3,878m → 4,836m) and net income collapsed 75.5% (USD 424m → 104m). This is the balance-sheet signature of a large, debt-and-equity-funded acquisition (James Hardie's purchase of the AZEK Company, a US composite-decking maker, is publicly known and consistent with this pattern), not organic deterioration. Understanding the *combined* entity — fibre cement plus composite decking, under materially higher leverage — requires judging integration execution and capital allocation on a scale James Hardie management hasn't previously operated at. I'm proceeding, but flagging this boundary explicitly rather than treating it as core-competence territory.

### 2. Key Assumptions (3–5)

1. AZEK integration delivers targeted synergies without material cost overruns or channel disruption in the decking business.
2. US repair-and-remodel demand normalizes as rates ease, supporting mid-single-digit organic volume growth in fibre cement.
3. Fibre cement continues taking share from vinyl and wood siding at a similar pace to its historical trend.
4. Net debt (~AUD 6.44bn per the valuation model) is paid down over 3–5 years without a dilutive follow-on raise or distressed refinancing.
5. No re-emergence of legacy asbestos-compensation liabilities beyond the existing funded scheme (AICF) that would impair capital available for debt reduction.

### 3. Business Quality & Moat

**Moat type:** Cost/scale (manufacturing footprint, distribution density, building-code specification) plus a modest intangible/brand component (contractor and builder preference for "Hardie board"). **Strength:** Wide in the standalone fibre-cement business (affirmed independently by Morningstar's Wide rating) — narrower and less proven in the newly-combined decking segment, where AZEK competes with Trex on different economics. **Trend:** Stable-to-widening in core siding; temporarily clouded at the group level by the diversification and leverage step-up.

**Five-year moat durability test:** Fibre cement's advantages — high fixed-capital manufacturing barriers, entrenched distributor relationships, building-code embedding that raises switching costs for builders — are durable and unlikely to erode within five years absent a technological substitute. The decking segment's moat durability is less tested under JHX ownership; the honest answer is "not yet demonstrated."
