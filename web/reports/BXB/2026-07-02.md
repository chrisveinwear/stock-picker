## PART A — BUFFETT VALUE FRAMEWORK

### 1. Circle of Competence
**Inside.** Brambles operates the world's largest pooling equipment business (pallets, crates, containers) under the CHEP brand. The model is elegantly simple: customers rent rather than buy pallets; Brambles manages the repair, retrieval, and redistribution network. This is a capital-light, asset-turnover business with high switching costs and network density advantages — squarely within the "infrastructure/industrial services" circle of competence. The US pallet repair bottleneck disclosed in May 2026 is operational, not conceptual; it does not push the business outside the circle.

### 2. Key Assumptions (3–5)
1. **US pallet repair capacity normalises by H2 FY27** — The FY26 guidance cut stems from repair labour/asset constraints; management's US$400m buyback signals confidence this is temporary.
2. **Structural pricing power persists** — CHEP's ~60%+ market share in major geographies and ~3–4 year customer contracts allow mid-single-digit price increases to offset cost inflation.
3. **Capital intensity declines** — Capex peaked at US$1.7bn (FY22–23); FY24 capex fell to US$1.15bn. We assume maintenance capex ~US$0.9–1.0bn/year, freeing incremental FCF.
4. **No disruptive substitution** — Plastic pallets, slip-sheets, and automated warehouses have nibbled at the edges for 20 years; wood pooled pallets remain the low-cost standard for FMCG supply chains.
5. **FX stability** — Reporting currency is USD; we assume AUD/USD ≈ 0.67 (current spot). A 10% AUD appreciation reduces AUD earnings ~10%.

### 3. Business Quality & Moat
**Moat type:** Switching costs (primary) + Scale/Network effect (secondary)  
**Strength:** **Wide** (Morningstar agrees)  
**Trend:** **Stable** — US repair strain is a near-term operational headwind, not a structural moat impairment.

**Five-year moat durability test:**
| Factor | Assessment |
|--------|------------|
| Customer captivity | High — CHEP pallets are embedded in retailer/manufacturer supply chains; switching requires retooling automation, renegotiating contracts, and building alternative retrieval networks. |
