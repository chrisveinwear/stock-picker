## PART A — BUFFETT VALUE FRAMEWORK

### 1. Circle of Competence

**Inside.** Brambles' CHEP business is one of the most conceptually simple models in global industrials: it owns a pool of wooden pallets and containers, lends them to manufacturers and retailers across closed-loop supply chains, and charges a rental fee per trip. The economics require no specialist knowledge — the business earns per-unit pricing power from network scale, collects deposits and fees, and recycles assets continuously. The reinvestment logic (build scale, deepen network density, charge incrementally more per trip) is entirely legible.

The complexity lies in geography and contract structure, not in the business model itself. Operating across 60+ countries and ~330 service centres, Brambles achieves pooling efficiency that an isolated pallet owner cannot replicate. The business can be explained to a twelve-year-old: you rent out pallets instead of selling them, and you get them back. That simplicity masks a formidable competitive position.

**Classification:** Inside the circle of competence.

---

### 2. Key Assumptions (Thesis Depends On)

1. **Pricing power endures.** CHEP's rental contracts embed annual price escalations. The thesis assumes customers continue renewing rather than reverting to white-wood (buy-use-discard) ownership — a risk that rises if commodity pallet prices collapse.

2. **FCF normalises higher as capex moderates.** Capex fell sharply from USD 1,685m (FY2023) to USD 1,149m (FY2024), driving the FCF recovery from USD 307m to USD 655m. The thesis depends on this normalisation persisting, not reversing toward growth-capex peaks.

3. **Revenue growth re-accelerates from FY2026 onwards.** FY2025 revenue grew only 1.8% — a sharp deceleration from the 8.5% two-year CAGR through FY2024. The model embeds 11.4% stage-1 earnings growth derived from analyst forward estimates; if volume headwinds from US tariff uncertainty and the May 2026 guidance cut persist, this assumption is at risk.

4. **Subcontractor exit delivers structural margin uplift.** Goldman Sachs upgraded BXB specifically on the impact of exiting white-label subcontracting. The thesis requires this structural change to translate into sustained operating margin expansion above the current 21.8%.

5. **US$400m buyback is completed at depressed prices.** The buyback announced May 2026 at ~AUD 17.72 accrets ~2.4% per share if executed at or below current prices. Completion risk is low for an investment-grade borrower, but cancellation would remove an EPS tailwind.

### 3. Business Quality & Moat

**Moat type:** Cost advantage + switching costs + network effects
**Moat strength:** Wide
**Moat trend:** Stable, with mild widening potential as digital pallet-tracking deepens customer integration

The CHEP network is a near-natural monopoly in pooled pallet logistics. The economics compound at each additional service centre: more pick-up points reduce pallet travel distance, improve asset utilisation, and lower per-unit cost — advantages a new entrant cannot replicate without decades of capital spending and customer relationships. The 2024 EBITDA margin of approximately 31.6% (USD 2.07bn EBITDA on USD 6.55bn revenue) and ROE of 28.1% are the quantitative fingerprints of durable competitive advantage.

**Switching costs** are structural. A manufacturer shifting from CHEP to a competitor or white-wood would need to: (1) renegotiate distribution contracts that specify pallet compatibility, (2) absorb the capital cost of pallet ownership, (3) retrain logistics staff, and (4) rebuild reverse-logistics for pallet collection. These costs are rarely worth bearing for cost savings that are marginal at scale.

**Five-year moat durability test:** The primary threat over five years is plastic pallets (lighter, RFID-compatible, longer lifespan) which could shift customer economics. Brambles has invested in plastic pallet options through its Brambles Industries platforms. The risk is manageable and the transition, if it occurs, will be slow. Autonomous logistics and RFID integration could actually deepen switching costs if BXB leads the digitalisation of pallet pooling. Net assessment: moat intact for five years with high confidence.

- Strength: No rental commitment; lower perceived per-trip cost for high-volume, simple routes

- Opportunity: Price-sensitive customers in inflationary environments
