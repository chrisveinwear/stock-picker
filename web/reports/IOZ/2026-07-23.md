## PART A — BUFFETT VALUE FRAMEWORK

### 1. Circle of Competence

IOZ is not an operating business — it is a passive, physically-replicating exchange-traded fund managed by BlackRock/iShares that holds the ~200 constituents of the S&P/ASX 200 Index in market-cap-weighted proportions. The analytical question is therefore different from analysing a single company: instead of "can I explain how this business makes money," the question is "can I explain how this *vehicle* delivers the index's return, and is the aggregate index cheap or dear."

Both are simply explainable: IOZ charges a low management fee (publicly disclosed by iShares as ~0.05% p.a. — **assumption, not in the provided dataset**, flagged as such) to hold the ASX 200 in-specie, distribute dividends received, and track the benchmark before fees. There is no moat, product cycle, or management strategy to misjudge at the fund level — the only judgement required is on the *index itself*: its constituent quality (heavily bank- and miner-weighted), and whether the aggregate market multiple (P/E 20.6x trailing) reflects fair value. This sits **inside** circle of competence — it is index/vehicle economics, not single-business moat analysis, and Buffett himself has repeatedly endorsed low-cost broad index funds as the default vehicle for investors who don't want to underwrite individual moats.

### 3. Business Quality & Moat

There is no company-level moat to assess. The relevant "moat" is the **fund structure's** durability: (a) scale — IOZ is one of the largest and most liquid ASX-listed broad-market ETFs, which itself is a modest scale/liquidity moat (tight bid-ask spreads, deep creation/redemption); (b) an intangible-asset-style moat in the iShares/BlackRock brand and distribution relationships that keep flows sticky; (c) a **structural fee moat** — once an index fund is priced near-zero, competitors can only compete on fee, and IOZ already sits at the low end of published ASX ETF fee schedules (assumption, not in supplied data).

Moat strength: **narrow, stable** — commoditised passive-index products face constant fee compression risk from newer entrants (e.g. Betashares A200), so durability is a function of scale/liquidity advantage, not a wide, defensible moat. Five-year durability test: passes on liquidity/scale, but the fee-compression trend across the ETF industry means today's cost advantage is not permanent.

### 6. Valuation & Margin of Safety

Because IOZ is an open-ended, physically-backed ETF, its market price is continuously arbitraged by authorised participants against underlying NAV — meaning, unlike a single stock, there is essentially **no persistent pricing gap to exploit at the fund-wrapper level**. The only "mispricing" question that matters is whether the **underlying index** is cheap or expensive relative to a value investor's required return. Three methods:

**a) Owner-earnings / Gordon-growth proxy (in place of a full DCF):** Using trailing EPS of AUD 1.71 as the earnings-yield base, required return r = 9.5–10.0%, and assumed long-run nominal earnings growth g = 4.0–4.5% (assumption #2 above): fair earnings yield = r − g ≈ 5.0–6.0%, implying a fair aggregate P/E of ~16.7x–20.0x, or a fair value of **AUD 28.5 – 34.2**. This band is highly sensitive to the r−g spread (a hallmark of Gordon-growth models near a narrow spread), so it is treated as a lower-anchor estimate.

**b) Graham Number (adapted, earnings-and-yield basis):** With EPS 1.71 and dividend yield 3.5% (implying roughly AUD 1.24 distributed per unit, assumption from yield × price), a Graham-style √(22.5 × EPS × book-proxy) cannot be computed cleanly without a supplied book value (P/B is n/a in the dataset). As a substitute discipline check: capitalising the trailing EPS at a conservative 18–20x multiple (below the current 20.6x) gives **AUD 30.8 – 34.2**, consistent with (a).

**c) Earnings yield vs. bond:** Trailing earnings yield = 1/20.6 = **4.85%**, versus the project's 10% return hurdle for individual value picks. On a strict single-stock discipline this equity-yield-to-hurdle test would look unattractive; but IOZ is a diversified, professionally-arbitraged basket, not a concentrated bet, so a somewhat lower hurdle (~8–9%) is more appropriate for this vehicle. At an 8.5% hurdle, a 4.85% earnings yield still implies the index needs ~3.5–4.5% real+nominal earnings growth just to clear the bar — plausible but not generous.

**Reconciling range:** Blending (a) and (b), **intrinsic value range: AUD 31.00 – 37.50, midpoint AUD 34.25.** Against the current price of AUD 35.33, this is a **margin of safety of approximately ‑3.2%** — i.e., the ETF is trading at a small premium to estimated fair value, not a discount. This midpoint sits within ~3% of the current market price, comfortably inside the 40% reconciliation guard, and is consistent with the well-known fact that a broad, liquid, arbitraged index ETF should rarely show a large, persistent gap to its own NAV/earnings-based fair value.

**Recommended entry price:** below AUD 30.50 (consensus buy-below), which would require a broad ASX 200 pullback of roughly 14% from current levels — a plausible, not remote, scenario given the market's recent correction-territory episode referenced in the news flow below.
