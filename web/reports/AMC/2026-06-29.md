## PART A — BUFFETT VALUE FRAMEWORK

### 1. Circle of Competence

**Inside the circle — straightforward.**

Amcor makes the packaging that surrounds consumer products: flexible films (coffee pouches, pharmaceutical blisters, pet food pouches), rigid containers (pharmaceutical bottles, jars), specialty cartons, and closures. Revenue is generated per unit of packaging produced and sold to multi-year contracted customers in food, beverage, pharmaceutical, personal care, and homecare categories.

The business model is simple enough to explain to a bright ten-year-old: Amcor makes the bag your biscuits come in, the sachet your medicine is sealed in, and the bottle your shampoo is packaged in. Revenue grows at roughly the rate of consumer staples volume plus pricing. Customers pay predictable prices per unit; contracts are long (typically 3–7 years); switching is costly because regulatory re-approval (pharma), filling line reconfiguration, or reformulation risk acts as a natural brake.

Post-Berry integration, the business is arguably even simpler to understand — two major divisions (flexible and rigid packaging), geographically diversified revenue, and a clear path to synergy delivery. No unusual accounting complexity, no derivative books, no financial engineering in the operating business.

**Verdict: Inside circle. Proceed.**

---

### 2. Key Assumptions (Thesis Depends On These)

1. **Berry Global synergies of USD 650m+ are achieved by FY28.** Management reaffirmed this target after the H1 FY26 result in February 2026. Failure to deliver synergies on schedule means forward EPS of AUD 6.25 is not achieved and the balance sheet remains over-leveraged.

2. **Free cash flow normalises to USD 1.5bn+ by FY27.** TTM FCF of USD 0.15bn is severely depressed by integration costs, working capital absorption, and elevated interest payments. If FCF does not recover, the dividend (~USD 1.15bn total) is existentially threatened.

3. **Net debt/EBITDA declines to below 4× by FY27–28** (from current est. 4.7×). Requires sustained FCF improvement and no further acquisitions. Breach of this path would impair the investment thesis materially.

4. **Organic volume growth of 2–3% per annum tracks global consumer staples.** Packaging demand is largely secular and stable. A sustained global recession or structural volume decline in end markets would undermine revenue assumptions.

5. **No structural regulatory or market shift destroys the flexible plastics model in the medium term.** Single-use plastic regulation (EU, Australia, others) is a long-run headwind, but transition timelines (2028–2035) are manageable with Amcor's investment in mono-material recyclable films.

### 3. Business Quality & Moat

**Moat type:** Switching costs (primary) + Scale advantages (secondary) + Regulatory/technical expertise intangibles (tertiary)
**Moat strength:** Narrow — consistent with Morningstar's independent assessment
**Moat trend:** Stable; mild long-term headwind from sustainability regulation

**Switching costs:** The most durable element of Amcor's moat. Pharmaceutical customers require FDA/TGA revalidation of packaging changes — a process that takes 12–24 months and costs millions in testing. Food customers risk reformulation or filling-line downtime. Global CPG players (Nestlé, Unilever, Procter & Gamble) value a single supplier that can serve 40+ country manufacturing networks. These are not insurmountable barriers, but they are substantial enough to make churning suppliers an active cost and risk decision rather than a pricing exercise.

**Scale advantages:** Post-Berry, Amcor is the undisputed global leader in flexible packaging (USD 12bn+ segment revenue) and a major competitor in rigid packaging. This scale enables procurement savings on raw materials (resins, aluminium, paper) and R&D investment (sustainable packaging, active packaging technologies) that second-tier competitors cannot match. Berry's North American rigid packaging network adds material complementary scale.

**Intangible assets:** 50+ years of pharma packaging regulatory track record, proprietary film and barrier coating technologies, and a global intellectual property portfolio in reclosable and active packaging. Importantly, these are process and specification intangibles — not brand intangibles. Amcor's name does not appear on end-consumer products.

**Moat threats:**
- *Sustainability regulation:* EU packaging regulations (PPWR) and potential Australian equivalents accelerate the shift away from multi-layer flexible plastics. Amcor is investing in recyclable/compostable alternatives but faces capital costs and customer re-qualification.
- *Commodity film competition:* Lower-value flexible packaging (commodity polyethylene films) is contestable by regional manufacturers with lower cost bases in Asia. Amcor's response is premiumisation and specialty.
- *Berry integration execution risk:* A poor integration damages customer relationships, accelerates switching, and eliminates the moat-widening potential of the deal.

**Five-year moat durability:** The moat is unlikely to be destroyed within five years. Switching costs are structural. Scale advantages persist. However, the moat is not widening at present — margin pressure and sustainability transition costs restrain returns on invested capital.

### 4. Management Assessment

**Integrity test:** Pass. No governance red flags, related-party concerns, or accounting irregularities identified. Dual-listed (NYSE + ASX) with full US SEC and ASX disclosure obligations. Auto-veto not triggered.

**Capital allocation track record:**
- *Bemis acquisition (2019, USD 6.8bn):* Broadly successful — integrated US flexible packaging operations into Amcor's global platform, achieved stated synergies within 3 years, ROIC recovered. This provides a template for the Berry integration.
- *Berry Global acquisition (closed mid-2025, ~USD 8.4bn including assumed debt):* Transformed Amcor into the undisputed global leader in flexible packaging and added significant rigid packaging capacity. However, the acquisition price stretched the balance sheet materially; D/E of 143.7 and net debt ~USD 15.2bn are uncomfortable.
- *Dividend record:* 30+ consecutive years of dividend maintenance, including through the 2008 GFC and COVID-19. This is a genuine track record commitment. However, maintaining dividends in excess of free cash flow (as is currently the case) is capital destruction unless FCF recovers quickly.
- *Reverse stock split (January 2026, 1-for-5):* A cosmetic corporate action that consolidated shares from ~2.3bn to ~462.3m. No economic impact. Done to move the ASX/NYSE price into a more conventional institutional range post-Berry dilution.

**Owner mentality:** CEO Ron Delia (tenure since 2015) has demonstrated long-term orientation. Insider ownership is modest by Buffett standards. No aggressive options grants or short-term incentive misalignment detected.

**Institutional imperative warnings:** The Berry deal was aggressive in leverage terms. Management is clearly aware — guidance is focused entirely on deleveraging and integration rather than further M&A. No new acquisitions flagged. This is appropriate post-Berry behaviour.

**Overall assessment:** Above-average management team for an ASX-listed industrial. Strong integrity; borderline on capital allocation given Berry leverage. Track record of eventual synergy delivery (Bemis precedent) provides some confidence.

#### Valuation Reconciliation

**Code model fair value AUD 47.39 vs my midpoint AUD 62.50 — divergence +32%. This exceeds the ~20% threshold and requires transparent explanation.**

**Step 1 — Input audit:**
The code's inputs are internally consistent. The WACC of 8.0% (floored from CAPM-derived 5.7%) is appropriate and conservative given D/E 143.7. Stage-1 growth 4%, terminal growth 2.5%, and exit multiple 12× EBIT are all defensible defaults. Share count 462.3m and net debt USD 15.17bn are authoritative. No individual input is an error or hallucination.

**Step 2 — The single assumption driving the gap:**
The code normalises owner earnings using the median of three values: forward NI (AUD 2,889m), margin-normalised (AUD 1,753m), and historical median OE (AUD 1,260m). The median of these three is AUD 1,753m, which reflects a blend of pre-Berry and post-Berry Amcor. The critical issue is that the historical median anchors to years when Amcor was a USD 12–15bn revenue company. Post-Berry, it is a structurally different USD 22bn entity. Applying AUD 1,753m to the new-scale business systematically understates earnings power by ~39%.

**Step 3 — Adjusted assumption with rationale:**
The first full combined-operations year (FY26) guides to forward NI of AUD 2,889m. This represents the post-Berry scale, but it embeds optimistic synergy assumptions. To account for (a) integration execution risk and (b) the elevated debt burden's sensitivity to refinancing conditions, I apply a 20% haircut: AUD 2,889m × 0.80 = AUD 2,311m as the reconciled normalised base.

**Step 4 — Reconciled IV:**

**Residual gaps explained:**
- *vs code model (AUD 47.39):* +32% gap driven entirely by earnings normalisation base. My higher base (AUD 2,311m vs AUD 1,753m) reflects the post-Berry structural step-change. If synergies disappoint and earnings revert toward historical, code model is correct.
- *vs analyst consensus (AUD 73.3):* −15% — I apply greater caution on leverage risk (D/E 143.7) and use WACC 8% floor. Analysts may be using lower discount rates and fuller synergy credit.
- *vs Morningstar (AUD 84.4):* −26% — Morningstar likely applies a lower structural WACC (possibly 5.7% without the floor) and assigns fuller Berry synergy value. I believe the 8% WACC floor is appropriate given the debt profile; a half-turn deterioration in EBITDA coverage would make refinancing materially more expensive.

**Model warnings noted:**
- WACC floored at 8.0%: the raw CAPM gives 5.7%. The floor adds conservatism that is warranted given high leverage.
- IV swings 96% across the sensitivity grid: the wide range (code: AUD 16.53–56.03) reflects genuine uncertainty. My wider reconciled range (AUD 54–71) is appropriate. Investors should widen their required MOS accordingly — the standard 30% MOS should be treated as a floor, not a ceiling.

### 17. McKinsey Macro Context (Australia)

If the US Fed and global rates continue to ease through H2 2026 and 2027, Amcor's interest burden declines materially — directly accelerating FCF recovery. **Rate easing is the single biggest macro tailwind for AMC.**

**Australian GDP and consumer spending:**
Australian GDP growth projected at ~2.0% for CY2026. Consumer spending on staples is stable-to-modest. Domestically, Amcor's Australian revenue is a small fraction of global sales — Australian macro drives the share price primarily through AUD/USD, not through local sales.

**AUD/USD sensitivity:**

- A 5% AUD appreciation (to 0.67) reduces AUD-equivalent EPS by ~5%
- A 5% AUD depreciation (to 0.61) increases AUD-equivalent EPS by ~5%
RBA easing relative to the Fed could keep AUD subdued or weaker — a modest tailwind for AUD investors holding AMC.
