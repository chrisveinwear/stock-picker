## Part A — Commodity Value Framework

### 1. Commodity Classification & Characteristics
**Monetary metal** — Gold's primary role is store of value and reserve asset. Price drivers (in order): (1) US real interest rates (10yr TIPS yield), (2) USD strength (DXY), (3) Central bank demand (~1,000t/yr since 2022), (4) ETF flows, (5) Geopolitical risk premium, (6) Jewellery demand (price-elastic, seasonal). No industrial consumption to speak of; ~95% of demand is monetary/investment/jewellery. Australian context: AUD gold = USD gold / AUDUSD. AUD typically weakens in risk-off (when USD gold rises) → natural double-kicker for AUD-based investors.

### 2. Supply Structure
- **Top producers**: China (~370t), Australia (~310t), Russia (~310t), Canada (~200t), USA (~170t). Top 5 = ~60% of ~3,600t mine supply.
- **AISC distribution**: 10th pct ~US$950, 50th pct US$1,560, 90th pct US$2,075 (S&P Global Mine Cost Outlook Jan-2026; WisdomTree/Bloomberg Aug-2025 median US$1,600). Cost curve is relatively flat — no extreme tail like copper.
- **Lead time**: Greenfield gold mine 7–10 years (permitting, construction). Brownfield expansions 3–5 years.
- **Structural constraints**: Declining ore grades (global average ~1.0 g/t vs 1.5 g/t a decade ago), permitting delays (especially in Western jurisdictions), energy cost inflation. Recycling provides ~1,200t/yr (price-elastic).
- **Country risk**: Russia sanctions (limited impact on gold flow), China export controls (not applied to gold), Australia stable.

### 3. Demand Structure
- **Top 3 drivers**: (1) Central bank net buying (official sector ~1,000t/yr, 25% of total demand), (2) Investment (ETFs, bar/coin ~1,200t/yr, highly price-sensitive), (3) Jewellery (~2,000t/yr, price-elastic, China/India dominant).
- **Growth rate**: Central bank buying structural since 2022 (de-dollarisation, reserve diversification). Mine supply growth ~1.5%/yr. Net deficit ~300t/yr filled by disinvestment/recycling.
- **Price elasticity**: Jewellery highly elastic (demand falls >20% when price rises 10%). Investment demand inelastic at extremes (safe-haven buying accelerates on spikes).
- **Structural shift**: Official sector demand shifted from net selling (1990s–2009) to net buying (2010–present), accelerating post-2022 Russia sanctions. This is a regime change.
- **China role**: ~30% of jewellery demand, ~20% of mine supply, central bank buying opaque but significant (PBOC reported 72t in H1-2026).
