## Part A — Commodity Value Framework

### 1. Asset Classification & Thesis Boundaries

Gold is a **dual-natured asset** and requires two analytical frameworks operating simultaneously.

**As a commodity**, gold obeys supply/demand and cost-curve logic. Mine supply is inelastic over 10–15-year development cycles. Industrial and jewellery demand is price-elastic. The marginal cost of production provides a genuine long-run floor. Cost-curve analysis is fully applicable here.

**As a monetary metal**, gold behaves like a reserve currency: it has no cash flows, its "fundamental value" is partly set by collective confidence in fiat systems, and central bank demand is a policy choice rather than a market mechanism. No earnings yield, DCF, or P/E equivalent applies. The monetary premium is real but mean-reverting.

**Analytical conclusion:** The cost model gives the *floor*, not the ceiling. The "fair value" of gold under normal macro conditions is the incentive price (~$2,550); the "monetarily-adjusted fair value" in a period of dollar stress is $3,060–$3,570. Spot at $4,113 exceeds both.

**Thesis boundaries for this investor:** Gold is firmly within the circle of competence. The asset is well-understood, held via liquid vehicles (Perth Mint physical, PMGOLD.AX), and serves a clear portfolio role as an AUD hedge and safe-haven diversifier. The question is not *whether* to hold gold but *whether to add at current prices*. At $4,113, the answer is no.
