## PART A — COMMODITY VALUE FRAMEWORK

### 1. Asset Class Context & Circle of Competence

Gold sits firmly within analytical competence. As a physical monetary metal with 5,000+ years of price history, a well-documented global cost structure across ~90 producing jurisdictions, and comprehensive supply/demand data from the World Gold Council, GFMS, and Metals Focus, gold is one of the most analytically tractable commodity markets.

Its dual nature must be declared upfront: gold is simultaneously a mined commodity with a definable cost curve and a monetary asset whose price reflects real interest rate dynamics, monetary system confidence, and geopolitical risk premiums that can divorce spot from mining economics for multi-year periods. Both lenses are necessary — neither alone is sufficient at current prices.

The analytical framework applied here (cost-curve incentive price as anchor, monetary premium as a quantified but bounded overlay) is appropriate and well-precedented. **Assessment: Inside circle of competence. Full analysis warranted.**

### 2. Key Thesis Assumptions

The bull case for gold at US$4,079 rests on these explicit assumptions. Track each quarterly and reassess the verdict if more than two are undermined simultaneously.

1. **De-dollarisation is structural, not cyclical.** Central banks (led by China, India, Russia, Middle East sovereigns) continue diversifying reserves from US Treasuries into gold at ≥600 tonnes/year, providing a permanent demand floor well above the historical jewellery/industrial baseline.

2. **Real interest rates remain suppressed.** Gold's opportunity cost is the real yield on US 10-year TIPS. The monetary case holds as long as real rates stay below +1.5%. A sustained break above +2.0% historically compresses gold by 20–35%.

3. **Supply is inelastic over 3–5 years.** Even at US$4,079, greenfield mine supply cannot respond in less than 7–10 years. Near-term production growth is modest; new incentivised supply will not be a meaningful market force until 2029–2031.

4. **Geopolitical fragmentation continues.** Sanctions risks, US-China strategic decoupling, and multi-polar reserve currency dynamics continue to reward non-sovereign, apolitical stores of value.

5. **Monetary/fiscal credibility remains challenged.** No credible US fiscal consolidation emerges; deficits remain structurally elevated, sustaining the monetary debasement argument.

*Single biggest thesis risk:* Assumptions 2 and 5 are the most vulnerable. A persistent real yield rise above +2% combined with any meaningful US fiscal improvement would be a severe double headwind.

### 3. Supply Structure & Scarcity Economics

**Mine Supply (~3,800 t/yr):**
Global mine production has been stable at 3,700–3,900 tonnes per year for the past decade. This stability reflects declining ore grades at major deposits, rare large-scale discoveries, and reserve replacement ratios across the industry of ~0.7–0.9 oz replaced per 1 oz mined. The resource base is not being replenished at the pace of extraction. At US$4,079, exploration budgets are at record highs, but the discovery-to-production pipeline is 7–10 years for greenfield and 3–5 years for brownfield expansion — meaningful supply response is not a 2026–2028 story.

**Recycling Supply (~1,100 t/yr):**
Gold recycling (primarily scrap jewellery) is price-responsive and is running elevated at current prices. This is the market's natural self-correcting mechanism. Recycling supply at US$4,079 is estimated 15–25% above its 5-year average, providing a modest but real counterweight to demand-driven price pressure.

**Central Bank Demand (~800–1,000 t/yr):**
This is the structural shift that has transformed gold since 2022. Emerging market central banks have been net buyers at historically unprecedented rates. This demand is strategic rather than price-sensitive — it reflects reserve diversification from USD-denominated assets following geopolitical events — and effectively absorbs a disproportionate share of annual mine supply. The durability of this demand is the most critical variable in the long-run price outlook.

**Jewellery & Industrial Demand (~2,200–2,400 t/yr combined):**
Jewellery demand (the single largest demand category) is highly price-elastic. Indian and Chinese consumers — who together account for ~55% of global jewellery demand — have shown clear demand destruction above US$3,500. At US$4,079, jewellery volume is materially below its long-run trend. Industrial demand (electronics, medical, dental) is small (~330 t) and stable.

**ETF & Investment Demand (~400–600 t/yr currently):**
ETF holdings had rebuilt from the 2022–2023 lows as Western investors re-engaged with gold following a period of outflows during the rate rise cycle. This re-engagement of Western financial investors has been a significant 2025–2026 price catalyst alongside the uninterrupted central bank buying.

*Supply/demand imbalance summary:* The physical market is broadly balanced on a flow basis, with central bank buying absorbing the slack left by jewellery demand destruction. The 85% premium to incentive price is not a supply/demand imbalance story — it is a monetary repricing story. Risk: if sentiment toward gold's monetary role shifts, there is no fundamental scarcity argument to provide support above US$2,500–2,800.

### 10. Wood Mackenzie — Cost Curve Model

**The cost curve at US$4,079 spot:**

At current prices, virtually every gold mine globally is profitable. The 90th-percentile AISC of US$1,950/oz means even the highest-cost producers earn US$2,129/oz — margins that were previously only available to the most efficient producers at historically peak prices.

*Cost curve implications:*

**Incentivisation of new supply:** At US$4,079, virtually any project with a reasonable grade and jurisdiction can be economically justified. This will stimulate a wave of feasibility studies and investment decisions across 2026–2028. However, production lag means no material output before 2030.

**Brownfield acceleration:** Existing operations are fast-tracking mill expansions, pit pushbacks, and underground extensions. This is the most immediate supply risk — likely adding 1–3% to annual mine supply by 2027–2028.

**Cost inflation spiral:** Extraordinary margins are triggering supply chain tightness across the mining ecosystem. Labour costs in key jurisdictions (Australia, Canada, West Africa) are rising 8–12% annually. Diesel, grinding media, and cyanide costs are elevated. The 90th-percentile AISC of US$1,950 is likely to drift toward US$2,100–2,200 by 2028, which would mechanically increase the incentive price — but not nearly enough to explain or justify the current premium.

### 13. Bridgewater — Macro Risk Assessment

**Interest rate sensitivity:** Gold is negatively correlated with real interest rates (correlation ~-0.65 over 10 years). Every 100bps rise in US 10-year TIPS yield historically reduces gold by 10–15% in the short term and 20–30% over 12–18 months. At US$4,079, a return to real yields of +2.0% represents a potential 20–30% downside catalyst — the single largest quantifiable tail risk.

**Inflation sensitivity:** Gold is commonly viewed as an inflation hedge, though the empirical record is more nuanced than the narrative. It outperforms during stagflationary episodes (rising prices, slowing growth) but underperforms equities during growth-driven inflation. At current prices, the inflation premium appears largely embedded — further inflation alone is unlikely to drive significant additional upside.

**USD sensitivity:** Gold and the USD Index (DXY) carry a ~-0.6 correlation over 5 years. The structural USD bear case (persistent US fiscal deficits, reserve currency diversification) is embedded in current gold pricing. Any improvement in US fiscal credibility or a sentiment shift toward USD assets is a material downside risk.

**Recession response:** Gold's behaviour in recessions is counter-intuitive. In the initial phase of a deep recession — characterised by liquidity demands and margin calls — gold typically sells off 15–25% before recovering as monetary stimulus is deployed. A recession is not a short-term positive for gold; it is only positive once the policy response (rate cuts, QE) is in motion.

**Geopolitical risk premium:** An estimated US$300–600/oz of the current price reflects geopolitical risk — ongoing conflicts, sanctions fragmentation, and US-China decoupling. A meaningful de-escalation event could compress this premium rapidly.

*Bridgewater verdict:* Risk profile is negatively asymmetric at current levels. Downside scenarios are more numerous, more probable, and larger in magnitude than upside scenarios. Not a position for new capital.

### 17. McKinsey — Macro Context: Australian Perspective

**RBA interest rate environment:**
The RBA's cash rate path affects gold's attractiveness to Australian investors through two channels: (1) opportunity cost — higher term deposit and bond rates raise the cost of holding non-yielding gold; and (2) AUD/USD dynamics — rate differentials between the RBA and Fed influence the exchange rate that translates USD gold into AUD gold. A dovish RBA (cutting into weakness) is moderately supportive for AUD gold.

**Australian dollar dynamics:**
AUD/USD at 0.690 is the critical variable. Australia's terms of trade are tied to commodity prices broadly; iron ore and coal remain the dominant export earners. A Chinese growth recovery that lifts bulk commodity prices would likely strengthen AUD — compressing the AUD gold price even if USD gold holds. Conversely, an Australian domestic slowdown would weaken AUD, providing a partial currency hedge. The path of AUD is a primary risk factor for Australian gold holders that is entirely separate from the gold price itself.

**Domestic inflation and real returns:**
Australian CPI remains above the RBA's 2–3% target band. The real return on Australian term deposits (nominal rate minus CPI) is low but positive — not zero as it was during the 2021–2022 ZIRP period. This modest positive real rate is a headwind to gold relative to that period.

**ASX gold miners as a proxy:**
The ASX gold sector (Newmont Australia division/Newcrest legacy, Evolution Mining [ASX:EVN], Northern Star Resources [ASX:NST], Gold Road Resources [ASX:GOR], Regis Resources [ASX:RRL]) is generating exceptional margins at A$5,911/oz spot. However, these equities have re-rated significantly and are not obviously cheap on a NAV basis. The leverage that miners provide in an up-move works equally in reverse on a price correction — at A$5,911 spot, a 20% correction to ~A$4,729 would compress miner earnings by 40–60% due to operating leverage. Gold equities are not a conservative expression of gold exposure at current prices.

**Perth Mint and Australian gold infrastructure:**
Australia remains one of the world's top gold producers (~10% of global mine supply) and the Perth Mint is a world-class vault and refining facility. For Australian investors, direct allocated gold storage at the Perth Mint (with WA Government backing) remains the lowest-risk vehicle for physical exposure.

**Australian macro verdict:** The macro environment in Australia provides a broadly neutral backdrop for gold. The weak AUD has amplified returns for Australian holders, but this tailwind is vulnerable to a domestic or commodity cycle recovery. The opportunity cost of holding gold (vs. Australian fixed income at current yields) has risen modestly from zero. No Australian-specific macro catalyst that would add to the global monetary premium.
