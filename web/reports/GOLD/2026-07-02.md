## Part A — Commodity Value Framework

### 1. Commodity Classification & Characteristics
**Class: Monetary Metal** — Gold's primary price drivers are US real interest rates (10yr TIPS yield), USD strength (DXY), central bank reserve allocation, and geopolitical risk premia. It has no earnings, no yield, and minimal industrial demand (~7% of total). Its value derives from its role as a non-sovereign store of value and portfolio hedge. Australian investors gain a double-kicker: USD gold rises in risk-off episodes while AUD typically weakens, amplifying AUD-denominated returns.

### 2. Supply Structure
- **Top producers (2025):** China (~370t), Russia (~320t), Australia (~290t), Canada (~200t), USA (~170t). Top 5 = ~60% of ~3,600t mine supply.
- **Cost curve (2025 weighted avg AISC):** 10th pct ~US$1,100; 50th pct US$1,560; 90th pct US$2,075 (S&P Global Mine Cost Outlook Jan-2026; WisdomTree/Bloomberg Aug-2025).
- **Lead time:** Greenfield gold mine 7–12 years (discovery → permitting → construction). Brownfield expansions 2–4 years.
- **Structural constraints:** Declining grades (avg ~1.0 g/t vs 1.5 g/t a decade ago), permitting delays (Western democracies), and ESG capital allocation limits.
- **Country risk:** Russia/China ~38% of supply — sanctions and export policy risk. Australia/Canada stable but high-cost.

### 3. Demand Structure
- **Top 3 drivers (2025 WGC data):** Central bank net buying (~1,000t/yr, 28% of demand), bar & coin investment (~1,200t, 33%), jewellery (~2,100t, 55% but price-elastic).
- **Growth:** Central bank buying structural since 2022 (de-dollarisation, reserve diversification). ETF flows volatile (GLD holdings 780t vs 2020 peak 3,800t). Jewellery demand flat-to-down in China/India at high prices.
- **Price elasticity:** Jewellery highly elastic (demand falls >20% when price rises >15% YoY). Central bank demand inelastic (policy-driven). ETF flows momentum-driven.
- **China role:** ~30% of jewellery demand, ~25% of bar/coin, central bank buying opaque but significant (PBOC reported 72.8t in H1 2025).

**Conclusion:** The cost model is not "wrong" — it correctly values the *produced commodity* component. The monetary thesis adds a structural premium, but not 60%. The current price discounts a "perfect storm" (recession + Fed pivot + debt crisis + war) that is not base case.
