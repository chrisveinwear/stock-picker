## PART A — BUFFETT VALUE FRAMEWORK

### 1. Circle of Competence

**Inside.**

The a2 Milk Company is a consumer-branded business that sources milk from cows naturally producing only the A2 variant of beta-casein protein, processes it into infant formula and fresh dairy products, and sells under the "a2" brand primarily in China, Australia, New Zealand, and the United States. The value proposition is simple: A2-only dairy is marketed as easier to digest than conventional A1/A2 milk. Revenue flows from product sales; the economics are those of a branded consumer staple — high gross margins, low capital intensity, repeat-purchase loyalty.

The complexity is not in the model but in distribution: China sales flow partly through daigou (personal shoppers) and cross-border ecommerce channels, which creates regulatory exposure. This complexity is understandable with modest research. The underlying economics — branded premium product, 45.9% gross margin, minimal capex, loyal customer base — are firmly within the circle of competence for a generalist value investor.

### 2. Key Assumptions

The following assumptions underpin the thesis; each should be verified at every reporting cycle:

1. **China market access remains open.** Regulatory settings continue to permit cross-border imported infant formula. Any restriction analogous to the 2016–2018 ecommerce crackdown would be the largest single risk to the thesis.
2. **Premiumisation offsets the birth rate decline.** Fewer babies in China does not mean less revenue per cohort if A2M captures a larger share of per-child spending. This requires ongoing verification via market share data.
3. **A2 protein science remains defensible.** The A2 protein digestive-health claim has not been validated by large-scale randomised trials. A major adverse clinical study would erode the brand premium. No such study is anticipated currently.
4. **US market achieves positive EBITDA contribution within three years.** Management is investing capital in the US; the thesis assumes this is disciplined capital allocation, not vanity growth.
5. **NZD/AUD exchange rate ≈ 0.90 AUD per NZD.** This rate is used throughout all currency conversions. Every AUD 0.05 move in NZD/AUD changes AUD-reported EPS by approximately 3%.

### 3. Business Quality & Moat

**Moat type:** Intangible (brand) reinforced by switching-cost characteristics — once a baby is established on a formula without digestive distress, parents are strongly reluctant to change brands.
**Moat strength:** Narrow (independent confirmation from Morningstar).
**Moat trend:** Stable; gradual narrowing risk from well-resourced competitors entering the A2 protein space.

**Five-year moat durability assessment:**

The A2M moat rests on three pillars. First, brand monopoly — in China the "a2" trademark has achieved category ownership in the A2-protein formula segment to a degree that would take years and enormous capital to erode. Post-Melamine crisis, Chinese parents treat brand as a safety proxy; this loyalty is sticky and cross-generational. Second, supply chain control — A2M accredits farms capable of producing A2-only milk, creating a modest supply-side barrier to replication at scale. Third, first-mover positioning — no competitor has yet matched A2M's brand equity in this niche on either price or quality perception, although Feihe's A2 product line is the most credible emerging threat.

The 45.9% gross margin is the strongest quantitative evidence that the moat is functioning. A moatless commodity dairy company does not sustain 45%+ gross margins. That said, the moat is genuinely narrow: core A2 beta-casein patents have largely expired, and Nestlé, Danone, Feihe, and Ausnutria can and are launching A2 variants. A2M must continue to invest in brand and distribution to stay ahead.

### 4. Management Assessment

**Integrity:** No red flags. The June 2026 voluntary product recall in the US market was handled proactively — this is a positive signal about management culture. **Pass.**

**Capital allocation track record:** CEO David Bortolussi's tenure shows consistent improvement across every financial metric. Net income grew from NZD 123m (FY22) to NZD 203m (FY25); gross margins have held above 45%; FCF has averaged NZD 183m over the period. The February 2026 half-year result was described as delivered "a year ahead of schedule," and the market rewarded this with a 12–13.5% two-day share price rally. These are characteristics of a management team that under-promises and over-delivers.

**Owner mentality:** The NZD 1.1bn cash hoard (FY25) is both a strength and a question mark. With minimal debt (NZD 101m), the company carries significant excess capital that is not earning an adequate return. The 2.2% dividend yield is modest for a cash-rich consumer staples company with this earnings quality. A structured buyback program — particularly at prices below AUD 6.50 — would create meaningful per-share value.

**Institutional imperative warning:** The US market expansion is the clearest execution risk. The US infant formula market is dominated by domestic players (Abbott, Mead Johnson) and the A2 narrative resonates differently in a market not shaped by Chinese cross-border commerce. Management confidence is high; the thesis demands continuous validation that US investment generates positive EBITDA contribution, not empire-building.

### 13. Bridgewater Risk Assessment

**Interest rate sensitivity:** Low. A2M is a net-cash company (NZD ~1.0bn net cash). Rising rates increase return on cash holdings; falling rates reduce the equity discount rate and support valuation multiples. In either direction, the operating business economics are minimally affected. **Low risk.**

**Inflation sensitivity:** Moderately positive. Premium infant formula has demonstrated pricing power — A2M has historically raised prices modestly without meaningful volume loss. Input costs (dairy) are commodity-linked and represent the primary COGS exposure; moderate inflation is manageable within the 45.9% gross margin structure. A severe input-cost spike with no pricing offset would compress margins. **Net modestly positive in moderate inflation.**

**Recession stress test:** Mild negative impact anticipated. Infant formula is a necessity product for its users; parents are among the last consumers to trade down in a recession. However, severe recession would cause some trade-down to generic or lower-cost formula brands. Estimated drawdown under a severe recession scenario: 25–35% from current price (to approximately AUD 5.00–5.75), supported by the fortress balance sheet preventing forced-selling dynamics.

**Liquidity risk:** Very low. Market cap AUD 5.54bn; daily volume well above institutional minimum thresholds. Corporate balance sheet is net-cash.

**Leverage risk:** Very low. Net cash position of NZD ~1.0bn; minimal financial debt (NZD 101m).

**Tail risk scenarios:**

- **Low probability / very high impact:** Chinese government restricts imported infant formula cross-border sales. Revenue impact: –40 to –60%. Share price impact: –50%+. This is the single largest tail risk for A2M and cannot be hedged operationally.
- **Medium probability / medium impact:** Second significant product quality incident. A2M's brand recovered from the 2020 Botulism scare; a second major recall would cause more permanent reputational damage in China. Impact: –20 to –35% share price.
