## PART A — BUFFETT VALUE FRAMEWORK

### 1. Circle of Competence

**Inside, with a caveat.** A2M is a branded infant-formula and dairy-nutrition company — a simple consumer-staples business model to understand: buy milk, process/contract-manufacture it into formula and liquid milk, market it under a trusted "a2 Protein" brand, sell through retail and cross-border e-commerce (CBEC) channels into China and ANZ. The economics (branded margin over commodity dairy input costs, working-capital-light licensing/contract-manufacturing model) are legible. The caveat: the single biggest swing factor in the thesis — Chinese birth rates, daigou/CBEC channel dynamics, and China State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) formula registration rules — is a geopolitical and demographic variable outside direct company control and outside an ASX investor's easy ability to verify in real time. I keep it inside the circle because the *business model* is simple even though one *input* (China demand) requires humility about forecasting precision.

### 2. Key Assumptions (3–5)

1. Chinese infant-formula birth-rate decline continues to be offset by share gains and premiumisation, as management has argued and as FY26 execution (HY26 profit +19%, FY26 sales target reportedly hit a year early per the February 2026 business update) currently supports.
2. A2M retains its "narrow moat" (Morningstar's independent rating) via brand trust and the A1-protein-free positioning — i.e., no material erosion in Chinese consumer willingness to pay a premium for the a2 brand over the next 3–5 years.
3. No debt is taken on and the balance sheet's net-cash position is maintained, preserving optionality and downside protection (a structural, long-standing feature of A2M's capital structure, not sourced from this data pull but consistent with its history as a licensing/asset-light model).
4. Margins continue to benefit from the shift toward the China-label (rather than English-label/daigou) channel, which management has been steering toward for several years.
5. No further regulatory shock (e.g., a SAMR re-registration disruption or new China dairy-safety scandal analogous to 2008 melamine) resets consumer trust in the category.

If assumption 1 breaks — i.e., share gains stop offsetting demographic decline — the entire long-term growth case collapses to a low-single-digit or negative growth annuity, and fair value should be re-cut materially lower.

### 3. Business Quality & Moat

**Moat type:** Intangible asset (brand trust, particularly the "a2 Protein" health positioning) plus a smaller switching-cost element in infant formula (parents rarely switch formula brands mid-feeding-cycle once a baby is established on one). **Strength: Narrow** — this matches Morningstar's independently published rating, which I take as a useful cross-check rather than blend into my own maths. A2M does not have the structural pricing power of a true wide-moat consumer franchise (no patents, contract-manufactured rather than owned production for much of its volume, and channel economics that can be disrupted by CBEC platform shifts). **Trend: Stable, tentatively improving** — FY25 EBITDA growth of 17% to NZD 274m and the HY26 profit increase of 19% (both from the news flow supplied) suggest operating leverage is working in the company's favour right now, and management's claim of hitting FY26 infant-formula/dairy sales targets a year ahead of schedule (February 2026 reporting) is a genuine positive data point if it holds up in the full-year result.

**Five-year moat durability test:** Would a well-capitalised competitor entering China formula distribution today be able to replicate a2's shelf position and consumer trust within five years? Partially — global majors (Nestlé, Danone, FrieslandCampina, Feihe domestically) already compete hard in this category and have deeper China distribution in some tiers. A2M's edge is the differentiated protein-science positioning, which is defensible but not un-copyable (competitors have launched their own A1-free/goat-milk alternatives). I would not call this a moat that widens on autopilot — it needs continued marketing investment and share defence, consistent with a "narrow" rather than "wide" rating.
