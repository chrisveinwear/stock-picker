## PART A — BUFFETT VALUE FRAMEWORK

### 1. Circle of Competence

**Inside circle — proceed.**

Endeavour Group operates two straightforward businesses demerged from Woolworths in 2021:

- **Drinks retail (~83% of revenue):** Dan Murphy's (~270 large-format destination stores) and BWS (~1,400+ convenience stores). Revenue model: buy alcohol wholesale at scale, sell at retail margin. Price positioning: Dan Murphy's anchors on a lowest-price guarantee; BWS competes on convenience. Both are understandable.
- **Hotels (~17% of revenue):** ~350 licensed venues across Australia generating revenue from gaming machines (pokies), bars, bistros, and accommodation. Revenue model: footfall + gaming yield per machine. Regulatory: each venue holds state-issued gaming and liquor licences; licence conditions are understandable even if the political backdrop requires monitoring.

There is no complex financial engineering, no derivatives exposure, no material international operations. A child can explain Dan Murphy's. The only boundary-case complexity is AASB 16 (right-of-use asset accounting for store leases), which inflates the stated debt figure but does not obscure the underlying cash economics once understood. **Circle: Inside.**

---

### 2. Key Assumptions (Thesis Depends On)

These are the explicit assumptions that drive the investment case. Each must be monitored.

1. **Transformation delivers.** The three-year plan announced May 2026 targets ~AUD 300m in structural cost savings. Assumes execution by an unproven CEO (Hrdlicka, 18 months in role) without material revenue sacrifice; operating margins recover toward 9%+ by FY28.
2. **Gaming regulation is stable.** NSW and Victoria are the dominant hotel-gaming markets. No further reduction in machine allowances, operating hours, or cashless-gaming mandates that would impair hotel revenue >5%.
3. **Deleveraging path is clear.** FCF exceeds capex + dividends post-payout cut, reducing net debt from ~AUD 5.5bn toward AUD 4.5bn by FY28. No acquisitions that reset the debt clock.
4. **Drinks retail volumes inflect.** Consumer spending normalises post-cost-of-living peak, supporting low single-digit volume recovery in FY27 and beyond. No structural shift away from in-store alcohol purchases at Dan Murphy's scale.
5. **Winery divestments proceed cleanly.** Sales of Chapel Hill, Oakridge, and other wine assets close near book value, simplifying the portfolio without material write-downs or reputational cost.

### 3. Business Quality & Moat

**Moat type:** Overlapping scale + intangible asset + regulatory barriers
**Moat strength:** Narrow-to-wide (Wide in hotels; Narrow-to-Wide in drinks retail)
**Moat trend:** Stable to slightly narrowing in drinks retail; stable in hotels

**Drinks retail moat (Narrow-to-Wide):**
Dan Murphy's is the #1 alcohol destination retailer in Australia by store count and revenue, with a brand built on price trust. Scale purchasing (~AUD 10bn in retail revenues) creates meaningful cost advantages over any new entrant, and the BWS convenience network of 1,400+ outlets creates switching friction. However, Coles Liquor (Liquorland + First Choice, ~920 stores) is a credible at-scale competitor, and online D2C channels (craft beer, wine subscription) nibble at the periphery. The moat is real but not impenetrable.

**Hotels/gaming moat (Wide):**
State-issued gaming machine licences are finite, non-replicable, and deeply embedded in communities and property values. Endeavour's ~350 hotels collectively hold thousands of gaming machine approvals. A new competitor cannot replicate this network at any price; regulators are politically constrained from issuing material new licences. This is a genuine regulatory moat, and gaming revenues (higher-margin) protect the earnings base in downturns when alcohol volumes soften.

**Five-year durability test:** Both franchises should be intact in five years. The moat is more likely to narrow than widen given e-commerce headwinds in retail andincremental regulatory tightening in gaming, but neither risk is existential over a five-year horizon. Morningstar's Wide Moat rating is defensible primarily on the hotel-gaming pillar; I rate the blended franchise Narrow-to-Wide.

**Institutional imperative risk:** Low. Management has made unpopular decisions (winery divestments, dividend cut, store rationalisation) rather than defending legacy positions. No governance red flags.

**Auto-veto test (integrity):** No integrity issues identified. **Veto: not triggered.**

**Overall:** Neutral. Credit for strategic courage; demerits for slow deleveraging through good years and poor earnings communication.

### 13. Bridgewater Risk Assessment

**Interest rate sensitivity: High (negative).**
Net debt of ~AUD 5.5bn at an estimated blended rate of ~6% = ~AUD 330m annual interest. If rates rise 100bps (unlikely in current RBA context but possible), interest expense increases ~AUD 55m, reducing pre-tax income by ~10%. Conversely, rate cuts reduce interest burden and stimulate consumer spending on alcohol and gaming — a meaningful tailwind. Net: interest rate risk is bilateral but the company is more exposed on the upside if rates stay higher for longer.

**Inflation sensitivity: Moderate (mixed).**
Cost-of-living inflation reduces consumer discretionary income, pressuring gaming machine turnover and premium spirits sales. Labour costs (a major component of hotel operations) inflate with CPI. EDV has partial ability to pass through cost increases in retail (beverage pricing) but less flexibility on gaming yield per machine (regulated caps in some states). Structural inflation is net negative for margins.

**Recession stress test:**
- Revenue −15% scenario: EBIT ~AUD 600m (at compressed 5.9% margin), interest ~AUD 330m, pre-tax ~AUD 270m → still profitable, debt serviceable.
- Revenue −30% scenario: EBIT ~AUD 425m, interest ~AUD 330m, pre-tax ~AUD 95m → barely serviceable; covenant risk. FCF potentially falls near zero.
- Estimated max drawdown in severe recession: **35–45%** from current price (to ~AUD 1.85–2.20), reflecting financial leverage amplifying equity volatility.

**Liquidity risk: Low under base case, elevated in stress.**
AUD 340m cash on hand plus undrawn revolving credit facilities (not specified in provided data). FY2025 FCF of AUD 816m comfortably covers interest, dividends (post-cut), and moderate capex. In a revenue shock, headroom narrows rapidly — see stress test above.

**Leverage risk: Elevated.** Net debt / EBITDA = 5,499 / 1,080 = 5.1×. This is high for a retail-hospitality operator. The beta clamping from 0.37 to 0.60 in the code model partially reflects this; a fair-value investor should apply an additional margin-of-safety buffer.
