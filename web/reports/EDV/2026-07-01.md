**VERDICT:** Watch — Endeavour's Dan Murphy's franchise is a genuine wide-moat business that Buffett would admire in better circumstances, but at AUD 3.37 the margin of safety is a thin 11%, the balance sheet carries more debt than a conservative owner can sleep soundly on, and a newly-announced transformation plan has yet to earn even one quarter of execution credibility; watch for either a cheaper price or concrete evidence of earnings recovery before committing capital.

## PART A — BUFFETT VALUE FRAMEWORK

### 1. Circle of Competence

**Inside.** Endeavour Group operates two distinct but intertwined businesses. The **Retail division** owns and operates approximately 1,700 stores across Dan Murphy's (destination off-premise liquor) and BWS (convenience off-premise liquor), collectively giving Endeavour an estimated 55% share of Australian packaged-liquor retail. The **Hotels division** operates ~340 ALH-branded licensed venues, generating revenue from bar trade, bistro dining, TAB wagering, and electronic gaming machines (EGMs). Revenue flows from high-frequency repeat purchases — wine, beer, spirits — and from the cash-generative gaming floor. The business is straightforward: Australians buy their wine at Dan Murphy's and place bets at the local ALH pub, and Endeavour owns both the bottle shop and the pokies room. Capital requirements include store fit-outs and hotel renovations; the model is moderately capital-intensive. One complexity worth noting is that the Hotels division's EGM revenue (roughly 40% of Hotels EBITDA) is subject to state-level regulation — a political variable that is harder to model than the retail business. Within that caveat, the business is comfortably inside the circle of competence.

### 2. Key Assumptions (5)

The thesis stands or falls on these five explicitly stated assumptions, each of which must be monitored quarterly:

1. **Transformation delivers AUD $300m in cost savings by FY28.** CEO Jayne Hrdlicka outlined this in May 2026 alongside a portfolio reset (winery divestments, store rationalisation). Concrete milestones must appear in H1 FY27 results. *Verify: operating margin trajectory each half.*

2. **Australian packaged-liquor market remains structurally stable.** Per-capita consumption is modestly declining, but off-premise channel growth (trading pub spend to home drinking) supports EDV's retail volumes. *Verify: category scan data; Coles Liquorland share disclosure.*

3. **EGM regulation does not materially impair ALH revenue.** State governments are reviewing harm-reduction policy. Any mandatory bet limits or cashless-gaming mandates affecting Victoria or Queensland would impair Hotels EBITDA by 15–30%. *Verify: state budget papers; Star/Crown regulatory precedents.*

4. **Net debt/EBITDA returns toward 3x over three years.** Currently 4.6x — uncomfortably high. Winery sale proceeds (~AUD 300m expected) and higher post-transformation FCF are the mechanisms. *Verify: H1 FY27 balance sheet; refinancing terms when announced.*

5. **Dan Murphy's brand dominance is sustained against Coles Liquorland and online delivery.** Dan Murphy's NPS consistently leads the category. Coles has gained modest share; online delivery is <5% of category. *Verify: annual category share reports; Coles liquor divisional disclosures.*

### 3. Business Quality & Moat

- **Moat type:** Scale cost advantage (primary) + intangible brand (secondary) + efficient scale in community gaming
- **Strength:** Wide — consistent with Morningstar's independent Wide designation
- **Trend:** Stable in liquor retail; narrowing in gaming due to regulatory headwinds

**Source of moat.** With AUD 12bn+ in annual procurement, Dan Murphy's purchasing power produces shelf prices competitors cannot sustainably match at equivalent quality. This is a self-reinforcing flywheel: lower prices drive higher volumes, which drives greater purchasing scale, which enables lower prices again. Replicating this advantage would require a competitor to simultaneously match EDV's store network density and procurement scale — effectively impossible from a standing start without years and billions of capital. The brand layer on top (Dan Murphy's as Australia's authoritative wine destination) adds emotional switching costs for higher-spending wine shoppers that purely price-based competitors cannot easily dislodge.

**Gaming moat.** The ~340 ALH venues operate under hard-to-obtain liquor and gaming licences. Regulatory barriers to new community hotel/gaming licences function as efficient-scale protection — the market is sized such that new entrants would destroy returns for existing operators. The moat is real but politically fragile.

**Durability test (10 years).** The liquor retail moat will likely remain intact, though marginally narrower as Coles reinvests and online delivery matures. The gaming moat faces genuine probability of regulatory tightening within the decade — the political trajectory in Australia is toward harm reduction, and some EGM restriction is probable. Net assessment: the moat exists, is wide today per both this analysis and Morningstar, but is more brittle than the unqualified Wide label implies. A 5–10% reduction in EGM revenue within 10 years is the base-case risk, not an edge case.

**Hypothetical competitor test.** Could Woolworths re-enter packaged liquor (having demerged EDV in 2021)? Theoretically yes, but the moat was built over decades and cannot be replicated quickly. Could Coles scale aggressively? Possible but would require AUD 2–3bn in capital over five years and faces ACCC scrutiny. Could Amazon disrupt? Only in ultra-premium or subscription niches — not a structural threat to the mass-market model. The moat passes this test.

### 4. Management Assessment

- **Integrity:** Pass — CEO Hrdlicka has been transparent, proactively cutting the dividend payout ratio and publicly disclosing transformation costs rather than burying them. Divesting underperforming wineries is the right structural decision made early in tenure. Caution flag: Hrdlicka previously led Virgin Australia (entered administration under her watch) and Jetstar — her execution track record in capital-constrained turnarounds is mixed. No related-party concerns identified.
- **Capital allocation:** Below average historically. The winery portfolio build-up under prior management was classic institutional imperative — deploying capital into adjacent categories (premium wine production) without generating adequate returns. The pivot to "returns-focused, capital-light" growth is directionally correct but untested at Endeavour.
- **Owner mentality:** Moderate. No disclosed on-market share purchases by Hrdlicka since appointment — a notable absence at a multi-year low price. Management's compensation structure links to EBITDA and TSR targets; meaningful personal shareholding would strengthen alignment.
- **Institutional imperative signals (present).** Prior management's winery acquisitions and hotel expansion programme bear hallmarks of size-worship — growing the asset base in pursuit of scale rather than per-share value. The new CEO's willingness to reverse this is a positive signal; the proof will be in FY27 results.
