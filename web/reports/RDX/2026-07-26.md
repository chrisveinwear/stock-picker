## PART A — BUFFETT VALUE FRAMEWORK

### 1. Circle of Competence

Redox is a chemicals and ingredients **distributor and formulator** — it buys industrial and specialty chemical raw materials in bulk, blends/repackages some of it, and resells to customers across mining, water treatment, agriculture, food & beverage, personal care and general manufacturing in Australia, NZ and increasingly Asia. The business model itself is simple to explain: buy chemicals wholesale, add technical/logistics value, sell at a margin, turn inventory. That puts it at the **boundary** of the circle of competence defined in this project's charter — it isn't consumer staples, banking, healthcare, infrastructure, or software, and it isn't a pure-play miner either. It is closest in spirit to the "resources-adjacent industrial" category, and it is genuinely understandable, so I will proceed rather than stop, but I hold it to a higher evidentiary bar than a core-circle name.

### 2. Key Assumptions (3–5)

1. Post-IPO deleveraging (debt AUD 218m → 47m between FY23 and FY24) reflects durable capital discipline, not a one-off IPO clean-up that reverses.
2. Revenue can keep compounding at a mid-single-digit to low-double-digit rate as Redox expands into Asian distribution and adjacent product categories, without a commensurate rise in low-margin, price-taking competition.
3. The FY25 fall in FCF (AUD 112m → 43m) and rising capex (AUD 4m → 5m) is a temporary reinvestment/working-capital effect rather than the start of structural margin erosion.
4. The current ~85% trailing payout ratio is sustainable given the net-cash balance sheet, and won't need to be cut as capex needs grow.
5. No wide economic moat exists (per Morningstar) — returns on capital will be competed toward normal industrial-distribution levels over time, so growth-heavy DCF assumptions should be discounted relative to static earnings-multiple checks.

### 3. Business Quality & Moat

**Moat type:** primarily **none to narrow**. What modest stickiness exists comes from (a) exclusive/agency distribution agreements with global chemical principals, (b) technical formulation know-how for regulated/customised blends, and (c) logistics/inventory infrastructure that is costly for a customer to replicate for itself. None of these constitute a classic wide moat (no network effects, no meaningful brand, low switching costs for commodity-grade product lines).

**Strength:** narrow at best, consistent with Morningstar's **"None"** rating.

**Trend:** stable. Nothing in the supplied financial history (steady ~1.1–1.3bn revenue band, net margin oscillating 6–8%) suggests the competitive position is either widening or collapsing.

**Five-year moat durability test:** would Redox still earn today's returns on capital in five years if a well-funded competitor tried to take share? Likely only partially — distribution agency relationships can be re-tendered, and specialty formulation IP is defensible but not patent-grade. This supports treating Redox as a **good, well-run cyclical distributor**, not a compounder with a moat.
