**PART A — BUFFETT VALUE FRAMEWORK**

**1. Circle of Competence**

Squarely **inside**. Telstra is Australia's dominant integrated telecommunications carrier: mobile network (~50%+ national market share), fixed-line/NBN wholesale reseller, enterprise & wholesale data services, and an infrastructure arm (towers, fibre, data centres via Amplitel/InfraCo). The business model is simple to explain: it owns the pipes and towers that carry Australia's voice and data traffic, charges subscription/usage fees, and earns a return on a capital-intensive network that is expensive and slow for competitors to replicate. This is a "toll road for data" business — exactly the kind of understandable, essential-service franchise that sits at the centre of the circle of competence, alongside CBA (payments toll road) and TCL (physical toll roads).

**2. Key Assumptions (3–5)**

1. Mobile remains a rational **duopoly-plus** structure (Telstra, Optus, TPG/Vodafone) with disciplined pricing rather than a price war — Telstra's premium network quality continues to support an ARPU premium.
2. Telstra sustains low-single-digit revenue growth (~2.5–3.5% p.a., consistent with the FY22–FY25 CAGR of ~2.8% in the financial history table) rather than reverting to the multi-year revenue stagnation seen during the NBN migration years.
3. Owner earnings continue to benefit from cost-out/network-simplification programs (T25/T30-style initiatives) that expand operating margin from the current 16.5% toward the high-teens over the decade, even as revenue growth stays modest.
4. Capital intensity moderates post the peak 5G/fibre build-out — capex last four years ranged AUD 3.1–5.1bn; a sustained step-down toward the lower end of that range is needed for FCF to keep growing faster than revenue.
5. Management continues returning capital via buybacks/dividends at a pace the balance sheet can sustain without impairing the investment-grade credit profile (total debt has risen from AUD 14.3bn to AUD 18.0bn over FY22–FY25 while equity has fallen from AUD 15.3bn to AUD 13.9bn, largely buyback-driven).

**3. Business Quality & Moat**

- **Moat type**: Scale + cost advantage (largest mobile network footprint and spectrum holdings in Australia) combined with modest switching costs (contracts, bundling, brand trust in a market where network reliability matters). Regulatory barriers (spectrum licensing, capital intensity of replicating a national network) reinforce the moat.
- **Strength**: **Narrow-to-wide**. Mobile is the crown jewel — a genuine oligopoly with rational pricing discipline and Telstra as the premium-priced share leader. Fixed-line/NBN reselling is a much weaker, largely commoditised business where Telstra is a wholesale customer of NBN Co rather than the infrastructure owner, capping the moat there at "none-to-narrow."
- **Trend**: **Stable to slightly widening** in mobile (network investment gap vs. peers, Amplitel tower monetisation freeing capital), roughly **stable** in fixed enterprise/wholesale.
- **Five-year moat durability test**: Would Telstra still enjoy structurally attractive returns in 2031 if a well-capitalised new entrant tried to compete? Spectrum scarcity, tower/site density, and the capital cost of a national network (tens of billions) make a fourth full-scale mobile entrant unlikely. The moat is durable in mobile; the main erosion risk is regulatory (ACCC intervention on roaming/wholesale access) or a return to irrational price competition, both plausible but not the base case.

**4. Management Assessment**

- **Integrity**: No red flags in the data provided. CEO Vicki Brady (since September 2022) has continued the T25 strategy with a clear, consistently communicated capital-allocation framework. No veto trigger identified.
- **Capital allocation track record**: Mixed-to-good. The FY26 half-year result (per the news flow below) showed profit growth alongside an **expanded share buyback** and a completed **AUD 1.25bn on-market buyback retiring ~246 million shares** — a meaningful ~2% reduction in share count that is EPS- and DPS-accretive if done below intrinsic value, value-destructive if done above it. Given the stock has traded in the AUD 4.6–5.6 band for the past year — around or above our fair-value estimate for much of that period — this buyback is not obviously being executed at a discount, which tempers enthusiasm about capital-allocation discipline.
- **Owner mentality**: Reasonable — dividend consistency and buyback activity signal a shareholder-return orientation, though rising leverage (D/E climbing from FY22 to FY25 in the financial history table) to fund both capex and buybacks simultaneously is worth monitoring.
- **Institutional imperative warnings**: Management's own February 2026 commentary **cautioning on AI overinvestment and the risk of lower returns** (see news item #2) is a genuinely encouraging sign — it suggests discipline against industry-wide capex peer pressure rather than "everyone else is spending, so must we" thinking.

**Valuation Reconciliation**

The code fair value of AUD 3.94 sits **19.5% below** the current price of AUD 4.89 and **24.5% below** the analyst consensus target of AUD 5.22 — inside the ~20% guard but close enough, and wide enough versus consensus, to warrant an explicit walk-through rather than accepting the headline number uncritically.

*(a) Input check*: All authoritative inputs (price, shares outstanding, revenue, EBITDA, FCF, debt, margins, financial history) reconcile cleanly against the data block — no evidence of a stale price, share-count, or corporate-action error. The model's own warnings flag the real issue: beta of 0.13 (implausibly low — likely a function of Telstra's bond-like trading pattern and thin correlation to the ASX 200 over the sample window) is clamped to the 0.60 floor, and the resulting cost of equity of 7.6% is further floored to 8.0% by policy. Both clamps are structural (I am not proposing to change them), but they mechanically depress every method that discounts cash flows, and the **wide 39% sensitivity-grid swing** confirms the model itself is telling us confidence is low here.

*(b) Which assumption drives the gap*: The AUD 3.94 headline is the **median of the three triangulation methods** (DCF 5.34, OEM 3.94, Graham 2.37). The median is dragged down almost entirely by the **Graham Number (AUD 2.37)**, a formula (22.5 × EPS × book value per share) designed for asset-heavy industrial businesses trading near net asset value. It is poorly suited to Telstra: a capital-light-relative-to-earnings-power, high-ROE (14.8%), duopoly-protected network operator whose book value per share has been mechanically shrunk by the FY22–FY25 buyback program (equity fell from AUD 15.3bn to AUD 13.9bn while debt rose). Penalising Telstra for a low book value that management deliberately shrank via accretive-if-timed-well buybacks double-counts a capital-structure choice as a valuation flaw. The DCF method (AUD 5.34) — the model's own most theoretically complete method for a stable, cash-generative, regulated-adjacent business — sits far closer to both the current price and the analyst consensus, and the reverse-DCF cross-check (market implying 6.9% growth vs. the model's 9.0% assumption) supports it.

*(c) Adjusted assumption*: Rather than an unweighted median across three methods of very different applicability, I weight **DCF 55% / owner-earnings-multiple 35% / Graham 10%** (down-weighted, not discarded — it remains a useful floor/sanity check). No change to discount rate, growth, or exit-multiple assumption values — only the weighting used to blend the triangulation methods, which is a judgment call the model leaves to the analyst.

*(d) Reconciled IV*: 0.55 × 5.34 + 0.35 × 3.94 + 0.10 × 2.37 = **AUD 4.60** point estimate. Layering in the sensitivity-grid dispersion gives a reconciled range of **AUD 4.00–5.30** (midpoint ≈ AUD 4.65), which brackets the current price (AUD 4.89) and sits just below the analyst consensus target (AUD 5.22) — consistent with the "hold" rating both the sell side (14 analysts) and this reconciliation land on independently.

*(e) Residual gap*: Reconciled midpoint (AUD 4.65) is ~5% below the current price of AUD 4.89 — the stock is **not** on sale; if anything it carries a slight premium. This residual is attributable to the market pricing more durability into the mobile duopoly moat (and possibly a bond-proxy yield bid, given Telstra's 4.3% yield and 0.13 beta in a rate-cutting environment) than the DCF's 9.0% stage-1 owner-earnings growth fading to 2.5% terminal fully captures. This is a plausible, modest premium — not a red flag — but it means there is currently **no margin of safety** by the 30% Buffett standard.

**Recommended entry price**: At or below AUD 4.00 (top of the reconciled IV range's low end), ideally in the low AUD 3s to secure a genuine 30%+ discount to the AUD 4.65 midpoint.
