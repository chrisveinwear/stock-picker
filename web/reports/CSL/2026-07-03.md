## PART A — BUFFETT VALUE FRAMEWORK

### 1. Circle of Competence

**Inside the circle.** CSL's core business is plasma collection, fractionation, and sale of plasma-derived biologic therapies — primarily immunoglobulins (IVIG/SCIG), albumin, and coagulation factors through CSL Behring, plus the iron-deficiency and nephrology franchise (Ferinject/Mircera) through CSL Vifor, and a vaccines business currently being spun off as Seqirus. The business model is straightforward: collect donated plasma, fractionate it into protein products with no synthetic substitute, and sell them to hospitals and patients with immune deficiencies, haemophilia, and related chronic conditions. The economics are durable, the product is a medical necessity, and the barriers to entry are structural. A diligent investor can understand and track the key variables: plasma collection volume, yield per litre, product mix, and pricing versus demand. **Verdict: firmly inside.**

### 2. Key Assumptions the Thesis Depends On

1. **Write-downs are one-off.** The H1 FY2026 USD ~1.6bn write-downs (driving the 81% NPAT decline) are restructuring charges related to Vifor Pharma integration and asset rationalisation, not permanent impairment of the core plasma business. If further write-downs emerge, the earnings floor is lower than modelled.
2. **Behring margins recover to 17–18% EBIT.** The FY2025 Behring margin was under pressure from plasma collection cost inflation post-COVID. The thesis requires margin recovery as the plasma network scales and cost efficiencies from the 3,000 job reduction programme materialise by FY2027.
3. **Vifor core franchise (Ferinject/Mircera) grows at 8–10% p.a.** The iron-deficiency space is structurally underpenetrated globally. Vifor's hospital-channel iron infusion products have a durable position, and the acquisition—whatever the write-down pain—adds a second compounding engine. If biosimilar Ferinject reaches key European markets faster than expected, this assumption fails.
4. **CEO transition is manageable.** Paul McKenzie's shock exit in February 2026 is unsettling but not a moat-destroyer. The institutional knowledge resides in the plasma collection infrastructure and long-term hospital contracts, not a single executive. Interim CEO Gordon Naylor's credibility with the market will be tested at the August 2026 full-year results.
5. **No generic/biosimilar disruption to IVIG within five years.** IVIG is extraordinarily complex to manufacture biosimilarly; no approved biosimilar IVIG exists in any major market as of 2026. This structural protection is the core moat assumption. Any regulatory approval of a biosimilar IVIG in the US or EU would be a material sell trigger.

### 3. Business Quality & Moat

**Moat type:** Intangible assets (plasma donor network, decades of regulatory dossiers) + cost advantages (vertical integration from collection through fractionation) + switching costs (long-term hospital contracts, patient immunoglobulin therapies requiring continuity of supply).

**Moat strength:** Narrow (consistent with Morningstar's independent rating). CSL Behring holds approximately one-third of global plasma-derived immunoglobulin supply. The plasma collection network — with hundreds of FDA-licensed plasmapheresis centres across the US and Europe — took 50 years and billions of capital to build and cannot be replicated in any commercially relevant timeframe. IVIG and albumin have no synthetic substitutes; demand is structurally rising with ageing populations and expanded autoimmune indications. However, the moat is not "wide" because Grifols and Takeda operate comparable (if less efficient) networks, pricing power is constrained by hospital/government payers, and the Vifor franchise faces eventual biosimilar risk.

**Moat trend:** Narrowing at the margin over 2025–2026. The Vifor write-downs signal that CSL overpaid for that business and/or integration has been harder than forecast. CEO turnover raises execution risk. The plasma Behring moat itself — the genuine engine of value — is stable to widening as post-COVID plasma collection volumes recover and smaller US competitors (Grifols's US operations) remain capital-constrained.

**Five-year durability test:** The IVIG structural franchise is secure through 2031 given the biological complexity of biosimilar development and existing regulatory exclusivity periods. Vifor's iron franchise has a shorter horizon if biosimilar Ferinject arrives in Europe by 2027–2028. Net assessment: narrow moat, durable core.

### 4. Management Assessment

**Integrity:** No fraud or financial irregularity concerns identified. The write-down reflects a strategic miscalculation on Vifor (acquisition price discipline failure), not accounting manipulation. Auto-veto not triggered. However, the scale of the write-down (USD ~1.6bn, or roughly 5% of the Vifor acquisition cost of ~AUD 16bn) and the shock CEO departure raise questions about whether shareholders received full transparency in the year prior. **Yellow flag, not red.**

**Capital allocation track record:** Mixed. CSL compounded brilliantly for two decades under Brian McNamee, generating exceptional ROIC by reinvesting in plasma collection and targeted bolt-on R&D. The Vifor acquisition (USD ~11.7bn completed August 2022) was a large diversifying move that has so far destroyed capital — the write-downs confirm overpayment. The restructuring (3,000 job cuts, vaccines spin-off) reflects management admitting the strategy needed correction. FCF improved dramatically in FY2025 to USD 2,544m (from USD 1,506m in FY2024 and USD 909m in FY2023), suggesting the operational discipline is now reasserting itself.

**Owner mentality:** CSL was predominantly insider-managed through its first three decades as a public company. The current professional management team is less owner-aligned than the McNamee era. Insider ownership data is not provided; cannot quantify. The 47% payout ratio and maintenance of the dividend through restructuring suggests management prioritises capital returns and recognises shareholder interests.

**Institutional imperative warnings:** The Vifor acquisition has the hallmarks of institutional imperative — a large, complex, high-profile M&A that added management prestige but has yet to create shareholder value. The vaccines spin-off (Seqirus) signals a belated refocus on core competencies.
