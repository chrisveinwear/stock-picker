## PART A — BUFFETT VALUE FRAMEWORK

---

### 1. Circle of Competence

**Inside circle.** CSL is explicitly listed in the project's healthcare circle of competence alongside RHC and SHL. The core CSL Behring business — collecting human plasma, fractionating it into immunoglobulins, albumin, clotting factors, and speciality proteins — is mechanically complex but conceptually transparent: a biological supply chain with high barriers to entry and recurring, life-sustaining demand. The CSL Vifor nephrology/iron deficiency business adds pharmaceutical complexity but is still within the domain of understanding. The Seqirus vaccines division, now being spun off, was the most commoditised segment and arguably always sat at the boundary. Post-spin, the business becomes more coherent.

The H1 2026 one-off complexity (impairments, restructuring charges, CEO departure) requires careful normalisation but does not push the business outside the circle. This analysis proceeds.

---

### 2. Key Assumptions (the thesis depends on all of these)

1. **The H1 2026 profit collapse is non-recurring.** The 81% NPAT decline was driven by restructuring charges and Vifor-related impairments, not a collapse in underlying plasma demand or pricing power. If one-offs recur, the normalised earnings base is overstated.

2. **Normalised net margin recovers toward 18–19%.** The trailing net margin of 9.1% reflects distortion from one-off charges. Forward EPS of AUD 9.29 on AUD/USD 0.65-adjusted revenue implies a normalised margin of approximately 18–19%, consistent with FY25 reported NPAT of USD 3.0 billion on revenue of approximately USD 14–15 billion.

3. **The plasma market grows at mid-single-digits through 2030.** Immunoglobulin demand is structurally underpinned by rising diagnosis rates for primary immune deficiencies, CIDP, and related conditions. Historical plasma market growth has been 6–8%; a more conservative 4–5% is assumed post-COVID normalisation.

4. **Vifor integration stabilises; impairments do not recur materially.** CSL paid ~USD 11.7 billion for Vifor Pharma in 2022. The subsequent impairment charges suggest the acquisition was expensive. This analysis assumes the write-down cycle is substantially complete and that the nephrology business can generate adequate, if not spectacular, returns.

5. **CEO succession is resolved without further strategic disruption.** The shock departure of CEO Paul McKenzie in February 2026, followed by interim CEO Gordon Naylor, introduces management risk. This analysis assumes interim leadership holds the organisation steady and that a credible permanent appointment occurs within 12 months.

---

### 3. Business Quality & Moat

**Moat type:** Intangible assets (proprietary fractionation technology, manufacturing scale, regulatory approvals) + Cost advantage (scale in plasma collection and manufacturing)
**Moat strength:** Narrow (confirmed by Morningstar; consistent with this analysis)
**Moat trend:** Stable to narrowing

**Assessment:**

CSL Behring operates in an oligopolistic global plasma market. Four players — CSL, Takeda, Grifols, and Octapharma — control the majority of global plasma fractionation capacity. The capital barriers are formidable: a plasma fractionation plant costs USD 500 million–USD 1 billion to build, requires a decade of regulatory qualification, and depends on an integrated plasma collection network of hundreds of donor centres. CSL operates approximately 350 collection centres globally, creating a structural cost and supply advantage that a new entrant cannot replicate quickly.

The immunoglobulin franchise (IVIG/SCIG) is particularly strong. CSL's Hizentra (SCIG) and Privigen (IVIG) have differentiated delivery profiles, long-standing physician relationships, and switching costs embedded in patient treatment protocols. Albuminex (albumin) and HAEGARDA (hereditary angioedema) represent narrower but highly defensible product niches.

However, "narrow" is the right characterisation rather than "wide" for four reasons:

1. **Grifols and Takeda are not trivial competitors.** Both have invested heavily in collection capacity and continue to erode CSL's global share in certain markets.
2. **Vifor's nephrology portfolio** faces patent expiries on Ferinject (ferric carboxymaltose), its key iron deficiency drug, over the coming years. Biosimilar entry is a real risk.
3. **The Seqirus vaccine spin-off** confirms management's acknowledgement that diversification beyond core plasma has not generated the synergies originally anticipated.
4. **The 81% H1 2026 profit drop** and CEO departure suggest operational complexity that is inconsistent with a wide-moat business operating at full efficiency.

**Five-year moat durability test:** The plasma network, manufacturing infrastructure, and regulatory approvals are durable over five years. The Vifor nephrology moat is less durable due to patent cliff risk. Net assessment: moat survives five years but may narrow at the margin if Vifor underperforms and Grifols/Takeda close the collection gap.

### 4. Management Assessment

**Integrity:** No evidence of fraud or malfeasance. Auto-veto threshold not triggered.

**Capital allocation track record:** Mixed. Under prior CEO Paul Perreault, CSL compounded intrinsic value per share at approximately 15% per annum over a decade — a genuinely outstanding record. Under Paul McKenzie (CEO from 2022), the strategic direction was coherent (scale the plasma network, extract synergies from Vifor, spin Seqirus), but execution faltered. The Vifor acquisition at a peak-cycle multiple of approximately 25–30x EV/EBITDA appears in hindsight to have destroyed value; the subsequent impairments in H1 2026 reflect this reality. The vaccines spin-off, while strategically sensible, adds near-term restructuring cost and management distraction.

**Owner mentality:** Historically strong. Long-term incentive structures have emphasised total shareholder return and economic profit. The shift to a restructuring mode is a departure from the compounding playbook that built CSL's reputation.

**Institutional imperative warnings:** The Vifor acquisition was in part a "management growth imperative" decision — a large, transformative deal executed at an expensive multiple when organic growth was solid. This is a classic warning sign. The departure of the CEO who championed the deal, followed by an 81% profit decline in the first half of the following fiscal year, is precisely the outcome Buffett describes when management overreaches.

**Interim CEO Gordon Naylor:** Naylor is a senior CSL Behring executive with operational credibility. His public statements ("it is not a crisis") are appropriate for stabilising sentiment but are not a substitute for permanent, credentialed leadership. The CEO succession process is a key near-term risk factor.

**Verdict:** Management quality has deteriorated from "excellent" to "adequate." The prior era of compounding excellence is not currently reflected in operational outcomes. This warrants a higher margin of safety than CSL would normally require.

#### Valuation Reconciliation

*Required because my weighted IV midpoint (AUD 132.50) diverges from the code fair value (AUD 107.28) by approximately 23%, which exceeds the 20% threshold.*

**Step (a) — Input audit:**
All authoritative inputs are accepted as provided: current price AUD 115.91, shares 478.9m, EPS forward AUD 9.29, P/B 2.06, FCF USD 1.85bn, normalised earnings base AUD 4,183,300,002. No hallucinated inputs detected.

**Step (b) — What drives the gap:**
The divergence is driven entirely by one assumption: the **owner earnings multiple**. The code model assigns a 10x multiple to narrow-moat companies. This produces an owner-earnings-multiple valuation of AUD 87.35, which dominates the weighted average and pulls the code fair value to AUD 107.28. My adjusted multiple of 13x produces AUD 113.49, and when blended with the DCF (AUD 134.85) and Graham (AUD 107.28), the weighted IV rises to AUD 132.50.

**Step (c) — Proposed assumption adjustment:**
A 10x multiple implies CSL is equivalent to a commoditised narrow-moat company with no durable competitive position. I argue 13x is more appropriate because:
1. CSL is the global leader (#1 or #2) in plasma fractionation — a position not easily replicated
2. Normalised earnings are backed by structural, life-sustaining demand (immunoglobulins for immune deficiencies)
3. The 81% H1 2026 profit drop is demonstrably non-recurring (one-off charges confirmed by management)
4. Even at trough, CSL generated USD 1.85bn FCF and AUD 3.7% dividend yield — inconsistent with a 10x distressed multiple

I retain 13x (not 15x or 17x) to reflect genuine execution risk from the Vifor integration failure and CEO vacancy.

**Step (d) — Reconciled IV:** AUD 110–155, midpoint AUD 132.50. Residual gap to code fair value of AUD 107.28: +23.5%. Gap to analyst consensus of AUD 140.08: −5.4% (within noise). Gap to Morningstar AUD 165.59: −20%.

**Step (e) — Model warnings noted:**
- WACC raw at 4.4% was floored to 8.0% (correct; 4.4% would mechanically overprice the stock)
- IV swings 43% across sensitivity grid — wide confidence interval warranting larger MOS requirement

**Morningstar reconciliation (external cross-check):**
Morningstar rates CSL at P/FV = 0.70, implying fair value AUD 165.59 — 25% above my midpoint of AUD 132.50. Morningstar's higher estimate likely reflects: (a) a higher normalised net margin assumption (Morningstar may use a 20–22% normalised margin rather than 18–19%); (b) a more optimistic Vifor recovery scenario; and/or (c) a higher terminal growth rate. My estimate is more conservative on the Vifor assumption and uses a slightly lower multiple. Both estimates agree the stock is currently undervalued — the key question is the quantum of discount. I maintain my more conservative estimate given execution risk.
