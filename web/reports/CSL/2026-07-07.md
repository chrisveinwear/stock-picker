## PART A — BUFFETT VALUE FRAMEWORK

### 1. Circle of Competence

**Inside the circle — with a bounded caveat.**

CSL's core business is straightforwardly understandable: collect human plasma through a global network of more than 320 donation centres, fractionate it into life-saving proteins — immunoglobulins, albumin, clotting factors, and specialty proteins — then distribute them to hospitals treating patients with primary immune deficiencies, haemophilia, rare bleeding disorders, and burns. The economics are clear: a natural raw material near-monopolised by scale and geography, a purified biologic product with no commodity substitute, and a captive patient population requiring lifelong treatment. This is CSL Behring, and it has operated this model for decades with consistently high returns on invested capital.

The caveat is CSL Vifor, acquired in January 2022 for approximately USD 11.7 billion. Vifor specialises in intravenous iron treatment for iron deficiency anaemia (Ferinject, Venofer) and renal anaemia management. This is a pharmaceutical business with more conventional risk: patent cycles, formulary battles, biosimilar competition, and pipeline execution risk. It is comprehensible but requires a different analytical lens. The H1 FY2026 write-downs of approximately AUD 1.6bn suggest the Vifor thesis has disappointed, and isolating its ongoing earnings power requires more forensic work than the Behring franchise demands.

**Conclusion: Inside the circle for CSL Behring; at the boundary for CSL Vifor. Proceed with heightened scrutiny on Vifor segment economics and proceed on the basis that Behring anchors the intrinsic value.**

### 2. Key Assumptions (5)

The investment thesis rests on these five explicit assumptions. Each should be verified at every quarterly monitoring date.

1. **Plasma demand growth continues at 6–9% per annum.** Immunoglobulin demand is structurally growing globally as diagnosis rates improve, treatment penetration rises, and subcutaneous delivery broadens the patient population. This assumption underpins 7–9% revenue CAGR for Behring over the next five years and is the dominant driver of the bull case.

2. **Vifor stabilises after the write-down.** The AUD ~1.6bn H1 FY2026 write-down does not represent complete impairment of Vifor's earnings power. The base Ferinject/Venofer franchise retains a marketable cash-generating position, and the write-down reflects the acquisition premium paid, not the business going to zero. Monitoring the Vifor EBIT contribution in H2 FY2026 results is critical.

3. **Operating margins recover toward 33–35% by FY2027–28.** The trailing 31.6% operating margin reflects the disruption of restructuring and integration costs. The restructuring program (headcount reductions, site consolidations, Vifor operational improvements) is assumed to restore margins toward historical levels.

4. **Permanent CEO appointment occurs within 12 months.** Interim CEO Gordon Naylor has stabilised the business and is publicly asking for investor patience. The thesis requires a credible permanent appointment with a clear mandate. Failure to appoint within 12 months would be a negative thesis signal.

5. **Free cash flow of USD 2.0–2.5bn annually services debt without an equity raise.** FY2025 FCF was USD 2,544m — a strong recovery from the USD 909m trough in FY2023. Continued capex discipline (FY2025 capex USD 1,017m vs USD 1,692m in FY2023) and organic earnings growth support this assumption.

### 3. Business Quality & Moat

**Moat type:** Cost advantage + intangible assets (regulatory approvals, collection network scale)
**Moat strength:** Narrow (Morningstar-consistent; confirmed here)
**Moat trend:** Stable-to-narrowing (Vifor dilutes overall quality; Behring moat is stable)

**CSL Behring** is the dominant value driver and the source of moat. The plasma fractionation business benefits from several structural advantages:

- **Scale-dependent collection infrastructure.** CSL's 320+ plasma donation centres in the US (the world's dominant plasma supply market), Europe, and China represent a network that took decades and billions of dollars to build. Regulatory approval for each centre, donor recruitment and retention systems, and cold-chain logistics create a barrier that newcomers simply cannot replicate in any reasonable timeframe. This is a genuine cost-advantage moat.
- **Regulatory barriers to biologic competition.** Plasma-derived biologics (Privigen, Hizentra, Kcentra, Berinert) require extensive clinical evidence for any regulatory approval. There is no equivalent to a small-molecule generic pathway. The FDA and EMA's biologics licensing requirements effectively protect the existing franchise for the foreseeable future.
- **Manufacturing economies of scale.** Plasma fractionation is highly capital-intensive with significant economies of scale — each additional litre processed spreads fixed manufacturing costs across a larger output, compressing unit costs. CSL's scale puts it at or near the industry cost frontier alongside Grifols and Takeda/BioLife.

**Moat limitations:** Plasma therapies face government and insurer pricing pressure, particularly in the US government payer channel. IRA drug negotiation risk (though plasma-derived biologics have different regulatory treatment from small molecules) and European reference pricing compress long-run pricing growth. The Vifor franchise lacks these structural defences — intravenous iron competes with Pharmacosmos (Monofer) and faces biosimilar pressure on older agents.

**ROIC context:** Using FY2025 data — net income USD 3,002m on invested capital of approximately USD 19,335m equity + USD 10,000m net debt = USD 29,335m — produces ROIC of approximately 10.2%. This is below the 15%+ threshold of a wide-moat business and reflects the Vifor acquisition goodwill sitting in the invested capital base. Pre-Vifor (FY2021 and earlier), CSL's ROIC consistently exceeded 25%. The moat itself has not deteriorated; it has been masked by an expensive acquisition and its associated restructuring costs.

**Five-year moat durability test:** The Behring plasma franchise is highly durable — collection infrastructure and manufacturing scale do not erode quickly, and global plasma demand is structurally growing. The Vifor franchise faces competitive pressure but retains its position in established markets. Overall assessment: narrow moat, stable for Behring, under pressure for Vifor. Moat will not be destroyed in the next five years barring a plasma safety crisis or a dramatic biosimilar incursion.

### 4. Management Assessment

**Integrity:** No concerns identified. CSL has maintained transparent disclosure standards through a genuinely difficult period. The H1 FY2026 write-downs were clearly explained and attributed to identifiable impairments. No earnings management signals.

**Capital allocation track record (mixed):** Prior to the Vifor acquisition, CSL's capital allocation record was outstanding. Disciplined bolt-on acquisitions (Aventis Behring 2004, Talecris 2011, Novartis influenza vaccines 2015), consistent R&D reinvestment, and a combination of dividends and buybacks produced decades of compounding. The Vifor acquisition at approximately USD 11.7bn — roughly 7× Vifor's revenue and a significant premium to public market equivalents — was aggressive. The AUD ~1.6bn write-down confirms the purchase price embedded optimistic integration and growth assumptions that have not materialised. This is a significant capital allocation error by any standard and would disqualify a management team from Warren Buffett's highest tier of confidence.

**CEO departure:** Paul McKenzie, who championed the Vifor transaction, departed in early 2026. The timing and shock nature of the departure, combined with the 81% NPAT decline, suggests the board lost confidence. Interim CEO Gordon Naylor's public plea for investor patience ("It is not a crisis") is reasonable but signals that the strategic response is still being formulated rather than executed.

**Owner mentality:** The pre-Vifor leadership team and board behaved as long-term stewards. The post-Vifor track record is under review. The business continues to invest in plasma collection infrastructure — a sign that long-term thinking persists at the operational level.

**Institutional imperative risk (elevated):** The Vifor acquisition exhibited the institutional imperative — pressure to deploy cash into a large deal to demonstrate strategic growth. A smaller, more disciplined acquisition would have been preferable. The new permanent CEO must demonstrate a return to Buffett-style capital discipline: bolt-on acquisitions at fair prices, organic investment at high returns, and buybacks when below intrinsic value.

**Verdict:** Integrity passes; capital allocation carries a documented failure (Vifor). Monitoring trigger: permanent CEO announcement and their stated capital allocation philosophy.
