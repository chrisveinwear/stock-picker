## PART A — BUFFETT VALUE FRAMEWORK

---

### 1. Circle of Competence

CSL is the world's leading plasma protein biotherapeutics company (CSL Behring), the second-largest global influenza vaccine manufacturer (Seqirus), and the global leader in iron-deficiency treatment for dialysis patients (CSL Vifor). The business is squarely **inside the circle of competence**. The economic logic is simple even if the science is complex: collect human plasma from a network of 900+ donor centres, fractionate it into life-saving proteins (albumin, immunoglobulins, clotting factors), and sell them under multi-year hospital supply contracts at high margins to patients who have no viable alternative. Demand is non-discretionary, patient-sticky, and growing with the ageing global population. **Inside the circle.**

---

### 2. Key Assumptions (3–5 Explicit)

1. **Plasma collection volumes grow 5–8% annually through FY28**, normalising from the COVID suppression period as 300+ new collection centres opened since 2020 reach full productivity.
2. **CSL Vifor generates positive segment EBITDA by FY27** after restructuring; the ~US$1.6B write-down recorded in H1 FY26 is a one-off goodwill/intangibles impairment, not a signal of permanent operating loss in the iron-deficiency franchise.
3. **The Seqirus strategic review (due Q4 CY2026) is value-neutral to accretive**, either via a spin-off unlocking $8–12B in hidden NAV or via confirmation of a renewed long-term standalone role within CSL, allowing CSL Behring to re-rate as a purer-play plasma compounder.
4. **A permanent CEO is appointed by end of CY2026** and maintains strategic continuity; Gordon Naylor's interim stewardship preserves institutional knowledge through the transition.
5. **AUDUSD remains in the 0.62–0.68 range**, meaning ~80% USD-denominated earnings translate at broadly favourable rates into AUD share price terms throughout the holding period.

### 3. Business Quality & Moat

**Moat type:** Multi-layered — Scale/Cost + Intangible (Regulatory/IP) + Switching Costs
**Moat strength:** Wide
**Moat trend:** Stable (Behring + Seqirus wide; Vifor narrow — creates near-term headline noise)

**CSL Behring — the core moat:**

The plasma collection network is the most important competitive asset. 900+ donor centres across 50+ countries represent a 15–20 year build and $8–10B of replacement value that cannot be replicated quickly by any competitor. Plasma is a biological raw material with finite global supply; controlling the donor relationship means controlling the production input. Beyond collection:

- **Fractionation technology:** Separating plasma into albumin, immunoglobulins, clotting factors, and hyperimmune specialties at best-in-class yields requires capital-intensive manufacturing subject to FDA/TGA/EMA biologic standards. CSL's Kankakee (Illinois) and Broadmeadows (Victoria) facilities operate at yields meaningfully above the industry average — a durable cost advantage.
- **Regulatory exclusivity:** US biologics receive 12-year market exclusivity vs 5 years for small molecules. Plasma-derived biologics face minimal biosimilar competition because the products are inherently variable and source-dependent — no two batches are identical, and regulators have been conservative in approving substitution.
- **Physician loyalty and patient stickiness:** Switching a primary immunodeficiency or haemophilia patient from one IVIG or Factor VIII brand to another carries meaningful clinical risk. Physicians rarely do so without compelling clinical evidence. This creates quasi-subscription demand.
- **R&D pipeline:** CSL112 (high-dose apoA-I infusion for acute coronary syndrome), GARADACIMAB (hereditary angioedema), and gene therapy partnerships create option value beyond the base plasma franchise.

**Seqirus** holds the #2 global influenza vaccine position, with long-term government supply contracts (US BARDA, Australian government) providing revenue visibility. The cell-culture and adjuvanted vaccine technology (FLUAD, FLUCELVAX) is differentiated.

**CSL Vifor** has a narrower moat — primarily brand loyalty in Ferinject/Injectafer within nephrology/dialysis, where switching is slow but possible. Vifor was acquired in 2022 at what is now clearly a cycle-peak price (US$11.7B). The integration has been harder than expected. A spin-off or disposal of Vifor would narrow the headline business but likely widen the perceived quality of the remaining CSL Behring + Seqirus combination.

**Five-year moat durability test:** The Behring plasma moat is one of the most durable in global healthcare — it will be as wide or wider in 2031 as it is today, underpinned by growing demand and high barriers to new plasma collection infrastructure. **Passes the five-year test comfortably.**

### 4. Management Assessment

**Integrity check:**
The departure of CEO Paul Perreault in February 2026 was sudden but appears to reflect performance accountability — the Vifor write-down, FY25 guidance miss, and restructuring announcement were attributed to strategic decisions made on his watch. There is no evidence of fraud, self-dealing, regulatory misconduct, or financial statement manipulation. A board removing underperforming management is a governance positive, not a negative. **No auto-veto triggered.**

**Capital allocation track record:**

| Decision | Verdict |
|----------|---------|
| Novartis flu vaccine acquisition (2015, US$275M) | Excellent — created Seqirus, now worth ~$8–10B; paid 30¢ for a dollar |
| Organic plasma centre expansion (continuous) | Disciplined and ROI-positive; each new centre pays back within 5–7 years |
| Aventis Behring acquisition (2004) and ZLB Bioplasma (2000) | Foundation-building; excellent long-term value creation |
| Vifor Pharma acquisition (2022, US$11.7B) | **Blemish** — paid a premium multiple in a competitive auction at cycle peak; Vifor EBITDA has underdelivered and required $1.6B in goodwill write-downs by H1 FY26 |
| Dividend policy (50% payout) | Consistent; balanced between shareholder return and capex funding |

**Owner mentality:** CSL has historically been managed with generational thinking — maintaining R&D through downturns, expanding the plasma collection network ahead of demand rather than reactively. The Vifor decision broke the pattern by showing the institutional imperative: the board felt pressure to deploy capital and scale into specialty pharma at a point in the cycle when competition for assets was fierce.

**Institutional imperative warning:** Vifor is the textbook example — a large, well-regarded company acquiring a business outside its deepest competency at a peak price because peers were also acquiring. This is the one stain on an otherwise superior record.

**Interim CEO Gordon Naylor** (previously CFO): financially disciplined; known as a cost-cutter and restructuring executor. Appropriate for the current phase. Not a visionary product leader but exactly what a company in restructuring mode needs.

**Overall management score: 7/10** — Excellent 20-year track record with one significant capital allocation error; currently headless at the CEO level which warrants a discount.

At 8.0% WACC: AUD $358 per share. At 9.8% WACC: AUD $292 per share.

**Method 2: Graham Number (conservative floor)**
- FY27E underlying EPS: AUD $11.92
- Book value per share: ~US$37.0 = AUD $58.7
- Graham Number: √(22.5 × $11.92 × $58.7) = √15,741 = **AUD $125**

The Graham Number appropriately signals a conservative floor — it assumes an average business. CSL deserves a meaningful premium above this floor for moat quality, growth, and cash conversion. The relevant benchmark is 20–28× normalised earnings.

**Method 3: Earnings Yield vs Bond Rate**
- Underlying FY27E EPS: AUD $11.92
- At AUD $244: earnings yield = **4.88%**
- 10-year Australian government bond yield: ~4.35%
- Earnings yield premium: +53bp — modestly attractive for a business growing earnings at 8–10% p.a.
- For a 10% required return: implied entry price = AUD $119 (too conservative; ignores growth)
- For 8% required return (appropriate for a wide-moat compounder with reliable growth): implied entry = AUD $149

*Note: Pure earnings yield methods systematically undervalue growing compounders. The appropriate framework is to pay 20–25× normalised earnings for a business with CSL's durability.*

## Summary — Putting It All Together

CSL is a world-class business with a genuinely wide moat built on irreplaceable plasma infrastructure, regulatory barriers, and decades of manufacturing expertise. The 2022 Vifor acquisition was a capital allocation error — overpaying at cycle peak in a competitive auction for a business outside CSL's deepest competency. The consequences of that error have now been largely absorbed via the H1 FY26 write-down. The residual risk is that Vifor continues to require operational support or further impairment — this is the primary **known unknown** in the investment case.

The core business (CSL Behring) is healthier than sentiment implies. Plasma collection volumes have normalised post-COVID; pricing power in the IVIG and albumin markets is intact; and the restructuring announced in August 2025 (3,000 job cuts, Seqirus review) is appropriate cost discipline for a business that grew its cost base through a period of elevated COVID-era revenue.

At AUD $244, the stock trades at a 25% discount to the midpoint intrinsic value of $325. This is a meaningful discount for a business of this quality, but not yet the 30% that the Buffett framework requires before committing capital with confidence. The CEO vacancy is a genuine overhang — not because of management quality risk, but because the market will not fully re-rate a business without a credible permanent leader.

**The path to a BUY rating:**
1. Price declines to AUD $228 (30% MOS to midpoint IV), **OR**
2. A credible permanent CEO is announced, compressing the leadership discount by ~5%, effectively lowering the required entry price, **OR**
3. FY26 full-year results on August 18 deliver a meaningful beat and positive FY27 guidance, shifting the IV midpoint higher and creating a buy trigger at or near current price levels

Until one of these catalysts materialises, CSL belongs firmly on the watchlist — a business Buffett would know, respect, and be prepared to buy, waiting for Mr Market to offer one more moment of pessimism.
