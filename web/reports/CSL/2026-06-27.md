## PART A — BUFFETT VALUE FRAMEWORK

---

### 1. Circle of Competence

**Verdict: Inside the circle.**

CSL's core business — collecting human plasma, fractionating it into life-saving protein therapies, and selling those therapies to patients with rare chronic diseases — is one of the most understandable recurring-revenue businesses in global healthcare. The science of plasma fractionation is complex, but the business model is simple:

- Patients with conditions like Primary Immunodeficiency (PI), Haemophilia A/B, Hereditary Angioedema, and immune thrombocytopenia require CSL's therapies for life. There is no substitute and no discretion in the purchase.
- CSL operates 340+ plasma donation centres across the United States, paying donors a fee and then processing the plasma into a portfolio of ~30 differentiated biologic products.
- CSL Behring (plasma therapies) generated the vast majority of FY25 revenue and essentially all of its economic value.
- CSL Vifor (iron deficiency / renal anaemia) and CSL Seqirus (influenza vaccines, being spun off) are adjacent businesses that a value investor can analyse with standard frameworks.

The business model is analogous to a toll road: CSL owns irreplaceable infrastructure (the plasma collection and manufacturing network), charges recurring tolls (product pricing), and benefits from a captive, growing user base. This is firmly inside the circle of competence.

**Complexity caveat:** The Vifor acquisition (2022, ~US$11.7B) introduced nephrology renal iron management, which involves hospital reimbursement dynamics and pharmaceutical sales cycles that are somewhat harder to model. Monitoring required.

### 2. Key Assumptions (The Thesis Depends On These)

1. **The 81% H1 FY26 profit decline is accounting noise, not economic reality.** The $1.6B in write-downs (likely Vifor goodwill impairment and restructuring charges) are one-off balance-sheet adjustments. The underlying cash generation of the Behring plasma business must be confirmed to be intact. If the writedowns reflect permanent economic impairment of the Vifor franchise, the thesis is materially weaker.

2. **Plasma demand continues to grow at 5–8% per annum.** Global plasma-derived therapy demand is driven by better diagnosis rates for rare disease, geographic expansion into emerging markets, and new clinical indications. This assumption is structural and multi-decade, not cyclical.

3. **A permanent CEO of calibre is appointed promptly.** The shock departure of Paul McKenzie in February 2026 creates near-term operational and strategic risk. Interim CEO Gordon Naylor is a known quantity internally but investors need a credible permanent leader to restore confidence.

4. **The Seqirus spin-off is value-neutral or value-accretive.** If the vaccines business is spun off cleanly with manageable stranded costs, the residual CSL (Behring + Vifor) will emerge with a sharper capital allocation focus and potentially higher multiple. If execution is messy, there is a meaningful valuation drag.

5. **Restructuring delivers AUD 250M+ in annualised cost savings.** The 3,000-job reduction and operational simplification should translate into margin recovery at Behring. Failure to deliver on this creates earnings-per-share dilution relative to current analyst consensus.

### 3. Business Quality & Moat

**Moat Type:** Intangible Assets + Cost Advantage + Switching Costs
**Moat Width:** Wide
**Moat Trend:** Stable (narrowing slightly at Vifor, stable-to-widening at Behring)

#### The Plasma Collection Network — The Crown Jewel

CSL Behring's plasma collection network is one of the most durable competitive advantages in global business. Consider what a competitor would need to replicate it:

- **300+ FDA-licensed collection centres** across the United States, each requiring FDA approval, accreditation by the AABB, and years of operational history before achieving volume productivity.
- **30+ years of accumulated operational know-how** in donor recruitment, retention, apheresis technique, and quality management.
- **Manufacturing scale** at the Kankakee, Illinois fractionation facility (one of the world's largest), and four other global manufacturing plants. Plasma fractionation yields are highly sensitive to scale — larger batches produce a more complete portfolio of products, dramatically reducing per-unit cost.
- **Regulatory exclusivity** for each biologic product (not patent protection — regulatory dossiers and manufacturing process approval, which are arguably more durable than patents).
- **30 years of pharmacovigilance data** on tens of millions of patient-doses, which constitutes an irreplaceable safety record that new entrants cannot acquire through capital spending alone.

The total capital investment required to replicate CSL's network from scratch is estimated at US$20B+ over 15–20 years, with no guarantee of achieving CSL's yield or regulatory standing. This is a structural moat.

#### Five-Year Moat Durability Test

| Threat | Timeline | Assessment |
|--------|----------|------------|
| Gene therapy (e.g. Roctavian, Hemgenix) for haemophilia | 2–5 years for meaningful uptake | Real but manageable — affects ~15% of Behring revenue; plasma immunoglobulins (the larger business) are unaffected |

**Durability verdict: Wide moat with high confidence over a five-year horizon. The moat at CSL Vifor is narrower (iron therapy has biosimilar pressure and hospital formulary competition).**

### 4. Management Assessment

**Integrity:** Pass (no fraud, no governance scandal) — but yellow flag on capital allocation discipline.

**Capital Allocation Track Record (Historical):**
Prior to the Vifor acquisition, CSL's management demonstrated exceptional capital allocation skill over three decades. ROIC averaged 25–35%. The company consistently returned excess capital via buybacks at sensible prices and funded organic growth (plasma centre expansion) with discipline. The decision to expand plasma collection capacity post-COVID through the Rika programme showed strong operational foresight.

**The Vifor Acquisition — A Yellow Flag:**
CSL paid US$11.7B for Vifor Pharma in December 2021, acquiring it at the height of healthcare M&A valuations. The subsequent writedown of $1.6B in H1 FY26 and management's own "shock" language around write-downs suggests the acquisition price was inflated. The hallmarks of the institutional imperative are present: pressure to demonstrate growth (CSL was trading at 40x PE), board enthusiasm for transformational deals, and a price paid that left no margin of safety.

This does not call management's integrity into question — but it represents a departure from Buffett's first rule: do not lose capital. A $1.6B+ writedown on an $11.7B acquisition within four years is a material error, albeit partially explained by the broader biopharma de-rating.

**CEO Departure (Paul McKenzie, February 2026):**
The shock nature of McKenzie's exit — announced simultaneously with an 81% profit decline — is the single largest near-term concern. Interim CEO Gordon Naylor's public "pleading for patience" is not language that inspires confidence. A permanent appointment with a credible operational track record is the most important near-term catalyst.

**Owner Mentality:** The CSL board and management have historically behaved like owners. The decision to cut 3,000 jobs (painful but necessary) and spin off the lower-quality Seqirus business signals a return to portfolio discipline. This is a positive sign.

**Verdict:** Management quality is in transition. The long-run track record is excellent. The Vifor overpayment and CEO departure are blemishes. Monitor for permanent CEO appointment and whether the FY26 full-year results reveal any additional legacy writedowns.

#### Method 2: Graham Number

Graham Number = √(22.5 × EPS × BVPS)

- EPS (AUD, FY25): AUD 10.74
- BVPS: Net assets ~US$14.5B / 430M shares = US$33.7 = AUD 51.8
- Graham Number: √(22.5 × 10.74 × 51.8) = √(12,521) = **AUD 111.9**

This is well below the current price and confirms CSL is not a Graham-style net-asset bargain. This is expected and appropriate — CSL's value lies in its earnings power and moat, not its book value. The Graham Number is not the primary valuation method for a high-ROIC business. Use as a floor check only.

#### Method 3: Earnings Yield vs Bond Rate

- FY25 EPS (AUD): AUD 10.74
- FY27E EPS (consensus, AUD): ~AUD 13.20 (recovery year post-restructuring)
- Earnings yield at AUD 265: 10.74/265 = 4.1% (FY25) or 13.20/265 = 4.98% (FY27)
- Australian 10-year bond yield (June 2026): ~4.3%
- Equity risk premium offered by CSL: −0.2% on FY25 basis; +0.7% on FY27 basis
- **Conclusion:** On current earnings, CSL offers no premium over the risk-free rate. On FY27 consensus recovery estimates, there is a thin 70bp equity risk premium — insufficient for a business with execution risk.
