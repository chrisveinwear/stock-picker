## PART A — BUFFETT VALUE FRAMEWORK

### 1. Circle of Competence
**Inside.** CSL's core business — fractionating human plasma into immunoglobulins, albumin, and coagulation factors, plus manufacturing influenza vaccines — is understandable, capital-intensive, and regulated. The plasma supply chain (collection centres → fractionation → distribution) has high barriers: FDA/EMA licences take years, donor centres require density, and fractionation yields are process-dependent. The Vifor acquisition (iron deficiency, nephrology) and CSL Seqirus (vaccines) extend into adjacent therapeutic areas but remain within the biopharmaceutical manufacturing circle. The recent strategic pivot (spinning off Seqirus, cutting 3,000 jobs, AUD 1.6bn write-downs) tests whether management stays within its circle or chases growth outside it.

### 2. Key Assumptions (3–5)
1. **Plasma supply normalises**: Donor volumes recover to pre-COVID trends (FY25 revenue +5% YoY suggests this is underway).
2. **Immunoglobulin (Ig) pricing power holds**: Ig represents ~60% of CSL Behring revenue; annual price increases of 3–5% are embedded in contracts.
3. **Vifor integration synergies materialise**: AUD 300m+ cost synergies by FY27; cross-selling iron products (Ferinject) to CSL's hospital network.
4. **Seqirus spin-out unlocks value**: Separating the volatile, lower-margin vaccine business (CSL Seqirus) allows both entities to allocate capital more efficiently; no fire-sale valuation.
5. **Regulatory risk contained**: No further FDA warning letters or facility shutdowns (2023–24 Broadmeadows/Brussels issues resolved).

### 3. Business Quality & Moat
**Moat type**: Intangible assets (licences, fractionation know-how) + Switching costs (hospital formularies, patient familiarity with specific Ig brands) + Scale (world's largest plasma collector: 320+ centres, ~14m donations/year).
**Strength**: **Narrow** (both code model and Morningstar agree). Not "wide" because: (a) Ig faces biosimilar/subcutaneous competition long-term; (b) vaccine business is commodity-like; (c) Vifor's nephrology franchise has patent cliffs (Ferroport expiry 2028–30).
**Trend**: **Narrowing**. The 81% H1 FY26 profit drop (AUD 1.6bn write-downs on Vifor intangibles, Seqirus inventory), CEO departure, and restructuring signal execution risk. Five-year durability test: Ig demand grows 6–8% annually (ageing populations, new indications), but supply growth from Grifols, Takeda, Octapharma, and Chinese entrants (Boyalife, Tiantan) pressures pricing. Moat holds if CSL maintains ~40% global Ig share and >50% plasma collection share.

### 4. Management Assessment
**Integrity**: **Pass** (no accounting fraud, related-party dealing, or options backdating). However, the Vifor acquisition (AUD 17bn, 2022) at 4.5× sales with heavy goodwill (AUD 8.6bn) now written down smells of "institutional imperative" — doing deals because peers do.
**Capital allocation**: Mixed. Pre-Vifor: disciplined buybacks, steady dividend growth (28-year streak), ROIC >20%. Post-Vifor: ROIC collapsed to ~6–7% (net income / invested capital), debt/EBITDA ~2.3×, buybacks paused. The 3,000 job cuts and Seqirus spin-out suggest course correction.
**Owner mentality**: Management owns ~0.5% of shares (modest). Interim CEO Gordon Naylor (ex-COO) is a company lifer — continuity over change.
**Red flag**: The shock CEO exit (Paul McKenzie, Feb 2026) after 6 years, combined with "clearly not satisfied" board commentary, suggests the board lost confidence in the integration thesis. Watch for new CEO's capital allocation framework.
