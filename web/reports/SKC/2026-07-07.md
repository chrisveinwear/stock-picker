## PART A — BUFFETT VALUE FRAMEWORK

---

### 1. Circle of Competence

SkyCity's business model is structurally simple: it operates government-licensed integrated casino-entertainment complexes and earns revenue from the mathematical edge embedded in its gaming tables and electronic gaming machines, supplemented by food and beverage, accommodation, and events. A child can explain the casino business — the house always wins on average, and the license prevents competition.

**Verdict: Boundary.** The commercial economics are inside the circle. The complexity arises from a layered regulatory environment spanning two sovereign jurisdictions (New Zealand and Australia), each with active licensing authorities (NZ Department of Internal Affairs, South Australia Consumer and Business Services), anti-money-laundering obligations under AUSTRAC and the NZ AML/CFT Act, and a history of enforcement action that has demonstrated the regulatory relationship is adversarial rather than routine. The interaction between regulatory discretion and commercial value sits at the outer edge of what can be confidently modelled. We proceed, but with explicit acknowledgment that regulatory outcomes are a material unknown that no financial model resolves.

---

### 2. Key Assumptions (Five — to be verified quarterly)

1. **Adelaide redevelopment completes and ramps.** The Adelaide Entertainment Centre capex programme (NZD 311m in FY24, NZD 164m in FY25) concludes by late FY26 or early FY27, and the new facilities drive meaningful incremental revenue. Without this, the capex destroys value rather than creates it.

2. **No further material regulatory enforcement.** The AML/CTF failures in both NZ and Adelaide have been substantially rectified under new management. No additional license restriction, suspension, or civil penalty materialises in the next 12–24 months that would further impair operating capacity.

3. **NZD/AUD remains near 0.89 (±5%).** SkyCity reports in NZD; its ASX equity is denominated in AUD. A 10% sustained NZD depreciation relative to AUD would reduce AUD-equivalent earnings by 10% and compress our AUD intrinsic value estimate by approximately AUD 0.06 at the central case.

4. **Debt is refinanced at current or lower rates; no equity dilution.** With NZD 803m in gross debt and NZD 51m cash, the company must continue to roll its facilities. Forced equity issuance at the current depressed price would be significantly dilutive to per-share intrinsic value.

5. **NZ gaming volumes stabilise.** Auckland remains SkyCity's earnings engine. The assumption is that online gambling growth takes market share at the margin but does not fundamentally impair total visitation or gaming revenue at the flagship property. This is the most uncertain structural assumption.

---

### 3. Business Quality and Economic Moat

**Moat type:** Government-granted license monopoly. SkyCity Auckland holds the only 24/7 casino license in New Zealand's largest city, operating under a license that runs to 2048. SkyCity Adelaide holds the exclusive casino license for South Australia. These are legally enforced barriers to entry that no competitor can replicate without government action.

**Moat strength:** Narrow — and trending narrower. Morningstar rates the moat as **None**, a view I partially share. The Auckland license is real and has 22 years of term, but the proven AML failures in both Auckland and Adelaide have materially damaged the relationship between SkyCity and its licensing authorities. A license that is periodically restricted (gaming floor closures, patron capacity limits) or that carries conditions requiring costly compliance infrastructure is a weaker moat than its statutory term suggests. The key test is whether a competitor could emerge: structurally, no. But the risk of effective impairment via regulatory restriction is meaningfully elevated versus three years ago.

**Moat trend:** Narrowing. The governance failures under previous management eroded regulatory goodwill that took decades to build. New management may restore the relationship, but this will require demonstrated operational compliance over multiple reporting cycles.

**Five-year moat durability test:** The Auckland license survives regardless of management quality — revocation is an extreme outcome that would require egregious repeated violations. Adelaide's license is more exposed given the AUSTRAC history. The moat's durability over five years depends heavily on management execution, not on competitive dynamics. I assign a 70% probability the moat remains narrow (constrained but intact) and a 20% probability it deteriorates further (operational restrictions tighten). Full revocation (10% tail) would be catastrophic.

### 4. Management Assessment

**Integrity:** Previous management presided over significant AML/CTF compliance failures across both the Auckland and Adelaide operations. These failures were not inadvertent administrative errors — they represented systemic failures in customer due diligence, transaction monitoring, and responsible gambling obligations over extended periods. This is an automatic integrity flag. New CEO Jason Walbridge (appointed 2024) has not yet accumulated enough of a track record to override this historical flag. **Assessment: Provisional negative; monitoring required over the next 2–3 reporting cycles.**

**Capital allocation track record:** The Adelaide Entertainment Centre expansion (estimated total capex NZD 475m+ across FY23–25) is the dominant capital decision of the past three years. At NZD 311m in FY24 alone, it consumed the entirety of operating cash generation and then some, driving sustained negative FCF. This was a large, committed bet made under significant uncertainty. If Adelaide delivers, the capital allocation will look prescient; if it does not, it will look reckless. The outcome is not yet known. Separately, the decision to proceed with this level of capex while under regulatory scrutiny and with rising debt was aggressive — it left no margin of safety on the balance sheet.

**Owner mentality:** Institutional ownership is the primary shareholder structure. AustralianSuper's recent disclosed reduction from a higher stake to 8.32% voting power is a mild negative signal — a long-term institutional investor trimming a position typically signals reduced conviction or a rebalancing away from risk.

**Institutional imperative warning:** The historical regulatory failures are a textbook example of management following the institutional imperative — optimising for short-term gaming yield metrics (turnover, margin per seat) at the expense of compliance obligations, because every other listed casino operator was also pushing the limits. The new management must actively break this cultural pattern.
