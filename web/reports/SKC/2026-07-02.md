## PART A — BUFFETT VALUE FRAMEWORK

---

### 1. Circle of Competence

**Verdict: On the boundary — proceed with caution, do not rely on memory of better days.**

The casino operating model is surface-level simple: admit patrons, collect gaming losses across tables and electronic gaming machines, supplement with hotel and food-and-beverage revenue. Revenue is structurally sticky because regulated exclusivity removes competitive pressure on the core gaming product.

However, the layers beneath are non-trivial for a Buffett analysis:

- **Regulatory complexity:** Casino licences in New Zealand and Australia are granted, monitored, and can be revoked by government. The rules governing anti-money laundering (AML), counter-terrorism financing (CTF), problem gambling harm minimisation, and venue design are voluminous, jurisdiction-specific, and evolving. SkyCity demonstrated in Adelaide that management of these obligations can fail badly at the institutional level.
- **AML/CTF operational risk:** This is not a peripheral risk for casinos — it is the operational core. Managing cash-intensive businesses with international patrons in a way that satisfies AUSTRAC and the NZ Department of Internal Affairs requires specialised compliance infrastructure. This is outside the typical investor's circle of competence.
- **Asset value vs. earnings power:** The Auckland real estate footprint (Sky Tower + casino complex in the CBD) has independent asset value that pure earnings-based frameworks undervalue. Appraising that asset value requires property expertise, not just business analysis.

**Conclusion:** SkyCity sits on the boundary of competence. A simplified Buffett framework understates the asset value; a pure earnings model understates the regulatory risk. Proceed, but widen the margin of safety demanded.

---

### 2. Key Assumptions

The following explicit assumptions underpin this thesis — each must be periodically verified:

1. **Auckland casino licence is secure to 2048.** The Auckland licence is NZ's only full-service casino licence and represents the vast majority of group earnings. Thesis collapses if this is compromised.
2. **Adelaide write-down is complete; no further material penalties.** The surrender of the Adelaide licence and the A$67m AUSTRAC civil penalty (2022) are assumed to be absorbed. No material new fines from NZ DIA or any fresh regulatory action is assumed.
3. **Normalised group earnings recover to NZD 60–80m net profit within 3–5 years.** Current forward NI of ~NZD 40m is assumed to be a trough driven by debt interest cost and post-Adelaide restructuring, not a permanent impairment of the Auckland operating business.
4. **NZD/AUD FX rate remains broadly stable at ~0.90.** A sustained NZD devaluation would reduce AUD-reported income and asset values.
5. **Debt reduction trajectory is maintained.** Total debt NZD 720m is the primary financial risk. Sustained deleveraging from NZICC completion and FCF recovery is assumed; a refinancing crisis at an elevated rate environment would materially impair equity value.

### 3. Business Quality & Moat

**Moat type:** Regulatory/intangible (government-granted exclusivity)
**Moat strength:** Narrow — not None
**Moat trend:** Narrowing (Adelaide revocation demonstrated fragility; NZ scrutiny elevated)
**Morningstar rating:** None (disagreed with in part — see below)

**Auckland Monopoly — The Core Asset**

SkyCity Auckland holds New Zealand's only full-service legal casino licence. There is no mechanism under current NZ law for a competing casino to be established in Auckland. The Sky Tower complex occupies a strategically irreplaceable position in the Auckland CBD. This exclusivity is genuine and long-dated (licence to 2048 on current understanding). In Porter terms, this is pricing power without a competitor threat — the textbook definition of a moat.

Morningstar's "No Moat" rating is understandable in the context of Adelaide — a moat that can be revoked is not permanent by definition. I rate it Narrow rather than None because: (a) the Auckland licence has materially stronger tenure expectations than Adelaide given it is NZ's sole facility and the NZ government co-benefits significantly from the licence; (b) switching costs for patrons in a monopoly market are zero for the regulator and operator but infinite for the customer (there is no alternative venue). The distinction is that the moat is government-granted, not structurally embedded in the economics — and the Adelaide precedent proves it can be destroyed by management behaviour.

**Five-Year Moat Durability Test**

| Factor | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Competitive entry | Extremely low — statutory exclusivity in Auckland market |
| Substitutes | Online gambling platforms are the primary threat; sports betting erodes table game volume at margin |
| Regulatory renewal risk | Moderate — NZ government is active regulator; track record improved post-AUSTRAC but not fully restored |
| Management execution on AML | High risk — two prior regulatory failures (NZ + AU); under new compliance infrastructure |
| Asset replacement value | Very high — Sky Tower + casino complex is effectively irreplaceable |

**Net assessment:** The moat is real but fragile. It earns a Narrow rating specifically because no competitor can build an Auckland casino, but it cannot earn Wide because regulators can revoke it at will and have shown willingness to do so.

### 4. Management Assessment

**Integrity:** Conditional pass — prior management failed; current management is reform-phase.

**The record must not be whitewashed.** AUSTRAC's 2022 civil penalty of A$67m found that SkyCity Entertainment's Australian entity (SkyCity Adelaide) engaged in serious, systematic AML/CTF Act contraventions. The NZ Department of Internal Affairs separately fined SkyCity NZ $4.16m for AML/CFT Act breaches at Auckland. These were not isolated incidents — regulators described a culture where compliance was subordinate to commercial imperatives.

The institutional imperative was in full display: management pursued Adelaide growth, signed the NZICC convention centre development contract, and allowed compliance infrastructure to lag behind business scale. The result was licence revocation in SA and near-crisis in Auckland.

**Current management:** CEO Michael Anderson was installed in the reform era (2022) specifically to rebuild the compliance culture and manage the regulatory relationship. Capital allocation is now focused on debt reduction, not growth. These are the right priorities. But the board that allowed the failures is partly overlapping with the board today, which warrants caution.

**Capital allocation track record:** Poor historically (Adelaide expansion, NZICC cost overruns, excessive leverage). Current track record: no dividend, deleveraging focus, operational rather than expansionary. Score: 4/10 historically; 6/10 under current mandate.

**Owner mentality:** Low. Management holds minimal personal stock. AustralianSuper, the largest disclosed shareholder, recently eased its voting power from above to 8.32% — a signal of institutional ambivalence rather than conviction.

**Auto-veto trigger:** Normally prior AML failures at the institutional level would be a disqualifying management integrity failure. I do NOT apply the auto-veto here because: (a) the responsible individuals have largely departed; (b) the current CEO was hired specifically to remediate; (c) the licence is still intact in NZ. However, this history must be weighted heavily in the margin of safety demanded.

- Reconciled owner-earnings value: A$35.77m × 10 / 1,103.1m = **A$0.32**

### 13. Bridgewater Risk Assessment

**Interest Rate Sensitivity**
SkyCity carries NZD 720m in debt. The RBNZ cut the OCR materially through 2024–2025 (from 5.5% to ~3.5%). This is a direct tailwind — every 50bps reduction in borrowing rates saves ~NZD 3.6m in annual interest, meaningful against A$35.8m normalised earnings. If global rates reverse (re-rate risk), SKC is exposed: interest coverage at EBIT level is ~1.0× currently.

**Sensitivity:** High — debt-heavy balance sheet, thin interest coverage, recovery thesis dependent on continuing rate normalisation.

**Inflation Sensitivity**
Moderate negative. Wage cost inflation (casino floor staff, hotel operations) compresses already-thin operating margins. Revenue has some pass-through (hospitality pricing) but gaming revenue is harder to reprice. Net: inflation is a modest headwind.

**Recession Stress Test**
- Estimated drawdown in a NZ recession: −35 to −50% from current price
- Gaming revenue is discretionary and cyclical. Auckland casino saw revenue fall ~35% during COVID
- At a 30% revenue decline, EBIT falls below interest expense (see Section 5 stress test) → covenant risk
- A severe NZ recession would raise probability of equity dilution to recapitalise

**Liquidity Risk:** Moderate. Cash A$99m vs. NZD debt. Refinancing risk if debt matures into a hostile credit environment. Monitor maturity profile (not disclosed in this data set).

**Leverage Risk:** High. Net debt/EBITDA ~3.8×. Investment-grade casino operators typically target 2.0–2.5×. SKC is at the outer edge of manageable leverage for a monopoly with predictable (if thin) revenue.
