## PART A — BUFFETT VALUE FRAMEWORK

### 1. Circle of Competence

**Inside — with significant caveats.** Casino operations are conceptually simple: a government-granted monopoly (or oligopoly) on table games and electronic gaming machines within a defined geography, monetising discretionary leisure spend. The business model — gaming revenue plus non-gaming (hotels, F&B, conventions) — is transparent. However, the sector sits at the boundary of my circle because: (a) regulatory risk is binary and opaque (licence renewals, tax rates, harm-minimisation mandates); (b) capital intensity is extreme (maintenance capex alone runs ~AUD 100–150m/yr); (c) cyclical sensitivity to tourism and consumer confidence is high; (d) online gaming encroachment introduces a structural threat that is difficult to quantify. I can analyse the financials and competitive position, but I cannot forecast regulatory outcomes or the pace of digital substitution with conviction.

### 2. Key Assumptions (3–5)

The thesis rests on the following explicit assumptions, each flagged for quarterly verification:

1. **FX stability**: NZD/AUD = 0.92 (1 NZD = 0.92 AUD) — used for all NZD→AUD conversions in this report. A 5% move changes AUD-equivalent debt by ~AUD 35m.
2. **Licence continuity**: Auckland, Hamilton, and Queenstown casino licences are renewed on terms materially similar to current (Auckland licence expires 2048; Adelaide 2035). Any adverse variation — higher tax, reduced tables/machines, smoking bans — impairs value immediately.
3. **Capex normalisation**: Maintenance capex settles at AUD 100–150m/yr (vs. AUD 286m in FY24 and AUD 151m in FY25 which included project spend). If growth capex persists, FCF remains negative indefinitely.
4. **No systemic closure events**: No repeat of COVID-mandated shutdowns or equivalent disruptive events (natural disasters, pandemics, regulatory moratoriums).
5. **Debt refinancing at ≤ 6.5%**: Weighted average cost of debt stays near current ~6% (implied by interest expense ~NZD 48m on NZD 803m debt). A 200bp rise adds ~AUD 14m/yr interest burden.

### 3. Business Quality & Moat

| Dimension | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| **Moat type** | None (regulatory barrier only) |
| **Moat strength** | None — Morningstar concurs |
| **Moat trend** | Narrowing |

**Analysis**: SkyCity's "moat" is purely a government-granted licence to operate casinos in specific locations. This is a *regulatory barrier*, not a competitive advantage arising from the business itself. It confers no pricing power (gaming margins are set by statute/market), no switching costs (patrons move freely between venues and online), no network effects, and no intangible brand value that drives excess returns. The regulatory barrier is also *wasting*: Auckland's licence expires 2048, Adelaide's 2035, and both face periodic review. Meanwhile, structural threats are mounting: (i) online gaming (Sportsbet, Bet365, offshore operators) captures discretionary gambling spend with lower friction; (ii) harm-minimisation regulation (mandatory carded play, bet limits, smoking bans) steadily erodes revenue per machine/table; (iii) capital intensity consumes all operating cash flow — the "moat" requires constant reinvestment just to maintain the asset base. Five-year durability is low: the regulatory umbrella may shrink, online substitution accelerates, and the capital treadmill leaves no room for error.
