## PART A — BUFFETT VALUE FRAMEWORK

### 1. Circle of Competence

SkyCity Entertainment Group operates three integrated resort-casinos: Auckland (New Zealand's largest, exclusivity to 2048), Hamilton (NZ, smaller venue), and Adelaide (South Australia, exclusivity to 2085). The business model is elementary — a government-granted monopoly extracts a mathematical edge from gaming machines and table games, while supplementary hotel, F&B, and events revenue monetises the captive foot traffic that the licence manufactures. Revenue is relatively predictable absent regulatory interference; fixed costs are high and operating leverage is substantial.

**Assessment: Boundary of circle.** The casino economics are fully understandable. The irreducible complexity is regulatory: SkyCity is mid-remediation on serious anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorism-financing (AML/CFT) failures in both NZ (where it signed a Deed of Agreement with the New Zealand Department of Internal Affairs) and South Australia (where Adelaide's Casino licence was under independent monitor oversight). I can evaluate the underlying economics with confidence but cannot model regulatory trajectory reliably. Proceed — but not with conviction.

---

### 2. Key Assumptions

The investment thesis depends on five assumptions, each subject to explicit monitoring:

1. **Regulatory remediation completes without licence revocation.** Both the NZ AML programme and Adelaide's probationary conditions resolve on schedule, without additional fines, operational restrictions, or revocation events.
2. **Revenue recovers toward NZD 850–870m.** Revenue declined from NZD 861m (FY2024) to NZD 821m (FY2025), partly reflecting consent-order restrictions. Normalised trading resumes as compliance programmes conclude.
3. **Capex cycle rolls off materially.** The NZD 311m FY2024 and NZD 164m FY2025 capex spends (NZICC fitout, Adelaide expansion) normalise to maintenance levels of NZD 60–80m p.a., converting FCF to positive territory by FY2027.
4. **Balance sheet is managed without a dilutive equity raise.** NZD 803m total debt against NZD 51m cash (FY2025) can be serviced and gradually reduced as FCF improves; no covenant breach or distressed refinancing.
5. **Auckland exclusivity retains commercial value through 2048.** NZ gambling legislation does not materially erode the Auckland exclusivity zone or impose gaming machine number/limit reductions that destroy revenue capacity.

### 3. Business Quality & Moat

**Moat type:** Government-granted intangible asset — exclusive casino licences in Auckland CBD (to 2048) and Adelaide city (to 2085). These are negotiated agreements with national and state governments; no competitor can legally open a rival casino in either location for decades.

**Moat strength: Narrow.** The structural moat is real — exclusivity creates pricing power and eliminates competitive entry risk. However, the moat does not currently operate at full strength:
- Consent orders restrict certain gaming operations and impose compliance costs that a clean-licence operator would not bear
- Online and digital gambling platforms gradually redirect recreational spend, particularly among younger demographics
- The moat protects against conventional competitors, not against regulatory expropriation of economics

**Moat trend: Narrowing (cyclically).** The AML failures have imposed both cost (AML compliance programme, fines, management bandwidth) and constraint (operational restrictions on certain VIP and gaming machine operations), compressing operating margins well below normalised levels. Whether the moat re-widens over three to five years depends on regulatory clearance — a binary outcome rather than a smooth trajectory.

**Five-year moat durability test:** The Auckland licence runs to 2048 and Adelaide to 2085. Both are backed by contractual agreements with sovereign governments — extraordinarily difficult to overturn absent deliberate regulatory non-compliance. On a five-year horizon the structural moat survives. The operating moat (ability to earn economic rents from the exclusivity) recovers as compliance programmes conclude. Risk: an undetected repeat compliance failure would accelerate moat impairment significantly.

**Morningstar moat rating:** Morningstar assigns "No Moat" — consistent with their view that regulatory risk is structural and ongoing, not merely cyclical. I disagree modestly; the licence exclusivity is real and durable, but I accept that the current regulatory environment narrows the economic moat to the point where a "Narrow" rating is the appropriate concession to Morningstar's scepticism.
