## PART A — BUFFETT VALUE FRAMEWORK

---

### 1. Circle of Competence

Inghams Group is Australia and New Zealand's largest integrated poultry producer. The business model can be explained in one sentence: hatch day-old chicks, grow them on owned and contracted farms, process the birds at large abattoirs, then sell branded and private-label chicken and turkey products to supermarkets (Woolworths, Coles), quick-service restaurants (McDonald's, KFC, Hungry Jack's), and the broader foodservice channel.

The economics are equally legible: revenue minus feed costs (corn, soy) minus labour minus processing and cold-chain costs equals a thin margin. There is no hidden complexity, no derivative book, no offshore regulatory patchwork.

**Assessment: Inside circle of competence.** The business is fully understandable. The caveat is that understanding the mechanics of a business is not the same as finding a worthy investment — commodity poultry processing has structural limitations that are apparent to any analyst willing to look.

---

### 2. Key Assumptions (the thesis depends on all five holding simultaneously)

1. **Feed cost normalisation.** Elevated grain prices (2022–2025) that compressed gross margins toward 17% revert toward historical averages (19–21%), restoring the ~5% operating margin Inghams earned at peak.
2. **Volume recovery.** FY25 saw declining poultry volumes (confirmed in news); the FY24 capital programme (AUD 162m capex, nearly 3× FY22 levels) has added processing capacity that will be absorbed as industry oversupply clears.
3. **Debt remains serviceable.** AUD 1.49bn of debt at an estimated 5–6% blended rate consumes roughly AUD 80–90m/year in interest. This is serviceable at current EBIT of ~AUD 123m but leaves very thin cover. RBA rate-cutting cycles and ongoing debt reduction (AUD 1,803m FY22 → AUD 1,490m TTM) must continue.
4. **Customer pricing pass-through.** Inghams can partially pass feed and labour cost inflation to major retail and QSR customers through annual contract renegotiations; without this, margin recovery is impossible.
5. **No structural demand erosion.** Per-capita chicken consumption in Australia remains stable or grows; plant-based alternatives have not materially displaced conventional poultry at scale.

If assumptions 1 and 2 disappoint simultaneously, the investment case is materially impaired.

---

### 3. Business Quality & Moat

**Moat type: None** (Morningstar's independent rating, confirmed)

Inghams possesses several competitive characteristics worth acknowledging before concluding they do not constitute a moat:

- **Scale.** It is the largest ASX-listed integrated poultry processor; scale creates purchasing leverage on feed inputs and lower per-unit processing costs than smaller competitors.
- **Customer relationships.** Long-term supply agreements with QSR majors and supermarket chains create some switching costs — changing primary poultry supplier is disruptive. However, these relationships are based on price-competitiveness first.
- **Vertical integration.** Inghams controls the full chain from hatchery to shelf. This improves quality consistency and biosecurity but is replicable by any well-capitalised entrant.

None of these characteristics prevent the following structural realities:

- **No pricing power.** Chicken is a commodity protein; Woolworths and Coles together hold ~70% of Australian grocery retail and apply relentless private-label pressure. Inghams' branded chicken commands minimal premium.
- **No meaningful switching costs.** McDonald's and KFC multi-source protein globally; Australian QSR operators can and do run tender processes. Inghams' relationships are real but not locked in.
- **Competent private-sector rivals.** JBS Australia (subsidiary of NYSE-listed JBS S.A.), Baiada Poultry, and Cordina Chicken are all large-scale private operators. Inghams' listed status does not confer a competitive edge over them.

**Moat trend: Stable to narrowing.** The FY24 capacity investment may improve long-run processing efficiency. However, the structural forces — retail consolidation, commodity input pricing, free entry for private operators with capital — are intensifying.

**Five-year moat durability test: Fails.** ROIC is approximately 4.9% (NOPAT of ~AUD 86m on AUD 1,745m invested capital), well below any reasonable estimate of WACC. The business has consistently destroyed economic value at the capital level even while generating nominal profits.

### 4. Management Assessment

**Integrity check:** No evidence of misconduct, accounting manipulation, or self-dealing appears in the provided data or news coverage. Integrity test: **Pass.**

**Capital allocation track record:**

The recent history is mixed. On the positive side, management has reduced gross debt from AUD 1,803m (FY22) to AUD 1,490m (TTM), demonstrating discipline on deleveraging while navigating a difficult margin environment. The FY25 dividend cut — painful for income holders but appropriate given a halving of earnings power — was the right call.

On the negative side, FY24 capex of AUD 162m (versus AUD 62m in FY22 and AUD 72m in FY23) represented a major capacity expansion at the trough of the margin cycle. While timing capital programmes at cycle lows can be value-accretive, the execution risk is elevated when interest coverage is already thin. H1 FY26 results (NI AUD 18.1m) suggest the new capacity has not yet generated the volumes needed to service the investment.

**Owner mentality:** Inghams was taken private by TPG Capital in 2012 and re-listed in 2016 with a heavily leveraged balance sheet typical of private equity exits. Current management inherited this structure and has been steadily improving it. The fact that D/E remains at 5.85× — ten years after listing — indicates the progress is slow.

**Institutional imperative warning:** The elevated FY24 capex cycle during an industry downturn warrants ongoing scrutiny. If the expansion was driven by maintenance/compliance obligations, it is defensible. If it was discretionary volume growth chasing, the cycle timing judgment is poor.

**Overall assessment: Adequate.** No integrity concerns; capital allocation is improving but history is blemished by a PE-loaded balance sheet and a cycle-ill-timed expansion. Not a disqualifier, but not a tailwind.

**(b) The critical input error — levered vs pre-interest cash flows:**

The canonical dcf-v2 model requires **post-interest, post-tax (levered) equity cash flows** as the base, because it discounts at the CAPM cost of equity (an equity, not enterprise, discount rate). Owner earnings in this context means what accrues to equity holders after debt service.

The histMedianOE of AUD 244m and histMedianFCF of AUD 280m are vastly larger than net income in those years (AUD 35m, AUD 60m, AUD 102m). The only explanation consistent with the financial history is that these figures represent **pre-interest operating cash flows** — i.e., they include the cash flow that services Inghams' ~AUD 82m/year interest bill and therefore do not represent equity owner earnings. Using them as the base for a levered equity DCF double-counts operating cash generation by ignoring the debt-service claim.

This is the sole driver of the inflated normalised base (AUD 159.6m vs the appropriate ~AUD 70m) and therefore of the overstated code fair value.

**(c) Adjusted normalised owner earnings base:**

The only reliable levered-equity-cashflow candidates are:
- Forward NI: AUD 75m (analyst consensus; requires H2 FY26 of ~AUD 56m vs H1's AUD 18.1m — a stretch)
