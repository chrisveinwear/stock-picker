## PART A — BUFFETT VALUE FRAMEWORK

---

### 1. Circle of Competence

**Inside — with caveats.**

Inghams Group is Australia's and New Zealand's largest integrated poultry producer. The business model is genuinely simple: breed chickens → hatch → contract-grow on third-party farms → slaughter and process at company-owned plants → sell dressed and further-processed product to quick-service restaurants (KFC, McDonald's), grocery retailers (Coles, Woolworths), and foodservice distributors.

Revenue (~AUD 3.15bn), cost structure (grain ~55–60% of COGS, energy, contract-grower payments, labour), and customer relationships are all understandable. The key analytical tasks — tracking grain futures, QSR contract renewal cycles, AASB 16 lease liability mechanics, and the two-player duopoly with Baiada/Steggles — are within competence.

The one complexity requiring discipline: the AUD 1.49bn "total debt" figure includes AASB 16 lease liabilities (likely AUD 600–750m) alongside financial bank debt (~AUD 700–800m). These two components behave differently in a stress scenario, and conflating them overstates the insolvency risk while understating the real cash cost of the operating lease structure.

**Conclusion:** Proceed with full analysis.

---

### 2. Key Assumptions (3–5)

The investment thesis rests on five assumptions. All five must be monitored quarterly and flagged if evidence turns contrary.

1. **Earnings trough and recovery:** The AUD 0.20 forward EPS represents a cyclical trough, not a structural floor. The model assumes earnings normalise to AUD 0.25–0.30 by FY27–FY28 as grain costs moderate (La Niña-driven crop improvement in eastern Australia), operational cost savings (management's AUD 40–50m annualised target) flow through, and QSR volumes recover.

2. **Debt is serviceable, not existential:** EBITDA of AUD 170m+ covers the estimated AUD 40–45m annual cash interest on financial debt (AASB 16 interest component of ~AUD 25–30m is largely non-cash P&L line). Bank facilities are assumed to be refinanced without material covenant breach in base case.

3. **Volume stability, not growth:** The thesis does not require Inghams to gain market share. It requires volume to stabilise at or above FY26 levels (~95% of prior-peak tonnage) as management addresses supply chain disruption highlighted in the FY26 H1 guidance cut.

4. **New management executes cost savings:** The new CEO and senior team (assuming FY26 results on 21 August establish the strategy) execute the known cost efficiency programme without further restructuring charges that impair free cash flow.

5. **No catastrophic supply-side disruption:** No Highly Pathogenic Avian Influenza (HPAI) outbreak in Australia requiring mass culls; no major drought impairing grain supply; no food-safety incident requiring facility closure.

**If assumptions 1 and 2 fail simultaneously, equity value under the DCF framework approaches zero** (see Section 6). This is the primary risk.

### 3. Business Quality & Moat

**Moat: None | Trend: Stable at None | Five-year durability: None expected**

Morningstar's independent assessment confirms no economic moat, and the financial data corroborates this decisively.

**Moat analysis by type:**

| Moat type | Assessment |
|-----------|-----------|
| Intangible assets (brand) | Weak — "Inghams" has modest retail recognition but QSR customers buy on price/reliability, not brand. Supermarkets sell under private label. |
| Cost advantage | Partial scale effect — Inghams is the largest AU/NZ integrated processor, giving modest procurement leverage on feed ingredients. This advantage is not durable: Baiada is similarly scaled. |
| Switching costs | Negligible — QSR chains (KFC, McDonald's) and supermarkets routinely dual-source and can switch primary suppliers on 3–6 month contract cycles. |
| Network effects | None. |
| Efficient scale | Arguable in specific regions — but the national market supports two equally-scaled competitors, eliminating the efficient scale advantage. |

**Structural evidence the moat is absent:**

- ROIC (estimated 5.1%) is materially below WACC (8.0%) — the business destroys value on incremental invested capital.
- Net margin 1.8% — commodity-processor-level margins with no pricing power.
- Revenue growth CAGR over 5 years approximately 0–1% — no structural volume growth, only price pass-through.
- Two consecutive guidance cuts in FY25–26, demonstrating inability to pass input cost inflation to customers at speed.

**Five-year moat durability test:** No pathway to moat development is visible. The business would need either (a) a proprietary product that commands a price premium, (b) vertical integration into retail that creates switching costs, or (c) a structural reduction of Baiada's competitive capacity. None of these is probable over five years.

### 4. Management Assessment

**Integrity:** No fraud, governance, or related-party concerns flagged. Institutional ownership at 54% (October 2025) suggests active oversight. Auto-veto criteria not triggered.

**Capital allocation track record:** Mixed to poor.

The debt legacy is the cardinal sin. Inghams was taken private by TPG Capital in 2012 in a leveraged buyout and relisted in 2016 with the LBO debt still substantially intact. Management since listing has reduced leverage slowly but the FY25–26 earnings downturn has halted deleveraging. The AUD 1.42bn net debt position against AUD 170m EBITDA (net debt/EBITDA ~8.4x) is not consistent with conservative capital allocation. Dividend cuts — the right call operationally — signal that management has been caught short by the earnings cycle.

**Owner mentality:** Evidence is mixed. The new CEO (as of FY26) has not yet established a public capital allocation philosophy. The interim CFO presenting the 21 August FY26 results adds uncertainty. The management team cannot be scored reliably until the FY26 strategy update.

**Institutional imperative warning:** The history of prioritising volume over margin (maintaining throughput to cover fixed costs even as input costs spike) is a classic institutional imperative failure — maintaining the appearance of revenue growth at the expense of profitability.

**Conclusion:** Management passes the integrity test but not the Buffett quality bar. The business requires a disciplined capital allocator committed to debt reduction and margin recovery above volume growth. Whether the new CEO will be that person is the key qualitative unknown for the FY26 results day.

### 5. Financial Snapshot

**All figures AUD, FY25 trailing unless noted. Shares outstanding: 371.7m.**

**Key ratios vs. CLAUDE.md screening thresholds:**

| Metric | ING value | Threshold | Signal |
|--------|-----------|-----------|--------|

| P/E forward | 10.4× | < 15× | Pass |
| P/B | 3.06× | < 2.5× | Borderline fail |
| ROE | 22.8% | > 15% | Pass (but leverage-inflated) |
| Net margin | 1.8% | > 10% | Hard fail |
| D/E | 585% (5.85×) | < 100% | Hard fail |
| Interest coverage (est.) | ~2.9× | > 5× | Hard fail |
| FCF positive | Yes (AUD 120m) | Yes | Pass |

**Note on ROE:** The 22.8% ROE is a product of financial leverage, not business quality. With book equity of only AUD 255m against AUD 1,490m total debt, even modest earnings generate a high return on a thin equity base. The ROIC — which measures returns on all invested capital and is leverage-neutral — tells the true story:

```
NOPAT = EBIT × (1 - tax) = (3,150m × 3.9%) × 0.7 = AUD 86m
Invested Capital = Book equity + Net debt = 255m + 1,420m = AUD 1,675m
ROIC = 86m / 1,675m = 5.1%
```

**ROIC of 5.1% against WACC of 8.0% = value destruction of 2.9% per year on invested capital.** This is the financial signature of a business with no economic moat.

**Owner earnings (trailing):**
Net income AUD 56.7m + D&A less maintenance capex ≈ AUD 70–80m. The code model uses forward NI of AUD 75.4m (AUD 0.20 EPS forward × 371.7m shares) as the normalised owner earnings base — this aligns with the trailing FCF of AUD 120m after adjusting for the ~AUD 40–45m of AASB 16 lease principal payments not captured in the FCF figure.

**Interest coverage calculation:**

```
EBIT = Revenue × Operating margin = 3,150m × 3.9% = AUD 122.9m
EBT  = Net income / (1 - 0.30) = 56.7m / 0.70 = AUD 81.0m
Implied interest = EBIT - EBT = AUD 41.9m
Coverage = EBIT / Interest = 122.9m / 41.9m = 2.93×
```

This includes AASB 16 interest on lease liabilities (~AUD 25–30m). On financial bank debt alone, coverage improves to approximately 4.0–5.0×. However, the total reported position (2.93×) remains well below the CLAUDE.md 5× threshold.

**Debt stress test (revenue −30%):**
If revenue fell to AUD 2.2bn and EBITDA declined proportionally to ~AUD 100m, interest of AUD 42m would still be covered, but the net debt/EBITDA ratio would reach ~14×. Bank covenant breach (typically set at net debt/EBITDA 3.5–4.5× on financial debt) would depend on the covenant structure and whether AASB 16 leases are included in the definition. The margin for error is thin.

| **WACC (CAPM raw)** | **2.9% (floored to 8.0%)** | **Per discount-rate policy** |

The raw CAPM WACC of 2.9% is floored at 8.0% per model policy. This is appropriate: a reported beta of 0.19 for a highly-leveraged commodity processor with AUD 1.49bn debt is implausibly low, likely reflecting the thinly-traded nature of the stock and the defensive perception of food staples rather than the true fundamental risk. The 8.0% WACC is the operative rate.

#### Step 3 — Stage-1 DCF (10-year)

Growing at 4.0% per year from AUD 75.36m base, discounted at 8.0%:

Present value of 10-year owner earnings stream:

```
PV = 75.36m × Σ(1.04/1.08)^n for n=1..10
   = 75.36m × 8.197
   ≈ AUD 618m
```

#### Step 4 — Terminal value (average of two methods)

**Perpetuity-growth method (g = 2.5%):**

```
OE_year11 = 75.36m × 1.04^10 × 1.025 = 75.36 × 1.4802 × 1.025 = AUD 114.3m
TV (perp) = 114.3m / (0.08 – 0.025) = 114.3m / 0.055 = AUD 2,078m
TV discounted = 2,078m / 1.08^10 = 2,078m / 2.159 = AUD 963m
```

**Exit-multiple method (12× EBIT):**

```
EBIT (current) = 3,150m × 3.9% = AUD 122.9m
Exit EV = 122.9m × 12 = AUD 1,475m
TV discounted = 1,475m / 1.08^10 = 1,475m / 2.159 = AUD 683m
```

Average terminal value: (963 + 683) / 2 = **AUD 823m**

#### Step 5 — Equity value per share

```
Enterprise value  = Stage-1 PV + TV = 618m + 823m = AUD 1,441m
Net debt          = Total debt – Cash = 1,490m – 70m = AUD 1,420m
Equity value      = AUD 1,441m – AUD 1,420m = AUD 21m
Per share         = AUD 21m / 371.7m = AUD 0.06/share
