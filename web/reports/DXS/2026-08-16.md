## PART A — BUFFETT VALUE FRAMEWORK

### 1. Circle of Competence

**Boundary — inside with caveats.** The core business is simple to explain: Dexus owns and manages commercial real estate (predominantly office, plus industrial, retail, healthcare and infrastructure-adjacent assets) and collects rent, topped up by a funds-management business that charges fees on third-party capital. That's a landlord model any investor can understand. The complexity creeps in at the edges: a stapled-security structure, active portfolio recycling (divestments in progress per recent news), minority stakes in unlisted infrastructure vehicles (including an airport interest currently the subject of a court dispute that froze trading in late May 2026), and earnings that swing wildly on non-cash property revaluations (net income ranged from +AUD 1,616m to -AUD 1,579m across FY22–FY25 on the same underlying rent roll). This is within the circle, but it demands looking through statutory earnings to FFO/owner earnings, and treating the funds-management and infrastructure-stake components as satellite businesses layered on a straightforward real estate core.

### 3. Business Quality & Moat

Morningstar assigns Dexus **no economic moat**, and that view is hard to argue with using first principles. Office space is largely a substitutable good — tenants can and do relocate at lease expiry, switching costs are low relative to other industries, and the asset class is prone to oversupply in any given submarket cycle. Dexus's prime CBD assets (Sydney, Melbourne) carry some locational scarcity value and its funds-management platform has modest scale advantages, but these are narrow, not durable, differentiators. **Moat strength: none-to-narrow. Trend: narrowing** — structural hybrid-work headwinds are a genuine, multi-year demand drag on office, not a cyclical blip. Five-year durability test: the business will still exist and collect rent in five years, but pricing power over tenants is weak and the asset base is being actively reshaped (divestments) rather than compounding — this is capital recycling to survive a hostile cycle, not moat-widening reinvestment.
