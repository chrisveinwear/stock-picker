## PART A — BUFFETT VALUE FRAMEWORK

---

### 1. Circle of Competence

**Assessment: At the boundary — proceed with disciplined conservatism.**

Commercial real estate is conceptually legible: own well-located buildings, lease them to high-quality tenants, collect rents indexed to CPI, and compound the asset base over time. Dexus's model — owning premium office towers and industrial parks across Sydney, Melbourne and Brisbane, while running a third-party funds management platform of approximately AUD 36bn in real estate assets — can be explained to a twelve-year-old.

Two factors push this to the boundary rather than comfortably inside the circle:

First, the structural impact of hybrid and remote work on Australian CBD office demand is genuinely unresolved. How much office space will the big four banks, major law firms, and government agencies require in 2031? There is no confident answer. Familiarity with real estate does not equal clarity on this question. An investor who mistakes one for the other risks systematically underestimating vacancy risk.

Second, the funds management component (fee income from managing AUD 36bn of third-party capital) is a capital-light business with different earnings economics and multiple characteristics than direct property ownership. Valuing it requires a separate analytical framework.

**Conclusion:** Inside the circle for direct property economics and income valuation; at the boundary for office structural demand and funds management growth. Proceed, but demand a wider margin of safety than usual.

---

### 2. Key Assumptions (3–5)

The investment thesis depends on the following assumptions. Each should be verified quarterly.

**Assumption 1: Sydney CBD office vacancy stabilises and begins recovering.** Current vacancy is approximately 10–12%. The thesis requires vacancy to peak at or below 13% and begin declining by FY2028 as new supply slows and occupiers commit to long-term leases. If vacancy rises above 15%, the thesis weakens materially and the distribution may be at risk.

**Assumption 2: The RBA delivers 1–2 rate cuts by mid-2027.** Markets are pricing this as at 1 July 2026. Lower rates reduce Dexus's refinancing costs on AUD 4.68bn of gross debt and expand property valuations through cap rate compression. Each 50bps fall in the 10-year Australian government bond yield adds approximately AUD 0.70–1.00 to NTA per security.

**Assumption 3: The annual distribution is maintained near 37 cents per security.** This assumes FFO per security remains at approximately AUD 0.60–0.63 and the payout ratio holds at 56–60%. A payout ratio this conservative (peers typically pay 75–90% of FFO) provides a meaningful buffer. If FFO falls below AUD 0.52/security, a distribution cut becomes probable.

**Assumption 4: Funds management AUM holds at approximately AUD 30–36bn.** The infrastructure fund mandate being contested by major banks (per recent news) represents a growth opportunity. Loss of a major fund mandate would reduce recurring fee income and impair the capital-light earnings story.

**Assumption 5: Gearing remains below 35% (net debt / total assets).** Current net gearing is estimated at approximately 32%. A 15–20% decline in office portfolio valuations could push this above covenant levels. Forced asset sales at depressed prices would be value-destructive.

### 3. Business Quality & Moat

**Moat type:** Location advantage and scale in Australian prime office — assessed as Narrow, narrowing.

**Moat strength:** Narrow (with a narrowing trend in the office segment; industrial is more durable).

Dexus's deepest competitive advantage is geography. It owns some of the finest commercial addresses in Australia — buildings that cannot be replicated at any price in their specific locations. Large corporations, law firms, and government agencies pay premium rents to be in premium buildings within walking distance of courts, clients, and transport hubs. Switching costs are real: a fit-out write-off, relocation disruption, staff attrition from changed commutes. These factors protect near-term rental income from prime assets.

The structural limits of this narrow moat are now visible and worsening:

- **Pricing power is being eroded by tenant optionality.** With hybrid work embedding permanently, many tenants are occupying 20–30% less space per employee at renewal. Even if Dexus retains the tenant, the leased area and rental income fall.
- **No switching cost moat for smaller tenants.** Only a handful of tenants (big four banks, global law firms) have true switching reluctance. The mid-market tenant base is mobile.
- **Industrial/logistics assets** held within the Dexus Industrial REIT and directly on balance sheet have better moat characteristics — proximity to major ports and distribution nodes with high barriers to land acquisition — but represent a minority of the overall portfolio.
- **Morningstar independently rates the moat as None**, reflecting the judgment that office property investment returns are not structurally above the cost of capital. I rate it Narrow rather than None, reflecting genuine location advantages in the prime tier, but the gap between our assessments is not material for investment purposes.

**Five-year moat durability test:** The prime CBD buildings remain relevant in 2031. But Dexus's ability to extract above-cost-of-capital returns across the full portfolio — particularly from secondary office space and assets with near-term lease expiries — is genuinely uncertain. The moat is narrowing, not widening.

### 4. Management Assessment

**Integrity:** No concerns. Dexus's corporate governance, disclosure practices, and regulatory compliance record are appropriate for a major ASX-listed REIT. Nothing in the public record suggests integrity issues.

**Capital allocation track record:**

The February 2026 buyback announcement — committing up to approximately AUD 200m to repurchase units at prices near the 52-week low (AUD 5.30–5.40) against an NTA of approximately AUD 8.97 — is a capital-allocation-positive signal. Buying back equity at a 40% discount to book value benefits remaining unitholders and is exactly the kind of return-focused decision Buffett would approve of.

The strategic pivot toward funds management is sensible: capital-light fee income reduces the balance sheet intensity of the business, provides AUM-linked recurring earnings, and ultimately deserves a higher earnings multiple. Dexus has grown third-party AUM from approximately AUD 20bn to AUD 36bn over five years, demonstrating the strategy is working.

The concern is leverage. Running gearing at the upper end of the REIT peer group leaves limited buffer for property valuation declines. Prior management decisions to acquire aggressively during the low-rate cycle now burden the balance sheet.

**Owner mentality:** Moderate. Dexus is a stapled trust structure managed by professional executives rather than founder-owners. Compensation metrics include total securityholder return and FFO per security, which are appropriate alignment tools. The buyback is the clearest recent expression of an owner mentality.

**Institutional imperative warning:** Monitor for rent-free period incentives and capital contribution deals to fill vacancies. Aggressive incentivisation can inflate reported occupancy and face rental rates while destroying economic returns on a discounted basis.
