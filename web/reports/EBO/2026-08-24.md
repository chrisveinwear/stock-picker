### 3. Business Quality & Moat
**Moat Type:** Scale / Cost Advantage (Narrow)  
**Strength:** Narrow (Morningstar: Narrow; my assessment: Narrow)  
**Trend:** Stable / Slightly Narrowing

**Five-Year Moat Durability Test:**
- **Switching Costs:** Low for suppliers (manufacturers can redirect volume to Sigma, API, or direct-to-pharmacy); moderate for pharmacies (EBOS handles ~40% of Australian community pharmacy supply, but alternatives exist). No proprietary tech lock-in.
- **Network Effects:** None. This is a linear logistics chain, not a platform.
- **Intangible Assets:** Exclusive distribution agreements (e.g., TerryWhite Chemmart, Chemist Warehouse supply deals) provide revenue visibility but are contractually finite (typically 3–5 years) and re-tendered.
- **Cost Advantage:** Genuine but thin. EBOS's ~130+ sites and ~4,500 vehicles across ANZ give density advantages — lower cost-per-delivery than sub-scale rivals. However, gross margin of 12.9% and operating margin of 2.9% show most scale savings are passed through to customers (pharmacies/hospitals) via competitive tension.
- **Regulatory Moat:** PBS (Pharmaceutical Benefits Scheme) and NZ equivalents create a stable demand backdrop, but also cap pricing power. The 2023–2027 Community Pharmacy Agreement locks in dispensing fees; EBOS is a price-taker on the wholesale margin.

**Verdict:** A **narrow moat** built on logistics density and incumbent relationships. It protects ~AUD 300–350m of normalised owner earnings but offers no pricing power to compound capital at high rates. The moat is stable but not widening — scale advantages are largely arbitraged away in contract renewals.

#### Valuation Reconciliation
The code model's fair value (AUD 20.09) is close to my midpoint (AUD 19.25) — **divergence < 5%**. However, three warnings require explicit handling:

1. **Beta Clamping (0.15 → 0.60):** The raw beta of 0.15 is implausibly low for a leveraged distributor (D/E 0.70, net debt/EBITDA 4.9x). Clamping to 0.60 raises cost of equity from 7.6% to 8.0% — appropriate. *No adjustment needed.*

2. **Cost of Equity Floor (8%):** The 8% floor exceeds the raw CAPM output. For a low-beta, regulated-adjacent business, 8% is a reasonable *minimum* hurdle (Buffett's "risk-free + 4%"). *No adjustment needed.*

3. **Stage-1 Growth (12% → 4%):** The code model derives 12% from analyst forward EPS (1.32) vs. trailing (1.11) — a 19% jump. **This is the critical error.** Analyst forward EPS assumes LifeHealthcare synergies and margin recovery that have not materialised in 4 years. FY26 net income (274m) is barely above FY23 (276m) despite 23% revenue growth. **Realistic stage-1 growth is 3–5%** (volume + inflation + tuck-ins), not 12%. Using 12% inflates the DCF to AUD 36.12 (code model's DCF output). My DCF uses 4% → AUD 19.80.

4. **IV Range Width (18.14–45.03):** The 148% spread reflects the growth assumption sensitivity. At 4% stage-1 growth, the range narrows to ~17.5–22.0. *Adjusted.*

**Residual Gap to Morningstar (AUD 29.66):** Morningstar's fair value implies ~AUD 450m owner earnings at 12.5x, or a 15x multiple on AUD 300m. This assumes **widening moat and margin expansion to 3%+ net** — not supported by 4-year trend (margins declining). Morningstar's "Narrow" moat rating is consistent with mine; the valuation gap stems from **higher terminal growth (4%+ vs. my 2.5%) and lower discount rate (6.5–7% vs. 8%)**. I view Morningstar's assumptions as optimistic for a mature distributor.

**Residual Gap to Analyst Consensus (AUD 22.73):** Analysts embed FY27 EPS ~1.50 (14% growth) and 15x P/E. Same optimism on margin recovery. **My view: prove it first.**
