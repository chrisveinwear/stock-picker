## PART A — BUFFETT VALUE FRAMEWORK

### 1. Circle of Competence

**Inside.** EBOS Group is Australasia's largest pharmaceutical and healthcare products distributor — essentially a high-volume logistics business with regulatory credentials. The model is straightforward: EBOS buys prescription medicines, medical devices, and animal care products from manufacturers and supplies them to pharmacies, hospitals, veterinary clinics, and aged care facilities across Australia and New Zealand. Revenue is earned on a margin-on-goods-sold basis plus logistics service fees.

The key value drivers — customer concentration, distribution network scale, PBS pricing mechanics, regulatory licence advantages, and cold-chain infrastructure — are all observable and analysable. The business is not complex to understand; assessing the *durability* of its moat after the CWG/Sigma structural disruption is the primary analytical challenge, but that challenge is tractable. EBOS also operates an Animal Care segment (veterinary distribution, pet nutrition) which provides meaningful diversification and typically carries higher margins than the pharmaceutical division.

**Verdict: Inside the circle of competence.** Continue analysis.

### 2. Key Assumptions (5)

1. **The CWG/Sigma customer transition is substantially complete.** The FY2025 revenue decline of 8.4% (AUD 14,411m → AUD 13,209m) reflects Chemist Warehouse pharmaceutical volumes migrating to Sigma Healthcare following the 2024 merger. H1 FY2026 revenue of AUD 6.77bn (+13% YoY) indicates EBOS has stabilised and is growing from a new base. The thesis assumes no further customer defection of comparable scale.

2. **Net margins recover toward 2.0–2.2% by FY2027.** FY2025 net margin compressed to 1.76% (from 2.06% in FY2024) due to operating deleverage on lost volumes. Revenue recovery should partially restore fixed-cost leverage. This is the central earnings thesis driver and requires quarterly monitoring.

3. **PBS pharmaceutical volume grows 3–5% pa.** An ageing Australian and New Zealand population structurally supports demand for dispensed medicines. Government PBS expenditure has grown consistently for two decades. This assumption is robust over a 5–10 year horizon.

4. **Dividend maintained at approximately AUD 0.92/share.** FCF payout is approximately 47% of FY2025 FCF (AUD 396m), providing real coverage for the AUD ~188m annual dividend bill. Even in a stress year, the dividend is affordable.

5. **No structural disintermediation over the investment horizon.** Regulatory requirements for controlled-substance handling, cold-chain certification, and PBS distribution approvals protect the wholesaler's role. Direct manufacturer-to-pharmacy distribution at scale remains logistically and regulatorily impractical over five to ten years.

### 3. Business Quality & Moat

**Moat type:** Cost/scale + switching costs | **Strength:** Narrow | **Trend:** Stable (tested but intact)

EBOS's competitive position rests on three interlocking advantages. First, *scale economics*: at AUD 13.2bn revenue, EBOS is the largest pharmaceutical distributor in Australasia, giving it negotiating leverage with manufacturers, cost amortisation across extensive infrastructure (cold chain, controlled-substance storage, national delivery network), and the ability to invest in regulatory compliance that sub-scale competitors cannot afford. Second, *switching costs*: pharmacies and hospital groups build procurement systems, formulary management, and cold-chain logistics around a single primary distributor. The operational and regulatory cost of switching is non-trivial. Third, *regulatory incumbency*: PBS distribution approvals, controlled-substance licences, and cold-chain certifications create meaningful barriers; new entrants require years of operational history to obtain them.

The CWG/Sigma merger was the most significant test of this moat in EBOS's listed history. When Sigma became the internal distributor for the Chemist Warehouse Group's ~4,900 pharmacies, EBOS lost a significant volume of pharmaceutical wholesale business. This event confirms that the moat is **narrow** — it does not prevent a vertically integrated competitor from withdrawing its custom — but it did not destroy it. EBOS retains dominant share of independent pharmacy, hospital, aged care, and veterinary markets, and the recovery in H1 FY2026 (+13% revenue) demonstrates that the remaining customer base continues to grow.

**Moat durability (5-year test):** Physical distribution infrastructure, regulatory licences, and a diversified remaining customer base provide a durable if modest competitive position. The Animal Care segment (structurally separate from pharmaceutical distribution, less susceptible to the same disruption risks) adds resilience. No new structural threat comparable to the CWG/Sigma merger is currently visible. **Narrow moat, stable trend.**

**Gross margin of 13.1%** is modest and reflects competitive pricing in commodity pharmaceutical distribution. **Net margin of 1.8%** leaves very little buffer — this is structurally characteristic of the distribution business model, not operational failure, but it means the margin of error on any assumption is low.

### 4. Management Assessment

**Integrity:** No disqualifying flags. EBOS's H1 FY2026 results (February 2026) were presented transparently, including clear disclosure of the revenue and EBITDA trajectory through the CWG/Sigma transition. No related-party transactions, earnings manipulation, unusual options activity, or governance concerns appear in available disclosures. The dividend was maintained through the earnings reset, which is a statement of management confidence rather than an integrity concern.

**Capital allocation track record:** Broadly adequate. Capex was reduced from AUD 107m (FY2023) to AUD 54m (FY2025) in response to the revenue headwind, demonstrating cost discipline. This drove FCF of AUD 396m in FY2025 — 171% of net income — which is strong cash conversion. Total debt fluctuated (AUD 1,827m → AUD 1,396m → AUD 1,795m → AUD 1,746m over FY2022–FY2025), reflecting acquisition activity and working capital movements; the FY2023 debt reduction was encouraging. M&A history includes defensible bolt-on acquisitions in animal care and homecare rather than empire-building at inflated multiples.

**Owner mentality:** Management has maintained the distribution network and supply quality through a disruptive transition rather than cutting capability. The dividend hold signals long-term orientation. There are no red flags of the type Buffett associates with the "institutional imperative" — chasing acquisitions to deploy capital, or prioritising executive compensation over shareholder returns.

**Assessment:** Adequate management of a complex logistics franchise. Not exceptional capital allocators, but honest stewards of a business with structurally thin margins. The capex discipline and dividend maintenance in a difficult year earns credit.
