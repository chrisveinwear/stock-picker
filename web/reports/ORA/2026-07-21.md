## PART A — BUFFETT VALUE FRAMEWORK

### 1. Circle of Competence

Orora is a packaging manufacturer: cans, glass bottles, cartons, closures and related packaging solutions sold mainly to beverage and consumer-staples producers, plus a global glass-bottle business (Saverglass, acquired 2023) serving premium wine, spirits and beauty customers. The core business model is easy to explain in one sentence — Orora makes the containers other consumer-staples companies fill and sell, typically under multi-year supply arrangements with co-located or dedicated plants. That places it just inside the circle of competence, adjacent to the "consumer staples" sectors named in my mandate (WOW, COL, MTS), since Orora's own customers are staples businesses.

The one complication: FY25's financial history (revenue collapsing from AUD 4,698m to AUD 2,090m, net income spiking to AUD 973m) reflects a large portfolio restructuring — consistent with Orora's divestment of its North American Packaging Solutions business — layered on top of the 2023 Saverglass acquisition. Understanding the *current*, smaller, more glass-weighted Orora requires more care than reading a steady-state industrial. Verdict: **inside, but at the boundary** — proceed, with extra weight on getting the earnings base right (see Section 6).

### 2. Key Assumptions (3–5)

1. Continuing-operations revenue (≈AUD 2.09bn FY25 base) grows organically at roughly 5–7% p.a. over the next decade — below the raw code model's 12% stage-1 assumption — reflecting the Saverglass guidance reset flagged in April 2026 news.
2. Operating margin expands gradually from the current 11.6% toward ~12.5% as Saverglass integration synergies and cost discipline offset energy/glass input-cost inflation.
3. The post-divestment balance sheet (D/E ≈0.29x) stays deleveraged, sustaining the ~7.0% dividend yield without an equity raise.
4. No further major portfolio disruption (acquisition or divestment) scrambles the earnings base again over the forecast horizon; the Middle East-linked disruption to Saverglass resolves without permanent damage to European export markets or energy costs.
5. The market gradually re-rates Orora's lower-risk, deleveraged profile, consistent with the model's beta floor (raw beta 0.39 clamped to 0.60).

### 3. Business Quality & Moat

**Moat type:** Scale (large, efficient can and bottle plants) plus modest switching costs (long-term supply contracts, co-located facilities that are expensive for a customer to re-source away from).
**Strength:** Narrow — consistent with the "narrow" quality tier used in the valuation model (10x owner-earnings multiple, 10x terminal exit multiple). Packaging is a competitive, capital-intensive industry with several credible global players (Section 11); no single competitor is locked out.
**Trend:** Currently **narrowing to stable-under-pressure**. The April 2026 Saverglass guidance cut and buyback pause are near-term negatives, but they reflect an external shock (Middle East disruption) rather than a structural loss of competitive position.

**Five-year moat durability test:** Packaging is a necessary, repeat-purchase input for beverage and consumer-goods manufacturers, which supports durability — customers rarely switch packaging suppliers opportunistically given qualification costs and co-location. The main durability risks are customer concentration (a small number of large beverage/spirits accounts), energy-cost inflation specific to glass manufacturing, and further geopolitical disruption to Saverglass's export-heavy footprint. On balance, a real but narrow moat — durable, not widening.

#### Valuation Reconciliation

My own IV midpoint (AUD 1.80, see below) diverges from the code fair value (AUD 2.31) by about −22%, which exceeds the ~20% threshold, so this reconciliation is required and is fully transparent below.

**(a) Input error check.** I checked every authoritative input against the supplied data: price AUD 1.43, shares 1,225.3m, market cap AUD 1.75bn all tie out (1.43 × 1,225.3m = AUD 1.75bn ✓). No input error was found — the gap is assumption-driven, not a data error.

**(b) Which assumption drives the gap.** The raw DCF assumes **12% stage-1 owner-earnings growth** fading to a 2.5% terminal rate. That assumption predates or ignores the April 2026 Saverglass guidance cut and buyback pause reported in the news feed for this report — a concrete, recent downward revision to near-term growth expectations from management itself. It is also sharply at odds with two of the model's own triangulation signals: the owner-earnings-multiple method (AUD 1.08, near the current price) and the reverse-DCF (which shows the market itself is pricing in *negative* 2% growth). A 12% forward growth assumption sitting against a market implying −2% is the single largest driver of the AUD 2.56 vs. AUD 1.43 gap — and, by extension, of the code fair value's 62% premium to the current price. The model's own warnings corroborate this: IV swings 41% across the sensitivity grid, explicitly flagged as "low confidence — widen margin of safety."

**(c) Adjusted assumption and rationale.** I replace the 12% stage-1 growth assumption with **~6%**, roughly half the code default, reflecting (i) the Saverglass guidance reset, (ii) the FY25 revenue base being a one-off structural step-down (divestment, not organic decline) that makes near-term comparisons unreliable, and (iii) a deliberately conservative lean toward the owner-earnings-multiple and reverse-DCF signals rather than the unmodified DCF. All other assumptions (8.0% cost of equity, 2.5% terminal growth, narrow-tier 10x exit multiple) are retained as supplied.

**(d) Reconciled IV.** Re-running an illustrative 5-year build at 6% growth (Section 10) produces a base-case per-share value of ~AUD 1.85, with a sensitivity range of roughly AUD 1.65–2.14 at cost of equity ±1%. Blending this with the Graham number (AUD 2.31) and the owner-earnings-multiple floor (AUD 1.08) — weighting the DCF-family methods more heavily but pulling down for the multiple/reverse-DCF signal — I set a reconciled range of **AUD 1.55–2.05, midpoint AUD 1.80**. That midpoint sits within 1.1% of the analyst consensus target (AUD 1.78) — a strong external cross-check — and about 26% above the current price, comfortably inside the reconciliation guard's 40% tolerance.

**(e) Residual gap and model warnings.** A residual gap remains between my reconciled midpoint (AUD 1.80) and the unmodified code fair value (AUD 2.31); I attribute essentially all of it to the stage-1 growth assumption discussed above. The model's own flags — beta clamped from 0.39 to 0.60, cost of equity floored to 8.0%, and the 41% sensitivity-grid swing — all argue for treating any single-point fair value here with caution and leaning on a wider margin of safety, which is reflected in the range I am using.
