## PART A — BUFFETT VALUE FRAMEWORK

### 1. Circle of Competence

**Inside.** Packaging is a tangible, asset-intensive business serving everyday consumer staples (beverages, food, household). The value chain — raw materials → conversion → distribution — is understandable. Orora operates in two clear segments: **Fibre** (corrugated boxes, paper) and **Glass** (bottles for wine, beer, spirits). Both are oligopolistic in Australia/New Zealand with high transport costs creating natural geographic moats. The recent demerger of the North American business (Saverglass stake sale, glass plant closures) simplifies the story to an ANZ-focused leader. This sits comfortably within circle of competence.

### 2. Key Assumptions (3–5)

The thesis rests on these explicit, verifiable assumptions:

1. **Earnings normalisation:** FY26's AUD -617m net loss includes a AUD 616.6m glass impairment (non-cash). Forward EPS of AUD 0.12 (consensus) implies ~AUD 147m normalised net income — a plausible run-rate for the post-demerger ANZ business.
2. **Cyclical recovery in Fibre:** Corrugated volumes correlate with industrial production and retail sales. Australian GDP growth of 1.5–2.0% supports low-single-digit volume growth; price/cost pass-through maintains margins.
3. **Glass segment stabilisation:** After FY26 impairment (capacity write-down), the remaining glass assets run at higher utilisation. No further major impairments; EBITDA margin converges to 15–18% (vs. 21.5% group gross margin).
4. **Capital allocation discipline:** Management maintains the on-market buyback (AUD 100m+ authorised) and 6.5% dividend yield, but prioritises debt reduction (net debt AUD 0.65bn, D/E 0.60x) over distribution growth.
5. **No structural disruption:** Reusable packaging, plastic substitution, or import competition do not materially erode Fibre/Glass volumes in the next 5 years.

*Verification triggers:* FY27 H1 results (Feb 2027) for impairment reversal evidence; net debt/EBITDA trajectory; volume trends in Fibre.

### 3. Business Quality & Moat

| Dimension | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| **Moat Type** | **Scale + Switching Costs (Narrow)** |
| **Strength** | Narrow — durable in ANZ, but commodity-like |
| **Trend** | Narrowing — glass impairment signals overcapacity; Fibre faces recycled fibre cost volatility |

**Moat Analysis:**

- **Scale (Fibre):** Orora is #1 in Australian corrugated packaging (~40% share). High fixed costs (paper machines, converting plants) and transport economics create a ~200km economic radius around each plant. New entrant would need ~AUD 500m+ capex for viable scale.
- **Switching Costs:** Large beverage/food customers (Treasury Wine, Foster's, Visy rivals) co-design packaging specs, integrate JIT delivery, and qualify glass moulds. Switching costs are real but not prohibitive — customers dual-source.
- **Intangible Assets:** Limited. Brands are B2B (Orora, Saverglass). No pricing power beyond cost pass-through.
- **Network Effect:** None.
- **Cost Advantage:** Integrated fibre chain (paper mill → corrugator) gives ~5–8% cost edge vs. pure converters. Glass benefits from cullet (recycled glass) procurement scale.

**Five-Year Durability Test:**  
Fibre moat holds — packaging intensity per unit of GDP is stable, e-commerce supports corrugated demand. Glass moat is fragile: wine industry structural decline (per capita consumption falling), overcapacity in ANZ, and the FY26 impairment proves assets were carried above recoverable value. **Net assessment: moat narrowing.** The demerger removed North American diversification, concentrating risk in a slow-growth ANZ market.
