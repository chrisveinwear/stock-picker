## PART A — BUFFETT VALUE FRAMEWORK

### 1. Circle of Competence

Gold mining is conceptually accessible — the company extracts ore from the ground, processes it into gold, and sells it on global commodity markets at the prevailing spot price. Anyone can understand the basic model.

However, genuine competence in evaluating a gold miner requires understanding: reserve grade (grams per tonne) and how declining grades compress margins over mine life; strip ratios and their impact on unit economics; All-In Sustaining Cost (AISC) per ounce and how it varies across a portfolio of 17+ operating mines; geopolitical jurisdiction risk across Ghana, Nevada, Australia, Peru, Suriname, and Papua New Guinea; royalty and streaming structures; and the mechanics of how a doubling of company scale through acquisition (Newcrest, November 2023) restructures every financial metric.

**Conclusion: Boundary of circle.** The commodity exposure and valuation framework are comprehensible. The mine-level operational details — what constitutes a good reserve life, whether AISC of $1,450/oz is exceptional or mediocre for a given jurisdiction, how to stress-test reserve estimates — require specialist knowledge. An investor without that expertise must rely heavily on management, independent reserve audits, and gold price assumptions they cannot confidently predict. Proceed with wide margin-of-safety requirements.

### 2. Key Assumptions (3–5)

For the Watch thesis to be correct — and for the stock to generate acceptable returns from here — the following must hold:

1. **Gold price remains above USD 2,600/oz on a mid-cycle basis.** Newmont's economics are leveraged to the gold price. At USD 3,200–3,400/oz (current spot, estimated), margins are exceptional. At USD 2,000/oz (a plausible historical mean-reversion scenario), FCF would compress dramatically. The reconciled IV of AUD 136 assumes a mid-cycle gold price of USD 2,800–3,000/oz. If gold corrects sharply, intrinsic value falls toward the Morningstar/Graham range of AUD 101–108.

2. **The Newcrest integration delivers its promised synergies without further impairments.** Newmont absorbed roughly USD 2.5bn in net losses in 2022–2023 partly due to Newcrest acquisition-related charges and pre-acquisition headwinds. The step-change in FCF from USD 97m (2023) to USD 7,299m (2025) represents integration success, but sustaining that trajectory requires that acquired assets perform to reserve estimates.

3. **Natascha Viljoen's leadership maintains capital discipline.** Tom Palmer's tenure was marked by balance sheet repair (debt from USD 9.4bn in 2023 to USD 5.5bn in 2025) and initiated buybacks. Viljoen inherits a strong balance sheet; the assumption is that capital is returned efficiently rather than reinvested in marginal acquisitions.

4. **AISC holds in the USD 1,400–1,600/oz range (estimate).** Operating leverage is the key value driver. If cost inflation, grade decline, or operational disruptions push AISC toward USD 1,800+/oz, the margin per ounce narrows dramatically and FCF suffers even at elevated gold prices.

5. **Ahafo North and organic project pipeline deliver as guided.** The first gold pour at Ahafo North (September 2025) is a meaningful volume addition. If development projects underperform or production volumes decline from the 6–7m oz/year level, the earnings base shrinks.

### 3. Business Quality & Moat

**Morningstar's verdict: No Economic Moat.** This is the most important single data point in this report and must be taken seriously. Morningstar's Price/Fair Value of 1.34 implies their own fair value estimate is approximately AUD 101.07 — 25% below the current price.

The No Moat rating reflects the fundamental economics of commodity extraction: Newmont does not set the price of gold. Gold is priced by global supply and demand, central bank purchasing, USD strength, and investor sentiment. Newmont has no pricing power whatsoever. Any ounce of gold Newmont produces is identical to any ounce produced by a competitor.

**What Newmont has instead of a moat:**

- *Scale.* As the world's largest gold producer (approximately 6–7 million ounces per year post-Newcrest), Newmont achieves procurement advantages, can attract top engineering talent, and can spread fixed overhead across more production. This is a cost advantage, not a durable moat — a competitor achieving similar scale would negate it.
- *Reserve base.* A large, diversified reserve base across multiple continents provides production longevity. But reserves are depleted assets; maintaining the base requires ongoing capital expenditure (capex was USD 3.0–3.4bn annually in 2024–2025).
- *Jurisdictional diversification.* Operating in Nevada, Australia, Ghana, Peru, and elsewhere reduces single-jurisdiction risk, though it introduces complexity and varying geopolitical risk.
- *Balance sheet strength.* Net cash of USD 3.25bn (AUD 4.71bn) is a genuine advantage in a capital-intensive cyclical industry.

**Moat assessment:** Narrow at best, structurally None. The business generates extraordinary returns at today's gold prices (ROE 25.8%, net margin 33.9%), but this is a gold-price phenomenon rather than a durable competitive advantage. When gold corrected in 2022–2023, Newmont generated cumulative net losses of USD 2.9bn. The quality is real but conditional.

**Five-year moat durability test:** The cost advantage from scale is durable over five years provided production volumes are maintained. The underlying commodity price risk is not within management's control. Rate moat as: Narrow/Conditional (below Morningstar's formal No Moat, but acknowledging the scale advantage).
