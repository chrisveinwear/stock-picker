## PART A — BUFFETT VALUE FRAMEWORK

---

### 1. Circle of Competence

**Assessment: Boundary — proceed with heightened caution**

Newmont's business mechanics can be explained plainly: dig gold out of the ground, sell it at the world price, reinvest a portion in new ore bodies, and return the rest to shareholders. Buffett has historically been sceptical of gold as a store of value ("it just sits there"), but gold *miners* are businesses with cash flows, balance sheets, and management teams — and Berkshire briefly held Barrick Gold shares in 2020, demonstrating the approach is not categorically off-limits.

The critical caveat is pricing power, or rather the complete absence of it. Newmont is a pure price-taker: the company cannot raise its realised gold price by one cent through any action available to management. This eliminates the defining characteristic Buffett seeks — the ability to increase prices faster than costs. Every dollar of earning power is contingent on the market price of gold, a variable determined by macroeconomics, geopolitics, central bank behaviour, and investor psychology, none of which Newmont controls.

**Decision:** This report proceeds on the basis that the business *can* be analysed using standard valuation frameworks, but the gold price dependency creates astructural earnings uncertainty that widens the required margin of safety beyond the standard 30% threshold to at least 35% for a high-conviction position.

---

### 2. Key Assumptions (3–5 explicit thesis dependencies)

1. **Gold price sustains above USD 2,400/oz over the 10-year DCF horizon.** Current spot is approximately USD 3,200/oz. The thesis does not require the current price to hold — it requires that the cycle-adjusted mid-price does not revert to pre-2020 levels (~USD 1,800/oz). If it does, earnings collapse by ~50% and the stock is materially overvalued at AUD 132.
2. **Newcrest integration delivers the guided USD 500m+ in annual synergies.** The acquisition closed November 2023 and added Cadia (NSW), Lihir (PNG), Red Chris (Canada), and Telfer (WA). Q1 2026 record earnings suggest integration is proceeding well, but synergy capture over the full cost base has not been independently verified.
3. **Reserve replacement rate stays at or above 100% annually.** Gold mines are depleting assets. Newmont's current reserve life is approximately 12–13 years. Failure to replace reserves transforms the company into a runoff asset and collapses terminal value.
4. **No material sovereign or permitting disruption in Ghana, Peru, or Papua New Guinea.** Roughly 40% of production originates in jurisdictions with elevated political risk. Nationalisation or punitive royalty regimes have precedent in gold-producing nations.
5. **New CEO Natascha Viljoen maintains the capital allocation discipline established under Tom Palmer.** Palmer's succession appears planned and orderly (announced September 2025, effective post-transition); June 2026 executive appointments reinforce the bench. A strategy pivot — particularly towards aggressive M&A at peak gold prices — would be the primary governance risk.

### 3. Business Quality & Moat

**Moat type:** None confirmed | **Strength:** None (Morningstar agrees — "No Economic Moat" as of 28 June 2026) | **Trend:** N/A

**Why there is no moat.** Gold is the world's oldest commodity. Every ounce of Newmont gold sells at exactly the same price as every ounce produced by Evolution Mining, Barrick, or an artisanal miner in West Africa. There are no switching costs (buyers of gold do not prefer Newmont gold), no network effects, no intangible assets that command a price premium, and no cost advantage durable enough to constitute a moat. Newmont's AISC is approximately USD 1,400–1,600/oz — respectable, but not structurally below peers and certainly not approaching the kind of cost leadership that would qualify as a Buffett-style moat.

**What Newmont does have — scale advantages that are real but not durable:**
- World's largest gold producer post-Newcrest (~8–8.5 million oz/year), giving superior access to capital markets, lower cost of debt, and greater diversification of geopolitical risk
- Portfolio of Tier 1 assets (Boddington WA, Tanami NT, Cadia NSW, Lihir PNG, Yanacocha Peru, Ahafo Ghana) that would take decades and tens of billions to replicate
- Balance sheet with net cash of USD 3.25bn (AUD 5.1bn) and D/E of only 15.8% — rare in mining

These advantages support a below-average cost of capital and help the company survive gold price troughs that eliminate marginal producers. They do not create pricing power or allow returns to exceed cost of capital on a normalised, through-cycle basis. At peak gold prices, ROIC is ~28%; cycle-adjusted ROIC falls to approximately 12–14% — adequate, but not exceptional.

**Moat durability five-year test:** No deterioration expected, because there is no moat to deteriorate. The risk is that elevated gold prices draw capital into new production, increasing global supply and eventually pressuring prices — the standard commodity cycle. New project economics require ~USD 1,800–2,000/oz to justify investment at current construction costs; current prices well above incentive price will attract supply over a 5–10 year lag.
