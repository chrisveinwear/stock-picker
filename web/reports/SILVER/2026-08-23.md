# SILVER — Physical Metal Investment Analysis

**VERDICT — avoid** — Silver trades at US$69.47/oz (A$98/oz), a 98% premium to its incentive price of US$35/oz and 132% above the 90th-percentile AISC of US$30/oz. The cost curve provides no meaningful price discipline because ~72% of supply is by-product of zinc, lead and copper mining. While structural solar/industrial demand growth and monetary tailwinds (gold proxy, central bank diversification) justify a persistent premium to marginal cost, the current level far exceeds any fundamental supply/demand or macro fair-value estimate. New capital should stay away; holders may trim on strength.

---

## 1. Commodity Classification & Characteristics

Silver is a **dual-purpose monetary/industrial metal** — the only commodity with material demand from both safe-haven/monetary flows and energy-transition industrial use.

| Characteristic | Assessment |
|----------------|------------|
| **Primary class** | Monetary (gold beta ~1.5×) with industrial overlay |
| **Monetary drivers** | Real yields, USD, central bank diversification, inflation expectations |
| **Industrial drivers** | Solar PV (~20% of demand, growing 10%+ YoY), electronics, EVs, brazing alloys |
| **Supply elasticity** | Very low — 72% by-product of base metals; primary silver mines <30% of supply |
| **Stock-to-flow** | ~25–30× (above-ground stocks ~1.8–2.0 Moz vs annual mine supply ~830 Moz) |
| **Price elasticity of demand** | Jewellery/silverware: elastic; Solar/industrial: inelastic (substitution limited) |
| **Key ratio** | Gold/Silver ratio currently ~88× (20-yr avg ~65–70×) → silver historically cheap vs gold |

**Implication:** Silver cannot be valued on cost-curve alone. The monetary bid sets a floor well above AISC, but the current 98% premium to incentive price requires extraordinary monetary/industrial demand to sustain.
