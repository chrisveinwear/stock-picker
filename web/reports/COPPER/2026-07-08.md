## PART A — COMMODITY FRAMEWORK

### 1. Circle of Competence

Copper is fully within the circle of competence for a value-oriented commodity investor. Its demand drivers are straightforward and countable: every metre of electrical cable, every electric-vehicle motor winding, every solar-panel junction box, every data-centre busbar. Supply is geologically constrained — ore bodies are fixed, grades decline as mines age, and new discoveries require a decade of permitting and construction before they produce a single tonne.

There is no opacity here. Copper cannot be disrupted by software. In most high-conductivity electrical applications it cannot be economically replaced (aluminium substitutes in some overhead power lines but not in motors, inverters, or precision electronics), and the physics of electrification mean every percentage point of EV fleet penetration adds a calculable, auditable tonnage of incremental demand. The business logic — spot versus incentive price, cost curve, inventory draws — is transparent and well-sourced.

**Circle of Competence verdict: Inside.** Proceed with full analysis.

### 2. Key Assumptions (5)

The investment thesis for physical copper rests on these five explicit, verifiable assumptions. Each carries a named failure mode that should be re-examined quarterly.

1. **Energy-transition demand materialises on schedule.** Global EV fleet penetration reaches 30%+ by 2030, with parallel build-out of wind, solar, and high-voltage transmission infrastructure adding 4–6 Mtpa of incremental copper demand above 2020 baseline by the early 2030s. *Failure mode: China EV export saturation slows; Western permitting delays defer grid investment; slower-than-expected ICE phase-out in emerging markets.*

2. **Chilean and Peruvian ore grade continues to decline.** Brownfield grades at Escondida, El Teniente, Collahuasi, and Antamina fall approximately 0.5% per year, tightening effective mine supply without new projects filling the gap. *Failure mode: High-grade discoveries at existing mine footprints or underground expansions arrest decline.*

3. **No major greenfield projects come online before 2028–2030.** The 10–15-year development cycle for copper mines means projects economically justified by today's prices will not produce at scale until the end of the decade. *Failure mode: Fast-tracked government-sponsored projects in Africa or Central Asia compress the timeline; DRC Kamoa-Kakula ramp exceeds plan.*

4. **China copper demand grows at 1–2% per annum, not collapses.** The post-property-sector rebalancing shifts Chinese copper consumption from construction pipe to EV motors and grid infrastructure, sustaining aggregate demand rather than destroying it. *Failure mode: Property contraction proves deeper than expected; China's per-capita copper intensity peaks earlier than models assume.*

5. **AUD/USD remains in the 0.62–0.70 range.** A materially stronger AUD (e.g., >0.75) reduces the AUD-denominated return on copper held in USD terms, even if USD copper prices are unchanged. *Failure mode: Commodity boom plus RBA caution pushes AUD sharply higher, eroding returns for Australian investors.*

### 3. Supply/Demand Balance & Structural Moat

**Demand — Structural Acceleration**

Copper demand is experiencing a once-in-a-generation structural step-change. Unlike prior commodity super-cycles driven by Chinese construction (cyclical), the current demand vector is electrification (structural and legislatively-mandated across multiple sovereign jurisdictions).

| Demand Driver | Cu Intensity | Incremental Demand by 2030 |
|---|---|---|
| Electric vehicles (4× copper of ICE) | ~85 kg/vehicle | +2.0–2.5 Mtpa |
| Offshore & onshore wind (3–4 t/MW) | ~3.5 t/MW | +0.8–1.2 Mtpa |
| Utility-scale solar PV (4–5 t/MW DC) | ~4.5 t/MW | +0.5–0.8 Mtpa |
| Grid modernisation & battery storage | Variable, high | +1.0–1.5 Mtpa |
| AI data centres & hyperscaler capex | Busbars, power distribution | +0.3–0.5 Mtpa |

Total incremental energy-transition demand by 2030: approximately **4–6 Mtpa** above 2020 baseline. Against 2025 mine supply of ~22–23 Mtpa, this represents a 20–25% demand growth that existing mines cannot absorb without meaningful price incentive for new supply.

**Supply — Structurally Constrained**

The supply-side response to high prices is slower in copper than in virtually any other industrial commodity:

- **Grade decline:** Average ore grade at Chilean mines has fallen from ~1.5% Cu in 1990 to below 0.7% today. Processing the same tonne of rock yields progressively less copper each year — a hidden "effective supply decline" invisible in headline production tonnage.
- **Mine development cycle:** From discovery to first production averages 16–17 years globally. Even fast-tracked projects take 8–10 years through permitting, construction, and commissioning.
- **Permitting headwinds:** Environmental approvals in Chile (water usage in the Atacama Desert), Peru (social licence from indigenous communities), and the US (NEPA review) have materially extended timelines across the industry. Cobre Panama — a ~400kt/year mine — remains shuttered since November 2023 following a Supreme Court ruling, removing ~2% of global supply from the market with no clear resolution timeline.
- **S&P Global estimate:** A cumulative supply gap of 10+ Mtpa could emerge by 2035 if no new major projects receive final investment decisions and are built to plan.

**Structural Moat Assessment**

Copper's moat as an investable commodity is geological scarcity combined with political and social complexity. Unlike oil (which can be produced rapidly from existing fields) or nickel (abundant in laterite deposits), copper supply is genuinely constrained by the accessible stock of high-grade, economically viable ore bodies — and that stock is declining. This creates a multi-year structural price support that is qualitatively different from the cyclical commodity markets.

**Moat: Wide | Stable-to-Widening | Duration: Decade-scale**

### 7. Sell Criteria Check

Four criteria adapted to physical commodity holdings:

**Criterion 1 — Significantly above incentive price (>30% premium)?**
At $6.17/lb vs $5.00/lb incentive, the current premium is 23.4%. The avoid zone threshold is $6.50/lb (~30% premium). The price is elevated but not yet in trim territory. **Not triggered** — but proximity is a warning: $0.33/lb separates the current price from the avoid zone.

**Criterion 2 — Structural deficit thesis destroyed?**
No. Grade declines at major Chilean mines are measurable, ongoing, and published monthly by Cochilco. Energy-transition demand is legislatively mandated across the EU, US (IRA/IIJA), and China (Made in China 2025, NEV mandate). The 10+ Mtpa long-run deficit estimate from S&P Global is not credibly challenged by any mainstream supply scenario. **Not triggered.**

**Criterion 3 — Better opportunity exists in the commodity universe?**
Partially. Lithium and nickel — adjacent electrification metals — are trading at or below their own cost floors following the 2022–2025 correction, potentially offering better risk/reward for investors willing to accept the supply-side complexities of those markets. Gold continues to trade at a substantial premium to AISC but is supported by central-bank buying and monetary demand that copper lacks. **Partially triggered — marginal argument for reviewing alternatives, not for exiting copper.**

**Criterion 4 — Macro black swan impairs the thesis (China demand collapse)?**
Not currently evidenced. Chinese copper imports remain robust; the sectoral pivot from construction pipe to EV and grid copper is progressing. Manufacturing PMI is mixed but not signalling collapse-level deterioration. **Not triggered.**

**Overall Sell Criteria Verdict: Hold.** One criterion partially triggered. Maintain existing physical positions. Trim aggressively only if spot pushes above US$6.50/lb (avoid zone) or if Chinese EV/grid demand data deteriorates over 2–3 consecutive quarters.

### 13. Bridgewater — Risk Assessment

**Interest Rate Sensitivity**

Copper has moderate negative sensitivity to rising real interest rates through two channels:
1. **USD channel:** Higher US real rates → stronger USD → lower commodity prices in USD terms (historically: every 5% DXY rise = ~5–8% copper price decline)
2. **Demand channel:** Higher borrowing costs → delayed infrastructure investment → lower medium-term demand

At current RBA rates (post-normalisation, ~3.5–4.0%), the domestic rate environment is broadly neutral. The key sensitivity is to US Fed policy and DXY movements.

**Inflation Sensitivity:** Positive in moderate ranges (2–5% CPI). Copper has historically outperformed in moderate inflationary environments as producer input costs rise, tightening supply margins for higher-cost producers. Extreme inflation (>8%) risks recession and demand destruction — net negative.
