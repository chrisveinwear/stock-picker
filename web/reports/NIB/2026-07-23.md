## PART A — BUFFETT VALUE FRAMEWORK

### 1. Circle of Competence

**Boundary — understandable business, but not a classic Buffett insurance economics.** nib is Australia's third-largest private health insurer (behind Medibank Private and Bupa), earning its money by collecting premiums from members and paying out claims to hospitals and providers, with a spread (the "management expense ratio" gap) plus investment income on reserves as the profit engine. The business model itself is simple to explain to a twelve-year-old: collect premiums, pay claims, keep the difference.

Where it departs from Buffett's favourite insurance economics (GEICO, National Indemnity) is that Australian private health insurance is **not a float-compounding business** in the way U.S. P&C insurance is. Premiums are government-regulated (annual increases require Health Minister approval), risk is pooled via a mandatory industry risk-equalisation scheme, and capital is held largely in low-risk instruments under APRA prudential rules rather than deployed opportunistically by management. That removes the single biggest lever Buffett prizes in insurers — the ability to invest float aggressively.

**Verdict: inside the circle, but at the boundary.** Proceed with the analysis, but temper return expectations versus a true float-compounder.
