### 3. Business Quality & Moat
**Moat type: Scale / Cost Advantage (Narrow but durable).**  
**Strength: Narrow.**  
**Trend: Stable.**

*Analysis:* IOO's moat is not the ETF itself — ETF wrappers are commoditised — but BlackRock's **iShares platform scale** (AUM >US$3.5tn globally) which drives: (a) lower all-in costs via securities lending revenue offsetting fees, (b) tighter creation/redemption spreads, (c) superior index replication through full replication (not optimisation) given the liquid large-cap universe. The S&P Global 100 index itself has **brand/intellectual property moat** — it is the de facto "global blue-chip" benchmark.

*Five-year durability test:*  
- ✅ Index methodology unchanged since 1999; S&P DJI has no incentive to alter a flagship product.  
- ✅ BlackRock's scale advantage compounds: larger AUM → lower cost → more flows → larger AUM.  
- ⚠️ Threat: Zero-fee ETF competitors (e.g., Fidelity ZERO, though not in Australia) and direct-indexing platforms (e.g., OpenInvest, Plenti) could erode the cost moat for large accounts.  
- ⚠️ Threat: Active ETFs and thematic ETFs siphon flow share, but core broad-market allocation remains sticky.

*Verdict:* A **high-quality vehicle** for a "buy the world's best businesses" strategy, but the moat protects BlackRock's franchise, not the investor's return. The investor's return is the market's return minus a slim fee.
