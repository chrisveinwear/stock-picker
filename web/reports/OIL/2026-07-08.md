Unlike gold, oil generates no monetary store-of-value premium and has no persistent above-ground stock. Unlike copper, it is consumed on use and cannot be recycled. **OPEC+ dynamics:**
The alliance produces approximately 40 Mbbl/d across 23 nations. Saudi Arabia (~9.5 Mbbl/d) is the effective swing producer with ~2.5 Mbbl/d of spare capacity at its disposal — the largest discretionary supply lever in any commodity market. Russia (~9–9.5 Mbbl/d) is constrained by Western sanctions on tanker and payment infrastructure but remains the second-largest OPEC+ contributor. In late 2025, OPEC+ announced a production pause (not an increase), signalling continued willingness to manage prices — the meeting decision rather than seasonal or physical-market dynamics is consistently the most powerful near-term price catalyst.

**US shale — the market's structural ceiling:**
The Dallas Fed Q1-2026 survey is the most authoritative breakeven dataset available:
- New-well breakeven: $66/bbl (range $62–70)
- Opex breakeven (existing wells): $43/bbl — at $72, essentially zero shutting in
- At $72 spot, new drilling earns $6/bbl above breakeven — profitable but not a boom-level incentive
- US production at ~13.4 Mbbl/d is near all-time highs; the Permian Basin alone produces ~6.4 Mbbl/d
- Capital discipline post-2022 means operators are growing output ~2–3% per year, not the 15–25% of the pre-2018 boom; if oil sustained above $78–82, expect production to ramp toward 14+ Mbbl/d within 12–18 months

**The critical structural asymmetry in oil supply:** A copper mine takes 10–15 years from discovery to production. A US shale well takes 4–6 months from drill rig to first barrel. This dramatically shorter supply response time is why oil price rallies are self-limiting in a way metals rallies are not — above ~$80, new US wells begin drilling almost immediately, creating a ceiling that has no equivalent in mining commodities.

**Country concentration and the Strait of Hormuz:** Approximately 20–21 Mbbl/d — roughly 20% of global oil supply — transits the Strait of Hormuz daily, including all Saudi Arabian, Iraqi, UAE, and Kuwaiti exports. The March 2026 episode validated the market's acute sensitivity to Hormuz disruption: even the threat of a blockade (not an actual closure) added $25–30/bbl in risk premium within days. The full deflation of this premium by July 2026 means current prices are priced for a fully benign geopolitical environment.

### 7. Geopolitical & Policy Risk

The March–July 2026 period illustrates oil's geopolitical sensitivity more dramatically than almost any equivalent commodity episode in recent years:

**The 2026 geopolitical arc:**
- **March 8–11:** Strait of Hormuz tensions — concerns about Iranian-backed threats to tanker traffic — drove Brent above US$99/bbl. At ~20% of global supply, any credible Hormuz disruption triggers immediate and extreme price responses. The $99 level priced in an approximately 20–30% probability of meaningful supply disruption.
- **April 7:** The US and Iran announced a conditional ceasefire framework. Oil plunged ~15% in days (from ~$99 to ~$84) — one of the largest single-week declines in years. The geopolitical risk premium deflated rapidly.
- **April 12:** Peace talks collapsed within 21 hours. Trump ordered a Strait of Hormuz blockade. Oil bounced sharply to the high $80s–low $90s.
- **May 5:** Fresh Iran negotiation hopes emerged, ASX banks and miners rallied, and oil slid again.
- **July 8:** Brent is at $72 — the entire geopolitical premium from the March episode is gone. The market is priced for a fully benign geopolitical backdrop.

**Geopolitical risk premium currently priced in: approximately zero.**

### 8. Position in the Commodity Cycle

**Oil cycles operate on two distinct timescales: the OPEC management cycle (2–4 years) and the broader energy capex cycle (8–12 years).**

**Current position — OPEC management cycle:**

| Cycle Phase | Characteristics | Current Status |
|-------------|----------------|----------------|
| Early cycle | Prices below cost floor; capex destruction; shutting wells | 2020 (COVID) |
| Recovery | Prices recovering; inventories drawing; OPEC cuts | 2021–2022 |
| Mid-cycle | Prices near incentive price; balanced market; OPEC managing | **2023–present** |
| Late-cycle | Prices well above incentive; new supply surge; OPEC losing discipline | The March $99 spike was a geopolitical episode, not a fundamental cycle position |
| Downcycle | Oversupply; price collapse below cost floor | Not here |

**The March 2026 spike to $99 was not a late-cycle fundamental event** — it was a geopolitical risk-premium episode overlaid on a mid-cycle balanced market. The underlying physical balance at the time of the spike was not materially different from today. Buying at $99 because "oil is in short supply" would have been wrong; selling on the ceasefire spike (to $84) and then watching prices fall further to $72 illustrates the danger of trading geopolitical events. The discipline is to anchor on the incentive price ($75) and the cost curve floor ($66–70), not on geopolitical premiums.

**Broader energy capex cycle:**
- Global upstream oil capex has recovered from the 2020 crash but remains below the 2013–2014 peak
- US independents are running "capital discipline" — dividends and buybacks before growth capex
- NOCs (OPEC national oil companies) are maintaining capacity capex but not aggressively expanding
- There is no wave of new non-OPEC supply threatening to overwhelm OPEC+ discipline (unlike 2014–2016 when US shale growth was unconstrained)
- This capex profile supports continued OPEC+ price management in the $70–80 range through at least 2027

**Cycle verdict:** Mid-cycle, OPEC-managed equilibrium. Not at the early-cycle undervaluation that made oil a compelling buy (2020: $20–40); not at the late-cycle excess that makes it an avoid (2022: $120+; March 2026: $99 geopolitical spike). The "watch" verdict reflects this mid-cycle positioning — the commodity is fairly to slightly attractively priced, and the best incremental entry would come on a pullback toward the $66–70 AISC support band.
