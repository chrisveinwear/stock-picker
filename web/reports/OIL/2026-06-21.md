## PART A — COMMODITY VALUE FRAMEWORK

---

### Q1. Commodity Classification & Characteristics

**Class: Energy** — Oil is the world's most important and most geopolitically sensitive traded commodity. Unlike industrial metals or monetary metals, crude oil is dominated by three interlocking dynamics:

1. **OPEC+ supply management** — a cartel controlling ~42% of global supply actively manages production to defend a target price band
2. **Geopolitical disruption premium** — the Strait of Hormuz, through which ~21 mb/d transits, is the single most vulnerable chokepoint in global commodity markets
3. **Short-cycle US shale elasticity** — uniquely, shale wells can be drilled and producing in 6–12 months, capping medium-term upsides in a way no other commodity has

**Defining event — June 2026:** The 2026 US-Iran war (commencing February 28) closed the Strait of Hormuz for ~3.5 months, triggering the **largest supply disruption in the history of the global oil market** (IEA characterisation). Brent peaked at US$126/bbl in March before falling ~36% as a US-Iran peace deal was announced in mid-June. The market is now transitioning from crisis pricing to structural fundamental pricing.

### Q3. Demand Structure

**2026E demand:** ~101.3–103.1 mb/d — **down ~1.1 mb/d YoY** from 2025. First meaningful non-COVID demand decline in modern history, driven by price-induced demand destruction at $100–126/bbl and the resulting economic slowdown.

**Top 3 demand drivers:**
1. **Transport (road + aviation + marine) — ~55%** — EV penetration eroding gasoline demand in OECD at ~2–3%/yr
2. **Industrial / Petrochemical — ~20%** — the only genuine long-term demand growth segment (not EV-displaced)
3. **China — ~11–12 mb/d** — the single most important swing factor; EV penetration rising, moderating net demand growth

**2027 outlook:** IEA projects demand to rebound **+2 mb/d** as high-price demand destruction reverses and economic growth normalises. The structural trajectory thereafter is toward a peak around 2028–2030.

This "stranded asset" risk structurally suppresses long-cycle FIDs even at $80 — paradoxically a medium-term supply constraint supporting prices.

**Australian context:** Australia's EV penetration (~20–25% of new car sales) lags OECD by 3–5 years. ASX oil producers (WPL, STO) may see domestic demand resilience slightly longer than global peers.

### Lens 7 — Bridgewater / Ray Dalio: Macro & Real Asset Framework

**All-Weather quadrant (June 2026):** Stagflation — falling growth (~2.3% global GDP) + rising inflation (~4.2% OECD CPI). Historically, commodities outperform in stagflation.

**However — this stagflation is self-correcting:**
Oil *caused* the stagflation by spiking to $126/bbl. As the cause unwinds (peace deal → Hormuz normalisation → lower oil prices), the inflation component falls simultaneously. This is categorically different from the 1970s stagflation where supply constraints were structural and multi-year.

**Macro dashboard:**

| China GDP | ~4.2% | Slowing | Key headwind |

**Dalio's debt cycle insight:** Heavily-indebted democracies cannot sustain commodity supply shocks for long — political costs of $5/gallon gasoline and industrial slowdowns force intervention (SPR releases, diplomatic resolution). This played out exactly as predicted in the 2026 conflict. **Macro verdict: Neutral to Mildly Bearish.** The stagflation tailwind is temporary and self-correcting.
