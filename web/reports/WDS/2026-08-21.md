## PART A — BUFFETT VALUE FRAMEWORK

### 1. Circle of Competence

**Inside.** Woodside is Australia's largest independent oil and gas producer with a clear, understandable business: develop hydrocarbon resources, produce and sell LNG, crude oil, condensate, and natural gas. The value chain — exploration → development → production → marketing — is transparent. Key assets (North West Shelf, Pluto LNG, Sangomar, Scarborough) are long-life, capital-intensive infrastructure with known reserve bases. The commodity price dependency (Brent crude, JKM LNG) is the primary external variable, but the operational mechanics are well within a value investor's circle of competence. The energy transition overlay (carbon management, new energy) adds complexity but remains a minority of capital allocation today.

### 2. Key Assumptions (3–5)

1. **Brent crude averages USD 70–80/bbl** over the next decade (current ~USD 75), supporting LNG netbacks above USD 10/mmBtu.
2. **Sangomar and Scarborough ramp to nameplate** without material cost overruns; Pluto Train 2 proceeds on schedule.
3. **Capital intensity moderates** post-2026 as major growth capex (Scarborough, Pluto T2) peaks, shifting cash flow from negative FCF to positive.
4. **Dividend policy holds** at ~80% of underlying net profit after tax, with franking credits valuable to Australian shareholders.
5. **No structural impairment** to LNG demand from accelerated electrification in key Asian markets (Japan, Korea, China, Taiwan) before 2035.

*Verification triggers:* Quarterly production reports, LNG contract pricing disclosures, capex guidance updates, dividend declarations.

### 3. Business Quality & Moat

**Moat Type:** **Scale / Cost Advantage (Narrow, Stable → Narrowing)**  
**Morningstar Moat Rating:** None (as of 2026-06-28) — I concur.

**Analysis:**  
Woodside's moat derives from **incumbency in Australian LNG infrastructure** — the North West Shelf (operating since 1989) and Pluto LNG (since 2012) are stranded assets with dedicated upstream gas supply, processing trains, and shipping logistics that cannot be replicated quickly or cheaply. This confers a **cost advantage** (lowest-quartile operating cost per boe among global LNG majors) and **switching costs** for buyers tied to long-term sale-and-purchase agreements (SPAs).

However, the moat is **narrow, not wide**:
- **Commodity price taker:** Zero pricing power; revenue is a function of Brent/JKM benchmarks.
- **Reserve life ~15 years** (1,200 MMboe 2P reserves / 199 MMboe annual production) — finite without exploration success or acquisition.
- **Capital intensity extreme:** 2025 capex USD 7.97bn vs EBITDA USD 9.28bn — FCF negative even at record production.
- **Energy transition risk:** Long-term demand outlook for LNG is contested; no meaningful moat in "new energy" (hydrogen, ammonia, CCS) yet.

**Five-Year Durability Test:** The physical LNG assets remain advantaged through 2030. But the *economic* moat narrows as (a) growth capex consumes cash flow, (b) NWS legacy contracts roll off (lower-margin tolling replaces equity gas), and (c) global LNG supply wave (Qatar, US) pressures netbacks post-2027. Moat trajectory: **narrowing**.
