## PART A — BUFFETT VALUE FRAMEWORK

### 1. Circle of Competence

Woodside Energy Group is Australia's largest independent oil and gas producer, operating as a fully integrated LNG company. Its core business is conceptually straightforward: extract hydrocarbons from offshore fields concentrated off Western Australia, liquefy natural gas at onshore LNG trains, and sell LNG, crude oil, and natural gas liquids under a combination of long-term offtake contracts and spot markets to Asian and European buyers.

The business model is understandable. Revenues equal production volumes multiplied by realised commodity prices, less operating costs, depreciation on large fixed assets, and interest on project-financing debt. The key value drivers — reservoir quality, LNG contract terms and duration, spot LNG (JKM) price cycles, capital project execution, and AUD/USD exchange rate — can each be studied and understood by a patient generalist investor.

**Assessment: Boundary of circle of competence.** The underlying business mechanics are clear. The challenge is that intrinsic value depends heavily on long-run LNG prices, which in turn depend on the speed of Asia's energy transition, Chinese industrial recovery, competing supply from US Gulf Coast LNG exporters, and geopolitical disruptions to existing supply chains. These variables introduce genuine uncertainty that cannot be resolved analytically — they require a probability-weighted scenario approach rather than a point estimate. This does not make Woodside uninvestable; commodity businesses with hard assets, contracted revenues, and long reserve lives can be excellent investments at the right price. But the valuation range must be wide, and the margin of safety correspondingly larger than for a pure consumer franchise.

### 2. Key Assumptions (thesis depends on all five)

1. **Asian LNG demand holds through the transition.** Japan, South Korea, and China continue to require LNG through 2035+ for base-load power and industrial heat. The rapid build-out of Asian renewables delays rather than eliminates LNG demand within the investment horizon.

2. **Scarborough/Pluto T2 delivers on schedule and within budget.** The project adds approximately 5 Mtpa of LNG capacity; first cargo is targeted ~2026–2027. A cost overrun greater than 20% or a schedule slip beyond 2028 would further strain FCF and balance sheet metrics, and would call management execution ability into question.

3. **Long-run LNG prices normalise around USD 9–12/MMBtu.** Post-Ukraine war LNG prices have partially normalised. The thesis does not require elevated spot prices — it requires that prices stay above the marginal cost of supply (approximately USD 8–9/MMBtu for Australian LNG), which would support reasonable asset returns.

4. **AUD/USD remains in the 0.63–0.75 range.** Woodside earns and reports in USD but trades on the ASX in AUD. A structurally stronger AUD (above 0.80) would mechanically compress AUD-denominated dividends and earnings for Australian investors without any operational deterioration.

5. **Management maintains capital discipline post-Scarborough.** The thesis requires that once the current peak capex cycle concludes (~2026–2027), management allows FCF to rebuild the balance sheet rather than immediately sanctioning another major project at the same scale.

### 3. Business Quality & Moat

**Moat type:** Narrow — anchored in long-duration LNG infrastructure, contracted offtake agreements, and geographic proximity to Asian demand.

**Morningstar moat reconciliation:** Morningstar assigns Woodside no economic moat, which is a coherent position: commodity producers without proprietary cost structures rarely earn consistent excess returns through a price cycle, and WDS's ROE averaged only ~10% over the available four-year history (well below typical wide-moat thresholds). I assign a narrow moat based on three factors that Morningstar's framework may not fully weight: (1) Woodside's integrated LNG infrastructure — the North West Shelf platform, Pluto LNG, and now Scarborough — represents decades of sunk capital and regulatory approvals that cannot be replicated on any reasonable timeline; (2) it holds 10–20 year offtake contracts with Japanese, Korean, and Taiwanese utilities, creating genuine switching costs for buyers who have built LNG regasification infrastructure around Woodside supply; and (3) geographic proximity to Asian demand centres provides a structural freight cost advantage over Gulf of Mexico LNG exporters (approximately USD 0.50–1.00/MMBtu freight savings per cargo on Asian routes). These factors support margins above the industry floor but not consistently above the industry average — hence narrow, not wide.

**Moat strength:** Narrow. **Moat trend:** Stable near-term (contracted revenues protect earnings through ~2032 on many facilities); narrowing beyond 10 years as energy transition accelerates.

**Five-year moat durability test:** Within a five-year horizon, the moat is reasonably durable. Long-term contracts extend well into the early 2030s. The Pluto platform, NWS, and the forthcoming Scarborough facility each have decades of productive life remaining. The risk to the moat within five years is low. The risk beyond ten years is material and must be monitored quarterly.

**Business quality verdict:** Good, not great. A high-margin, cash-generative business when commodity prices cooperate; capital-intensive and volatile when they do not. The FY2025 record production of 198.8 MMboe alongside negative FCF of -USD 782m captures this tension precisely.

### 4. Management Assessment

**Integrity:** No red flags detected. Woodside maintains standard ASX large-cap governance. CEO Liz Westcott (promoted from CFO in 2024) and Chair Richard Goyder addressed the April 2026 AGM without controversy. No management integrity issues are flagged in the available news or announcements.

**Capital allocation track record:** Mixed. The FY2022 merger with BHP Petroleum was strategically sensible — it diversified the reserve base and added Gulf of Mexico exposure at a reasonable price. However, it was followed almost immediately by the commitment to Scarborough, a USD 12bn+ project, resulting in total debt growing from USD 6.77bn (2022) to USD 13.72bn (2025). This sequencing — one major commitment layered directly onto another — is a pattern that deserves scrutiny. The record production in FY2025 (198.8 MMboe) validates operational strategy, but funding a USD 0.59/share dividend while FCF was -USD 782m is a yellow flag. This is balance sheet optionality being consumed to support the income investor base; it is acceptable for one year, but not as a multi-year posture during a capital build.

**Owner mentality:** Partially present. Woodside's dividend policy targets 50% of underlying NPAT — an appropriate capital allocation discipline that provides some protection against cash being misallocated. The concern is the dividend-during-negative-FCF decision, which prioritised yield optics over balance sheet repair. Post-Scarborough, this tension should resolve.

**Institutional imperative risk:** Elevated. The acquisition-then-build sequencing, combined with ongoing analyst and market pressure to grow production, creates a risk that management sanctions the next large project before balance sheet repair is complete. This is the single most important management risk to monitor.
