## PART A — BUFFETT VALUE FRAMEWORK

### 1. Circle of Competence

Domino's Pizza Enterprises (DMP.AX) is the master franchisee of the Domino's brand across Australia, New Zealand, Japan, and several European markets including France, Belgium, the Netherlands, Germany, Denmark, and Luxembourg. It earns royalties and fees from sub-franchisees, operates company-owned stores, and sells dough and ingredients into the network. The company is not Domino's Pizza Inc (DPZ.US) — it is the largest downstream operator of the Domino's franchise globally.

The business model is simple: customers order pizza digitally or by phone; franchisees make and deliver the product; DMP takes a royalty percentage of network sales plus a corporate margin on company-owned stores. The ordering technology (app, GPS order tracker, predictive ordering) and delivery logistics are understandable. The complexity lies in multi-regional operations — each of ANZ, Japan, and Europe has different competitive dynamics — but this is an analytical challenge, not a fundamental barrier to comprehension.

**Verdict: Inside circle of competence.** The unit economics of a pizza franchise are transparent. The investment risks are operational and financial, not structural or technological in a way that exceeds my understanding.

---

### 2. Key Assumptions

The thesis depends explicitly on the following assumptions, each of which must be verified at the next results announcement (FY2026, expected August 2026):

1. **Earnings recovery toward analyst forward EPS of AUD 1.35 is achievable.** This requires net income recovery from a loss of AUD 4m (FY2025) to approximately AUD 128m — an enormous swing that demands meaningful same-store sales recovery and margin expansion. The entire forward valuation case rests on this assumption.

2. **The "everyday low prices" strategy (announced August 2025) arrests the customer exodus in ANZ.** The H1 FY2026 results (released February 2026) confirmed customers were departing rather than accepting full prices. If the lower price points restore habitual frequency without proportional volume recovery, the strategy will be margin-dilutive.

3. **European operations stabilise or are restructured without a further equity injection or debt-funded write-off.** Germany has been chronically loss-making; if DMP divests (likely at a loss) or continues absorbing the drag, debt reduction stalls.

4. **Net debt of AUD 1.35bn is serviceable at current earnings.** Implied interest coverage is approximately 1.9× EBIT at current operating margins — critically low. Any further volume deterioration could create covenant pressure.

5. **New ANZ CEO Merrill Pereyra (appointed January 2026) executes the strategic reset.** His track record in this role covers fewer than seven months at the time of this report; no performance data exists.

### 3. Business Quality & Moat

**Moat type:** Intangible assets (Domino's brand) + moderate switching costs (habitual app-based ordering, order history, loyalty programme) + local delivery network density (store density enables faster delivery times, a historical competitive advantage).

**Moat strength: Narrow.** Morningstar independently rates DMP.AX as Narrow Moat — I concur without reservation.

**Evidence for the narrow moat:**
- Domino's is the world's largest pizza delivery chain by store count. In Australia, the brand has decades of recognition and a historically dominant position in pizza delivery.
- The proprietary ordering ecosystem (30-minute guarantee legacy, GPS tracking, app-based reorder convenience) creates moderate switching costs for habitual customers — people who have saved their address and payment details and have developed a weekly ordering habit.
- Local store density creates a delivery-speed advantage: when DMP has sufficient coverage, it can consistently outperform aggregator-dispattched competitors on delivery time — a meaningful advantage when hungry.

**Evidence against a wide moat (why not wide):**
- Pizza is a commodity product with low switching costs for occasional buyers. Brand loyalty is shallow: customers demonstrated they were loyal to the voucher, not the brand, when the August 2025 pricing reset triggered a measurable customer exodus.
- Uber Eats and DoorDash have commoditised the delivery aggregation layer. Domino's proprietary delivery network advantage — once the defining moat — is now replicable via a third-party app.
- Germany proved the brand does not travel universally. Local pizza culture and entrenched incumbents created structural barriers DMP could not overcome despite years of capital investment.
- The expiry in July 2026 of 227,750 employee options due to unmet performance criteria is explicit confirmation that store-level economics are not generating the returns management once projected.

**Moat trend: Narrowing.** The franchise structure and master franchise agreement with Domino's Pizza LLC (US parent) provide a durable legal moat on the brand licence. The competitive moat at the store-economics level is under sustained pressure from aggregators, consumer frugality, and self-inflicted brand damage from the discounting era.

**Five-year moat durability test:** The franchise agreements persist; the brand remains relevant in ANZ; the app ecosystem retains habitual customers. However, if ANZ same-store sales do not recover within two years, the durability case weakens materially. Verdict: Narrow moat survives five years with moderate confidence, provided the pricing strategy stabilises volume.

### 4. Management Assessment

**Integrity check:** No evidence of fraud, deliberate misrepresentation, or shareholder harm of the kind that triggers an auto-veto. However, the record of capital allocation and strategic judgement is poor enough to warrant a material credibility discount.

**Capital allocation track record (from financial history):**
- FY2022–FY2023: Total debt expanded from AUD 1.55bn to AUD 1.75bn to fund aggressive European and Japanese store rollouts. Net income collapsed from AUD 159m (FY2022) to AUD 41m (FY2023). The IRRs on these investments have clearly been deeply negative.
- FY2024: Partial correction — debt reduced to AUD 1.46bn, capex cut from AUD 158m to AUD 90m, net income partially recovered to AUD 96m.
- FY2025: Deterioration resumed — net loss of AUD 4m despite further capex discipline (AUD 76m). Revenue declined to AUD 2.30bn. The European and discounting headwinds overwhelmed the capex savings.
- The pattern is clear: management made a large, leveraged expansion bet in markets it did not understand well enough, and shareholders have paid for it through four years of earnings compression and balance sheet damage.

**Recent corrective signals:**
- Capex reduction from AUD 192m (FY2022) to AUD 76m (FY2025) — the most credible evidence of corrective discipline.
- FCF generation of AUD 91–147m across FY2023–FY2025 confirms the underlying franchise model works when investment is restrained.
- The August 2025 pricing strategy pivot — abandoning vouchers for everyday low prices — is strategically logical even if the execution has been painful.

**Owner mentality:** Jack Cowin's significant shareholding (exact percentage not provided) gives management skin in the game. This is a positive signal, though it has not prevented poor decisions.

**Institutional imperative risk:** Prior leadership followed the DPZ US store-count maximisation playbook without adequate market-specific due diligence. This is a textbook institutional imperative failure — doing what the franchisor encouraged rather than what the local economics warranted.

**Overall rating: Below average.** Auto-veto not triggered. Trust must be rebuilt through results, not promises. The board's decision to replace ANZ leadership (January 2026) is an acknowledgement of prior failure. The new team gets twelve months before a more definitive verdict.
