## PART A — BUFFETT VALUE FRAMEWORK

### 1. Circle of Competence

Domino's Pizza Enterprises (DMP) is the master franchisee for the Domino's brand across Australia, New Zealand, Japan, Germany, France, the Benelux countries, Denmark, Cambodia and Taiwan. The business model is simple to explain: DMP builds/owns a base of corporate stores and franchises the rest, earning royalties, franchise fees, and supply-chain margin on top of corporate-store profits. This is a straightforward consumer-franchise business — squarely **inside** the circle of competence (see CLAUDE.md's Consumer Staples/Cyclical remit), no more complex to underwrite than any QSR chain. The complication isn't comprehensibility, it's that the current unit economics are genuinely broken and mid-repair — that's a business-quality question (Section 3), not a competence question.

### 2. Key Assumptions (3–5)

1. Management's "everyday low price" reset (dropping discount vouchers in favour of Bunnings-style everyday-low-pricing, per the AFR's Aug 2025 reporting) successfully halts the same-store-sales decline and returns the network to volume growth by FY27, rather than simply extracting margin from a shrinking base.
2. No further material impairments beyond the $250m+ write-downs already flagged in the July 2026 trading update — i.e., FY26 H1/FY26 full-year mark the trough of the cycle rather than the start of a deeper one.
3. Net debt (~AUD 1.24–1.35bn) can be serviced and refinanced at reasonable terms against EBITDA of only ~AUD 0.20bn — this is the single biggest swing factor in the thesis.
4. The international network (Japan, France, Germany, Benelux) stabilises without further net store closures beyond the 312 stores closed globally through FY25.
5. Franchisee unit economics recover enough to support renewed network growth (store count expansion), not just a one-off margin bounce from lower promotional spend.

### 3. Business Quality & Moat

**Moat type:** Intangible asset (global brand recognition, Domino's master franchise rights) plus modest scale/network economics (supply-chain buying power across a large store base). **Strength: Narrow** — consistent with Morningstar's own rating below. **Trend: Narrowing.** The moat evidence has moved the wrong way over the past three years: revenue is essentially flat (FY22 $2,285m → FY25 $2,297m, a 3-year CAGR of ~0.2%), net margin has collapsed from 6.96% (FY22) to -0.17% (FY25), and the company posted its first net loss since listing in FY25 alongside 312 global store closures.

**Five-year moat durability test:** The brand itself remains recognisable and the franchise system structurally sound, but the pricing power that used to define the moat (ability to run heavy discounting while still growing volumes) has visibly broken — management has had to abandon the entire promotional playbook. A moat that requires a strategic reset to remain viable is, by definition, narrower than it was five years ago. Durability over the next five years now hinges entirely on execution of the pricing reset (Assumption #1) — this is a moat in the process of being re-proven, not a moat that can be taken for granted.

### 4. Management Assessment

**Integrity:** No evidence of fraud or governance breaches in the data provided — not an auto-veto. However, the pattern of repeated guidance disappointments (10% share-price fall on Feb 2026 H1 results, a further ~10–14% fall in April 2026, and a July 2026 update disclosing a quarter-billion dollars of write-downs alongside falling same-store sales) points to a credibility problem: the market has been surprised negatively, repeatedly, over an 18-month span. That's a soft flag on forecasting discipline and communication, worth weighting in position sizing even absent integrity concerns.

**Capital allocation:** Capex has been cut sharply and consistently — AUD 192m (FY22) → 158m (FY23) → 90m (FY24) → 76m (FY25) — a clear pivot toward capital discipline over network expansion. That preserved FCF positivity through the loss year (FY25 FCF of AUD 91m despite a net loss), which is a genuine positive. Dividend was reportedly lifted in the Feb 2026 H1 result despite a soft headline outcome — worth scrutinising given it was funded from cash flow, not statutory earnings (see Section 14).

**Owner mentality / institutional imperative:** Chairman Jack Cowin (a substantial, long-tenured shareholder) is publicly driving the reset strategy per the AFR reporting — a genuine owner pushing a hard strategic correction is a mild positive signal versus a hired-CEO drift scenario. But the years of aggressive discount-led volume growth that preceded this reset, now unwound at a cost of hundreds of millions in write-downs, is a textbook institutional-imperative pattern: growth-for-growth's-sake strategies that had to be reversed once unit economics broke.
