## PART A — BUFFETT VALUE FRAMEWORK

---

### 1. Circle of Competence

**Inside circle — with an energy-transition boundary flag.**

APA Group owns and operates approximately 15,000 km of natural gas transmission pipelines across mainland Australia and the Northern Territory, making it the country's largest gas infrastructure business. The business model is simple: own long-lived, regulated assets; collect contracted or regulated tolling revenues; distribute the majority of cash flow to securityholders. Revenue is approximately 85%+ contracted or regulated, providing multi-year cash-flow visibility.

Gas pipelines are understandable infrastructure: you build the pipe, you charge throughput fees, you re-invest in the regulatory asset base, you refinance long-term debt. There is no complex technology, no commodity pricing exposure, and no material customer concentration risk.

The boundary flag is energy transition. Natural gas is the fuel under medium-term scrutiny as Australia decarbonises. APA's assets could face throughput volume decline over a 10–15 year horizon as electrification and green hydrogen scale. APA is actively responding — the $480m gas grid expansion announced in February 2026 has been questioned by analysts in the AFR as potentially stranded investment risk. This does not remove the business from my circle, but it requires explicit assumption-making about long-term gas demand that carries genuine uncertainty.

**Assessment: Inside circle of competence. Proceed with analysis, but flag energy transition as a key monitored assumption.**

---

### 2. Key Assumptions (3–5)

These are the explicit assumptions the investment thesis depends on. Each will be checked quarterly.

1. **Gas demand stability (10-year horizon):** Natural gas remains a material part of Australia's energy mix through at least 2035, with total APA throughput volumes declining no faster than 1–2% per annum. This reflects gas as essential firming capacity alongside renewables and as industrial feedstock. If decarbonisation accelerates materially — driven by policy or technology — this assumption breaks first.

2. **Regulated returns remain adequate:** APA's regulatory asset base reviews (under AER/ERA frameworks) continue to deliver approved returns that sustain current EBITDA margins (~61%). A politically motivated reduction in allowable returns would compress distributable cash flows.

3. **Refinancing access on manageable terms:** APA refinances its AUD 13.22bn debt stack without material rate shock. APA is investment-grade (BBB-range). If the average cost of debt rises from an estimated ~5.0% to 6.5%+, the ~AUD 200m annual increase in interest expense would materially reduce distributable cash flow and put distribution coverage under pressure.

4. **Distributions of at least AUD 0.58/security per annum are sustained:** The FY26 distribution is confirmed at approximately AUD 0.580 (interim AUD 0.305 + expected final). This assumption underpins the DDM-based valuation.

5. **Energy transition creates new revenue streams:** APA's pivot toward hydrogen blending, renewable gas, and electrification infrastructure partially offsets declining traditional gas volumes over the medium term. If new-energy revenue is negligible by 2030, the growth rate assumption embedded in valuation (~2.5% terminal) may prove optimistic.

### 3. Business Quality & Moat

**Moat type:** Regulatory/natural monopoly (infrastructure) — a combination of intangible assets (government licences), geographic economies of scale (you cannot economically build a second pipeline network beside an existing one), and high switching costs (customers are locked in by long-term connection agreements and the absence of alternatives).

**Moat strength:** Narrow. APA is not wide-moat because:
- The long-term demand trajectory for natural gas introduces secular risk that could erode the moat's economic value even if the physical moat is intact.
- International expansion (US assets) is largely unwound following regulatory blocking of the CK Infrastructure takeover bid in 2018; APA is now predominantly domestic-focused.
- Return on invested capital (~6.8% ROE on book) is not above-average enough to signal exceptional moat quality.

**Morningstar concurs: Narrow Economic Moat.**

**Moat trend:** Stable for 5 years; narrowing over a 10-year horizon if green energy transition accelerates.

**Five-year moat durability test:** High confidence. Australia's east coast gas market is a critical piece of energy infrastructure supporting ~3 million households and major industrial users including fertiliser plants and LNG exporters. No credible substitute exists for APA's pipeline network on a 5-year horizon. Regulatory frameworks protect returns. The moat is durable through 2031.

**Competitive advantages that compound:**
- Irreplaceable pipeline network — near-impossible to replicate at equivalent cost.
- "Wallumbilla Hub" (Qld) is the critical gas exchange node for eastern Australia.
- Scale in operations: APA runs ~15,000 km across all mainland states; no competitor approaches this geographic footprint.
- Long-term contracted revenue (take-or-pay structures) provide predictable cash flow irrespective of throughput.

#### Valuation Reconciliation

**(a) Input verification**

All authoritative inputs are correct: revenue AUD 3.20bn, EBITDA AUD 1.96bn, net debt AUD 13.05bn, shares 1,323.8m, WACC 8.0% floored, normalized earnings median AUD 284.2m. No hallucinated inputs detected.

**(b) Which assumption drives the gap**

The entire divergence originates from one source: the code uses **NPAT-based owner earnings (AUD 284.2m)** rather than the economically correct distributable cash flow for an infrastructure trust.

For APA, NPAT is severely distorted by massive depreciation (AUD ~869m, derived from EBITDA – EBIT = AUD 1.96bn – AUD 1.09bn). The code's DCF produces a negative equity value because when NPAT-based enterprise value is computed at 8% WACC, the resulting EV (~AUD 7.1bn at 10-year horizon) is less than net debt (AUD 13.05bn), producing a negative equity claim.

This is a known structural limitation of NPAT-based DCF when applied to:
1. Highly leveraged infrastructure trusts where regulated D&A is not a proxy for economic value erosion
2. Businesses that distribute more than NPAT (because distributions include non-cash D&A pass-through, which is a feature of the infrastructure trust model, not a sign of paying out more than is earned)

The Graham Number (AUD 2.65) uses the historical median owner earnings (AUD 0.152/share) × book value (AUD 2.11/share) = sqrt(22.5 × 0.152 × 2.11) = AUD 2.69 ≈ AUD 2.65. This is technically correct arithmetic but the Graham Number is fundamentally inapplicable to a leveraged infrastructure trust — it was designed for asset-light businesses with moderate leverage.

The owner-earnings multiple (AUD 2.15) applies 10× to the normalized NPAT-based earnings of AUD 284m = AUD 2.84bn enterprise equity value / 1,323.8m shares = AUD 2.15. Again, this is not wrong arithmetic but uses the wrong earnings definition.

**(c) Adjusted assumptions with rationale**

The correct earnings base for APA is **distributable cash flow (DCF/security)**, defined as:

| Component | AUD (estimated) |
|-----------|----------------|
| EBITDA | 1,960m |
| Less: cash interest expense (5.0% × AUD 13.22bn) | (661m) |
| Less: maintenance capex (est. ~27% of revenue) | (530m) |
| Less: current tax (minimal — trust structure) | (30m) |
| **Distributable cash flow** | **~739–770m** |
| Per security (1,323.8m) | AUD 0.56–0.58 |

This figure is consistent with the company's actual confirmed FY26 distribution guidance of ~AUD 0.58/security. The distributable CF per security and the distribution are essentially equal (a ~100% payout ratio, typical for regulated infrastructure trusts and REITs), confirming the methodology.
