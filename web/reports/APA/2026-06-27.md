## PART A — BUFFETT VALUE FRAMEWORK

---

### 1. Circle of Competence

**Inside the circle — with one boundary condition.**

APA's business model can be explained to a child: customers pay a toll to move gas through APA's pipes. The pipes stretch approximately 15,000 km across Australia, from Dampier in Western Australia to Wallumbilla in Queensland, and the business is underpinned by long-term, take-or-pay contracts. Revenues are highly visible. The cash conversion from EBITDA to distributable cash is straightforward to model.

The boundary condition — and it is an important one — is the energy transition. The useful life of a pipeline depends entirely on whether gas continues to flow through it. If Australia's decarbonisation accelerates faster than the market currently prices, APA's asset base depreciates faster and contract renewals become harder. That uncertainty sits squarely on the edge of my circle of competence: the question of how quickly Australian households, industry, and generators switch away from gas is genuinely difficult to answer with confidence over a 20-year horizon.

**Verdict:** Inside the circle. The business mechanics are simple. The long-duration tail risk warrants ongoing monitoring. Do not ignore it, but do not let it alone disqualify the investment — the question is whether the current price compensates for it.

---

### 2. Key Assumptions (3–5 explicit thesis assumptions)

The investment case for APA rests on the following explicit assumptions. If any is falsified, the thesis must be revisited:

1. **Gas demand resilience through at least 2032.** Australian east coast gas demand — driven by LNG exports from Gladstone, industrial use, and peaking power generation — remains sufficient to sustain current pipeline utilisation levels for the next six to seven years. Beyond that, decline is possible but the pace is uncertain.

2. **Contract renewals at comparable economics.** Key transmission contracts (particularly on the east coast backbone and western pipelines) expire on a rolling basis through the late 2020s and early 2030s. This analysis assumes they are renewed at rates within 10–15% of current levels, reflecting APA's bottleneck monopoly position.

3. **The A$12.5bn debt stack is refinanced at manageable costs.** As the RBA cutting cycle continues (market pricing another 50bp of cuts through end-2026), APA's average cost of debt stabilises around 4.5–5.0%. A re-acceleration of inflation causing the RBA to pause would be materially negative.

4. **The $480m gas grid expansion delivers adequate returns.** APA management committed A$480m to gas grid expansion in February 2026, which was questioned by analysts. This report assumes the project generates an IRR of 8–9% (consistent with management's stated investment hurdle), and does not impair distributions over the FY27–FY29 period.

5. **Electricity transmission and hydrogen optionality provide partial long-term offset.** APA has been investing in electricity transmission assets and piloting hydrogen blending in sections of its pipelines. This analysis treats these options as real but not currently value-accretive — they provide downside protection rather than upside optionality in the base case.

### 3. Business Quality and Moat

**Moat Type:** Infrastructure monopoly (bottleneck/natural monopoly) + Regulatory entrenchment
**Moat Strength:** Narrow-to-Wide (varies by pipeline)
**Moat Trend:** Narrowing (energy transition headwind; 5–10 year horizon risk)

APA owns infrastructure that nobody will ever duplicate. The Dampier to Bunbury Natural Gas Pipeline (DBNGP) stretches 1,532 km across Western Australia. The Goldfields Gas Pipeline serves the Kalgoorlie mining region. The east coast backbone (Roma Brisbane, Moomba Sydney) connects Queensland gas fields to southern population centres. These assets took decades and tens of billions of dollars to build. No competitor is going to build a parallel pipeline — the economics are prohibitive, the land access is impossibly complex, and the regulatory process would take 15+ years.

This creates genuine bottleneck economics: if you want to move gas from the Carnarvon Basin to Perth, you use APA's pipe or you don't move the gas. That is a real, durable competitive advantage for as long as gas flows.

**The five-year moat durability test (through 2031):** I am reasonably confident the moat holds. The long-dated nature of existing contracts (typically 10–20 years, with take-or-pay provisions) means APA's near-term revenue is substantially locked in regardless of short-term demand fluctuations. The 2031 question is manageable.

**The ten-year moat durability test (through 2036):** This is where genuine uncertainty begins. Australia's Safeguard Mechanism and state-level net-zero targets create policy pressure that could accelerate the shift away from gas in buildings and light industry. LNG export demand is the critical swing factor — if global LNG demand remains robust (driven by Asia's energy security needs), east coast pipeline utilisation stays high. If not, the moat starts to thin.

**Assessment:** Wide moat in the near term on specific assets (DBNGP especially). Narrowing moat trend over the decade. The business is not a Coca-Cola — the moat is physical and regulatory, not demand-driven, which makes it more vulnerable to structural demand shifts.

### 4. Management Assessment

**CEO:** Adam Watson (appointed mid-2024, previously CFO)
**Chairman:** Michael Fraser

Watson's promotion from CFO is encouraging — he knows the balance sheet intimately and is not an empire-builder by reputation. His early actions since taking the helm have been shareholder-friendly: simplification of the business (divesting non-core assets), cost reduction, and focusing the organic growth pipeline on higher-return projects.

**Capital allocation track record:** APA's long-term record is solid. Management walked away from the Hong Kong-backed CK Infrastructure consortium's takeover approach in 2018 (blocked by FIRB) and subsequently demonstrated they could generate value independently. The distribution has grown every year since listing in 2000. They have not made a large, value-destroying acquisition that would warrant a permanent red flag.

**The $480m concern:** In February 2026, Watson committed A$480m to gas grid expansion at a time when the energy transition narrative was creating genuine uncertainty about long-term gas demand. Analysts pushed back hard in the results call, and the AFR ran a critical piece questioning the rationale. Watson will need to demonstrate clear milestones and returns on this investment. It is an amber flag, not a red flag — but it bears watching.

**Owner mentality:** Reasonable. Management remuneration is tied to total security holder return and distribution growth, which aligns incentives appropriately. No evidence of excessive dilution or opportunistic equity raisings.

**Institutional imperative warning:** The $480m capex commitment during a period of strategic uncertainty is consistent with management teams that invest to maintain relevance in their sector even when the long-term demand case is weakening. This is worth monitoring. If a second large discretionary capex program is announced in the next 12–18 months without clear return evidence, it would become a more serious concern.

**Integrity verdict:** No material red flags. Governance is standard for a large ASX infrastructure company.

### 7. Sell Criteria Check

*Buffett's four sell criteria, each explicitly evaluated:*

**(1) Is the stock severely overvalued?**
No. At A$9.35 versus a IV midpoint of A$9.65, APA is trading at roughly fair value — mildly cheap on the midpoint estimate, but the range of outcomes is wide enough that "severely overvalued" is not the right description. It is fully valued, not egregiously so.

**(2) Has the moat been destroyed?**
No — not yet. The pipelines remain essential infrastructure. APA retains its bottleneck position on every major transmission corridor it operates. The energy transition is a slow-moving structural headwind, not a sudden moat collapse. Monitor quarterly.

**(3) Is there a management integrity issue?**
No material issue identified. The $480m capex commitment is an amber flag that warrants scrutiny, but it does not rise to the level of an integrity concern. Watson's track record is short (appointed mid-2024) but positive so far.

**(4) Does a clearly better opportunity exist?**
This is the most relevant criterion at current prices. Infrastructure investors who want superior moat durability (less energy transition exposure) and similar or higher yield could consider TransUrban (TCL) for a road-based toll infrastructure business with clearer long-duration demand growth. For pure yield with less sector risk, CBA and the major banks are offering 5–6% fully-franked yields. APA at A$9.35 is not a compelling relative opportunity. This supports the WATCH verdict.

**Overall Sell Criteria verdict:** No current position to sell. For existing holders: no sell trigger is active. Continue holding; monitor the four criteria quarterly.

### 13. Bridgewater Risk Assessment

**Interest Rate Sensitivity: HIGH**

Infrastructure stapled securities are essentially long-duration bond proxies.
