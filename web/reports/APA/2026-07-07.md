## PART A — BUFFETT VALUE FRAMEWORK

### 1. Circle of Competence — Inside

APA Group is Australia's largest natural gas infrastructure business, owning and operating approximately 15,000 kilometres of high-pressure gas transmission pipelines across all mainland states, plus gas storage facilities, gas-fired power generation, and a growing electricity transmission portfolio. The economics are simple: APA builds or acquires physical infrastructure, signs long-dated contracts with gas shippers and generators, and collects toll-like fees regardless of commodity price movements. Approximately 70% of revenues are regulated or take-or-pay contracted. This is a classic infrastructure concession model — no manufacturing complexity, no consumer-demand forecasting, no near-term technology risk. It sits firmly inside the circle of competence.

The one complexity worth flagging is not a comprehension issue but an assumption issue: long-term natural gas demand under Australia's energy transition. That is addressed explicitly in Section 2.

### 2. Key Assumptions (5)

The entire thesis rests on five verifiable assumptions:

1. **Gas demand persists for 10+ years.** APA's pipeline utilisation must remain commercially viable through the contracted period. The thesis breaks if large industrial customers (east-coast ammonia producers, LNG liquefaction facilities, gas-fired generators) close or switch to electricity before contract expiry. Management's February 2026 commitment of AUD 480m to gas grid expansion signals its own confidence in gas through at least the mid-2030s.

2. **Interest rates peak and gradually ease.** APA carries AUD 14.0bn gross debt. At a blended cost of approximately 6%, annual interest consumes ~AUD 840m — roughly 73% of EBIT at AUD 34.1% operating margin on AUD 3.18bn revenue. Every sustained 100bp rate increase adds ~AUD 140m to annual interest, reducing distributable cash by approximately 18% of normalised net income.

3. **Regulatory frameworks remain supportive.** Regulated pipelines earn returns on the regulated asset base set by the Australian Energy Regulator or negotiated access agreements. An adverse regulatory reset — reducing the allowed rate of return — would impair earnings on roughly 40% of the asset base.

4. **The AUD 480m gas grid expansion proves value-accretive.** CEO Adam Watson committed to this expansion despite significant analyst pushback (per AFR, February 2026). The investment is value-neutral or destructive if contracted volumes disappoint; value-creating only if gas demand holds as management projects.

5. **Distributions are sustainable at AUD 0.57–0.60/security.** APA is a stapled security trust; distributions exceed GAAP net income because depreciation is returned to securityholders rather than reinvested. Sustainability depends on operating cash flow (post-interest, post-maintenance capex) exceeding distributions — a close-run calculation at current levels that is confirmed by FY26 guidance (both interim distribution of AUD 0.305 and FY26 distribution guidance confirmed per June 2026 ASX releases).

### 3. Business Quality & Moat

**Moat type:** Natural monopoly — efficient scale within a geographic corridor.

**Strength: Narrow.** Not wide, for three interconnected reasons. First, regulation can compress returns on ~40% of the asset base at each multi-year regulatory reset cycle. Second, Australia's energy transition introduces a credible 20–30-year demand displacement curve for natural gas — pipelines commissioned today could be materially underutilised before their physical life ends. Third, alternative energy carriers (electrification, green hydrogen) are potential long-run substitutes, though at high conversion cost and on very long time horizons. Morningstar independently rates the moat as Narrow — consistent with this assessment.

Within those constraints, the near-term moat is real and meaningful: it costs billions to replicate the eastern Australian gas network, shipper switching costs are prohibitive (there is physically no alternative route for most contracted volumes), and contracted revenue provides exceptional earnings visibility on a 5-year view.

**Trend: Stable to narrowing.** The electricity transmission push (South Australian electricity contracts, Cunningham Solar connection) is a genuine effort to extend the moat into energy transition infrastructure. However, APA is a newer entrant in electricity transmission, competing with state-owned incumbents and Transgrid that have decades of advantage. The moat in the legacy gas business is stable; the growth extension into electricity is unproven.

**Five-year moat durability test (to FY31):** Existing take-or-pay contracts typically run 15–20 years. On a five-year view, the moat is secure. On a 10–15-year view, the durability narrows materially. APA is better treated as an infrastructure income vehicle — held for distributions and gradual IV accretion — than as a Buffett-style permanent compounder.

### 4. Management Assessment

**Integrity: Pass.** APA has operated as an ASX-listed entity for over 20 years with consistent distribution payments and no material governance scandals or related-party concerns in the public record. No automatic veto triggered.

**Capital allocation track record: Mixed.** APA grew through a leveraged acquisition strategy from approximately 2005–2022, accumulating a large infrastructure portfolio funded by cheap debt. This strategy was rational when real rates were near zero but has left a structurally vulnerable balance sheet as rates normalised. The FY24 net income spike to AUD 978m (vs a normalised ~AUD 200–300m range in surrounding years) almost certainly reflects a significant asset disposal gain — consistent with the post-2022 strategy of portfolio simplification. FY25 net income's return to AUD 99m confirms the one-off nature of FY24. FCF recovery from AUD 26m (FY23) to AUD 320m (FY25) is the key evidence of capex-cycle discipline reasserting itself.

**The AUD 480m decision.** CEO Adam Watson's February 2026 commitment to gas grid expansion amid analyst scrutiny is the live test of capital discipline. The commitment appears to rest on contracted customer demand, but the timing — amid accelerating energy-transition policy — raises the institutional imperative risk Buffett warns about: infrastructure operators continue investing in the incumbent infrastructure model because that is what infrastructure operators do. Not an integrity failure; a judgment call that deserves close monitoring.

**Owner mentality: Acceptable.** APA is institutionally owned (IFM, AustralianSuper, and global infrastructure funds are typical large holders). Management compensation is tied to distributions per security and total securityholder return relative to ASX Utilities Index peers. Not owner-operator alignment, but not misaligned.

**Institutional imperative risk: Present.** The gas grid expansion decision, taken in the face of analyst pushback about energy-transition economics, is a classic institutional imperative signal. APA should be watched for further commitments to gas infrastructure that are difficult to justify on a 10-year NPV basis.
