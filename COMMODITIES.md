# Commodity Analysis Playbook

A comprehensive framework for analysing physical commodities — precious metals, battery/critical minerals, energy, and base metals — in the same structured way as equities, adapted for the unique price-discovery mechanics of physical markets.

## Why Commodity Analysis Differs From Equity Analysis

Commodities have **no earnings, no dividends, no management teams, no moat**. Price is set entirely by the physical balance between supply and demand at the marginal tonne. This changes everything:

| Equity Framework | Commodity Equivalent |
|-----------------|---------------------|
| Intrinsic value (DCF) | Incentive price / long-run marginal cost |
| Margin of safety | Discount to incentive price |
| Moat | Structural supply constraint / demand inelasticity |
| Management quality | Producer discipline / OPEC cohesion |
| Earnings growth | Demand growth rate |
| Revenue | Spot price × volume |
| Sell criteria | Price > 90th percentile AISC; demand shock |

The key insight: **commodities mean-revert to the cost of producing the marginal tonne**. Above that price, new supply enters. Below it, producers curtail. The incentive price is the commodity's centre of gravity — equivalent to intrinsic value for stocks.

## Part A — Commodity Value Framework (8 Questions)

Run these before any detailed analysis.

### 1. Commodity Classification & Characteristics
What type of commodity is it?
- **Monetary** (gold, silver) — store of value, real interest rate sensitive, central bank demand
- **Industrial** (copper, aluminium, zinc) — GDP and manufacturing cycle sensitive
- **Energy transition** (lithium, cobalt, graphite, nickel) — EV/battery demand driven
- **Energy** (oil, gas, coal) — geopolitical, weather, OPEC policy sensitive
- **Bulk** (iron ore, fertilisers) — China construction/agriculture sensitive

Each class has different primary price drivers. Identify the class first.

### 2. Supply Structure
- Who are the top 3 producers? What % of global supply do they control?
- What is the AISC (All-In Sustaining Cost) distribution? Draw the cost curve shape.
- How long does it take to bring new supply online? (Lithium: 3–5 yrs; copper mine: 10–15 yrs; oil well: 6–24 months)
- Are there structural supply constraints (geography, processing bottlenecks, capex cycles)?
- Country concentration risk — export restrictions possible? (e.g. China graphite, Indonesia nickel ore)

### 3. Demand Structure
- What are the top 3 demand drivers?
- Is demand growing or shrinking? At what annual rate?
- How price-elastic is demand? (Gold jewellery = elastic; semiconductor copper = inelastic)
- Is there a structural demand shift underway? (Energy transition, urbanisation, deglobalisation)
- China's role: % of global demand? Beijing policy sensitivity?

### 4. The Cost Curve (Incentive Price)
The single most important analytical tool in commodity analysis:
- **10th percentile AISC** = floor price (lowest cost producers survive)
- **50th percentile AISC** = mean-reversion target (average production economics)
- **90th percentile AISC** = near-term ceiling (high-cost producers marginal)
- **Incentive price** = cost of production at a greenfield project generating a 15% IRR over 10 years — this is what's required to incentivise new supply investment. It is the commodity's long-run equilibrium.

If spot price >> incentive price → oversupply risk. If spot price << incentive price → supply destruction / underinvestment in new projects.

### 5. Inventory & Physical Balance
- Current global stockpile days of supply (LME, COMEX, SHFE warehouses)
- Inventory trajectory: drawing down or building?
- Is the market in deficit or surplus? By how many days of supply?
- Seasonal inventory patterns
- A market in persistent deficit → structural bull case. A market with rising inventories → bear case.

### 6. Macro Sensitivity
- **USD** — nearly all commodities priced in USD; strong USD = commodity headwind
- **Real interest rates** — most critical for gold (negative real rates = gold bull)
- **Chinese GDP and industrial production** — most critical for base metals and bulk
- **Energy prices** — production input for most metals; high energy = cost support for all metals
- **Inflation** — commodities as real assets; often inflation leading indicators

### 7. Geopolitical & Policy Risk
- OPEC+ discipline and production decisions (oil)
- Critical mineral export controls / trade war risks
- Sanctions on major producers
- Environmental regulation tightening production
- Strategic reserve releases (US SPR for oil)

### 8. Position in the Commodity Cycle
Commodities move in multi-year cycles. Where are we?
- **Early cycle**: Low prices → producer capex cuts → supply destruction → inventory draw → price recovery
- **Mid cycle**: Rising prices → increased producer revenue → gradual capex recovery
- **Late cycle**: High prices → major new supply projects sanctioned → speculative demand → overshoot
- **Downcycle**: Oversupply hits → price falls below AISC → production cuts → reset

Ask: how many years into capex underinvestment are we? What is the pipeline of new supply?

### Lens 2 — Wood Mackenzie / CRU: Cost Curve Analysis

**Wood Mackenzie** and **CRU Group** are the industry standards for cost curve research. This is the equivalent of a Morgan Stanley DCF — it answers "what is fair value?"

**Produce:**
- Full global cost curve (AISC at each production decile)
- 10th / 50th / 90th percentile AISC in USD/unit
- Incentive price calculation (greenfield project IRR model)
- Which producers are in the top quartile (low cost, protected) vs bottom quartile (high cost, vulnerable)?
- Cost curve shift: is technology reducing AISC? Is ore grade declining (cost inflation)?

**Key insight:** When spot price dips below the 90th percentile AISC, that production becomes uneconomic → forced curtailment → floor for price. This is the commodity's margin of safety floor.

### Lens 3 — S&P Global Commodity Insights: Price Forecast Consensus

**S&P Global Commodity Insights** (formerly IHS Markit / Platts) aggregates forecasts from banks, producers, and consultants to produce a consensus view.

**Produce:**
- Consensus 1/2/5-year price forecast with range (high/low)
- Count of bullish vs bearish forecasters
- Identify the outlier thesis (what's the most contrarian credible view?)
- Futures curve: spot vs 12-month vs 5-year — is the curve in contango or backwardation?

**Backwardation vs Contango:**
- **Backwardation** (spot > futures) = market tight now, expected to loosen → near-term bullish signal
- **Contango** (futures > spot) = oversupplied now, expected to tighten → store-and-sell trade, near-term bearish

**Template:**

|-----------------|-----|-----------|------|
| 3-month | | | |

| 2-year | | | |
| 5-year (LT) | | | |

Futures curve shape: Backwardation / Flat / Contango
Market positioning: Net long / Net short (CFTC COT report)

### Lens 6 — IEA / Rystad Energy: Supply-Side Risk & Capex Cycle

**International Energy Agency (IEA)** and **Rystad Energy** are the authorities on energy supply-side dynamics. For metals, Wood Mackenzie and CRU play this role.

**Analyse:**
- Capex cycle: how many years of underinvestment in new supply?
- Project pipeline: what new supply is sanctioned/probable/speculative over 5 years?
- Depletion rates: existing mines/wells declining at what rate per year?
- Lead times: how long from sanction to first production?
- Capital intensity: how much capex per tonne of new production?

**The capex cycle is the most contrarian tool:**
- Extended period of low prices → producers cut capex → supply pipeline shrinks → deficit emerges 3–7 years later
- This is the playbook for copper (2015–2020 underinvestment → 2025–2030 deficit)
- Track: annual capex by major producers as % of revenue — declining = future supply risk

### Lens 7 — Bridgewater / Ray Dalio: Macro & Real Asset Framework

Ray Dalio's framework for commodities as real assets within a macro portfolio:

**The All-Weather Framework:**
- Rising growth + rising inflation → commodities outperform
- Rising growth + falling inflation → equities outperform
- Falling growth + rising inflation → commodities and gold outperform (stagflation)
- Falling growth + falling inflation → bonds outperform

**Produce:**
- Current macro quadrant: identify which of the 4 environments we're in
- Real interest rate level and direction (most critical for gold)
- USD cycle position (DXY): strong/weak/inflecting
- M2 money supply growth: expanding/contracting
- Inflation expectations: rising/falling/anchored
- Global debt-to-GDP: high debt levels = structural gold tailwind (debasement thesis)

**Gold-specific:**
- Gold's "fair value" by Bridgewater's model ≈ (M2 money supply) / (central bank gold holdings + private gold) × historical ratio
- When central banks debase currencies, gold is the release valve
- Real yield proxy: 10-yr TIPS yield — gold inversely correlates ~-0.7 with real yields

**Template:**

| DXY (USD index) | | | |
| US M2 growth YoY | | | |
| CPI YoY | | | |
| Central bank net buying | | | |
| Global debt/GDP | | | |

Macro verdict: Bullish / Neutral / Bearish for real assets

### Lens 8 — CFTC Commitments of Traders: Positioning & Sentiment

The **CFTC COT report** (published every Friday, data as of Tuesday) is the definitive read on speculative positioning in futures markets. No equivalent exists for equities.

**Read:**
- **Managed Money (speculators)** net long/short position → current market sentiment
- **Commercial hedgers** net position → what producers and consumers are doing
- **Net position as % of open interest** → how crowded is the trade?

**Contrarian signals:**
- Speculators at extreme net long → overcrowded, susceptible to washout
- Speculators at extreme net short → coiled spring, short-covering rally potential
- Commercials (producers) heavily hedged → they expect prices to fall
- Commercials (consumers) heavily hedged → they expect prices to rise

**Key sources:**
- CFTC website (free): cftc.gov
- Barchart.com — visual COT charts
- Macrotrends / SentimentTrader — historical context

**Template:**

| Metric | Current | 52-wk High | 52-wk Low | Signal |
|--------|---------|-----------|-----------|--------|
| Managed Money net long (contracts) | | | | |
| Net position as % of OI | | | | |
| Commercials net position | | | | |
| Open interest trend | | | | |
| COT index (0=extreme short, 100=extreme long) | | | | |

Positioning verdict: Extremely bullish / Neutral / Extremely bearish

### Lens 9 — Citadel / Technical Analysis: Price Structure & Entry

Commodity technical analysis uses the same tools as equities but with key additions:

**Standard tools:**
- Trend direction (daily / weekly / monthly)

- Gold:silver ratio on charts — ratio reversals precede metal price moves

### Lens 10 — Seasonal & Calendar Patterns

Commodities have well-documented seasonal patterns, more reliable than equities.

**Gold:**
- January–February: strong (Indian wedding season buying, Lunar New Year)
- June–July: weak (summer doldrums, low jewellery demand)
- August–November: strongest seasonal window historically
- December: positive (year-end central bank rebalancing, gift-buying)

**Silver:**
- Tracks gold but amplified; strong in Q1 and Q3 industrial demand
- Solar panel demand (Q1 installation pre-season) increasingly important

**Oil (Brent/WTI):**
- Q1: weak (end of winter heating season)
- Q2: rising (summer driving season buildup)
- Q3: peak (driving season demand)
- Q4: falling (post-driving season, winter stock build)
- OPEC meeting calendar is a bigger driver than seasonality

**Copper:**
- Chinese New Year = demand hiatus (January–February)
- Post-CNY restocking rally (March–April) historically reliable
- September–November: strong on infrastructure spending approvals

**Lithium / Battery Metals:**
- Q1 soft (EV manufacturers run down inventory post-holidays)
- Q2–Q3 firm (summer EV sales season, OEM inventory build)
- Q4 strong (year-end EV delivery targets by manufacturers)

**Iron Ore:**
- Chinese steel production schedule: typically ramps March–October
- Weak November–February (winter construction slowdown in China)
- Stimulus announcements from PBOC/NPC session (March) are the key event

**Nitrogen / Urea:**
- Northern hemisphere spring planting (Feb–April): strongest demand
- Post-harvest (Sep–Oct): second demand window
- Summer: weakest (between crop seasons)

## Commodity-Specific Deep Dives

### Gold — The Monetary Metal

**Price drivers (in order of importance):**
1. US real interest rates (10yr TIPS yield) — single strongest predictor
2. USD strength (DXY) — inverse relationship
3. Central bank demand — structural since 2022, buying ~1,000t/yr
4. ETF flows — GLD + SLV holdings as sentiment indicator
5. Geopolitical risk premium — spikes, not sustained
6. Indian and Chinese jewellery demand — seasonal, price-elastic

**Valuation anchor:** No dividend, no earnings. Value via:
- Real yield model: gold "fair value" rises as real yields fall
- M2/gold ratio: gold as a share of global money supply
- Gold/oil ratio: historically 15–20 barrels per oz
- Gold/housing ratio, gold/equities ratio (Dow/gold ratio) — cyclical extremes

**Australian-specific:**
- AUD-priced gold = USD gold / AUDUSD
- AUD tends to weaken in risk-off (when gold rises in USD) → double-kicker for AUD-based investors
- Perth Mint gold (PMGOLD.AX) tracks spot minus 0.15% management fee
- Gold ETF in AUD: GOLD.AX (ETFS Physical Gold), PMGOLD.AX (Perth Mint)
- ASX gold miners: NST (Northern Star), EVN (Evolution), NCM (now part of Newmont), RMS (Ramelius)

**Key metrics to track:**
- Gold/Silver ratio (current vs 65–70x historical average)
- GLD ETF holdings (tonnes)
- CFTC managed money net position
- World Gold Council quarterly demand data
- Central bank buying (monthly, IFS data)

**Trusted sources:**
- World Gold Council: gold.org
- Incrementum *In Gold We Trust*: ingoldwetrust.report (annual, May)
- Metals Focus: metalsfocus.com
- CPM Group: cpmgroup.com
- Kitco News: kitco.com (daily commentary)
- MacroVoices podcast (Erik Townsend — macro + metals commentary)

### Silver — The Dual-Metal

**Price drivers:**
1. Gold price (silver trades as a leveraged gold proxy — beta ~1.5x)
2. Industrial demand (solar panels = ~20% of silver demand and growing)
3. Gold/silver ratio mean reversion
4. Mine supply (70% is by-product of zinc, lead, copper mining — low price sensitivity)

**Key insight:** Silver is the only commodity with a structural demand tailwind from both monetary demand (gold proxy) and energy transition (solar PV uses ~100mg silver per panel). Solar demand is growing ~10% per year and the Silver Institute projects a structural deficit from ~2025 onward.

**Solar silver demand:** Each GW of installed solar uses ~50–60t of silver. Global solar installs are targeting 500+ GW/year by 2030 → ~30,000t/year demand vs ~800t mine production for solar → massive structural demand growth story.

---

### Copper — The Economy's Thermometer

**Why copper matters:** Widest applications of any industrial metal. Used in everything from power grids to EVs to construction. Often called "Dr. Copper" — its price predicts global economic health.

**Price drivers:**
1. Chinese GDP and property market (China = ~55% of global demand)
2. Power grid investment (energy transition = most copper-intensive)
3. Mine supply disruptions (Chile/Peru = ~40% of global production; labour strikes, weather, political risk)
4. Scrap supply (recycled copper = ~35% of supply)
5. LME warehouse inventory levels

**Energy transition demand:** An EV uses ~83 kg copper vs ~23 kg for an ICE car. Offshore wind turbines use ~4–6t per MW. A grid to 100% renewables requires ~3× current copper infrastructure. Benchmark projects a 10 million tonne copper supply gap by 2035.

**Valuation:** Incentive price currently ~US$4.50–5.00/lb (new greenfield mine economics). Long-run supply deficit thesis is the strongest in any industrial metal.

### Lithium — The Battery Metal

**Price drivers:**
1. EV sales volumes by region
2. Battery chemistry (LFP vs NMC — lithium content differs)
3. China EV policy (subsidies, purchase mandates)
4. Spodumene vs lithium carbonate/hydroxide spread
5. New mine supply (Australia spodumene → China processing → global supply)

**Key sources:**
- **Benchmark Mineral Intelligence**: benchmarkminerals.com — the definitive source
- **Fastmarkets**: fastmarkets.com — real-time lithium pricing
- **Roskill (Wood Mackenzie)**: critical minerals research
- **Canaccord Genuity ASX lithium research**: strong coverage

**Australian context:**
- Australia is the world's largest spodumene producer (Pilgangoora, Mt Marion, Greenbushes)
- Key ASX stocks: PLS (Pilbara Minerals), IGO (owns 25% Greenbushes stake), LTR (Liontown)
- Spodumene price (6% SC6) is the Australian miner's revenue driver
- China processes ~80% of global lithium into battery-grade chemicals — a critical bottleneck/risk

**Lithium cycle note:** Lithium crashed from US$80k/t LCE (2022 peak) to ~US$10k/t (2024) due to Chinese supply expansion outpacing EV demand growth. This is a classic commodity overshoot. Cost curve support is ~US$10–12k/t; incentive price for new supply is ~US$20–25k/t. Structural deficit thesis intact for 2027+.

### Uranium — The Contrarian Energy Metal

**Why uranium is different:**
- Not traded on a liquid futures exchange (no COMEX/LME contract)
- ~90 uranium producers globally but ~10 control 80% of supply
- Utilities buy on long-term contracts (3–10 years), not spot
- Spot market is thin and volatile — only ~15% of uranium trades spot

**Price drivers:**
1. Nuclear power plant restarts/builds (Japan, Germany reversal, new SMRs)
2. Kazakh production (Kazatomprom = ~45% of global supply)
3. US enrichment policy (anti-Russian uranium sanctions)
4. Utility contracting cycle (when utilities run low on contracted supply, they rush to contract)

**Key sources:**
- Cameco (CCO.TO) investor presentations
- World Nuclear Association: world-nuclear.org
- UxC: uxc.com — specialist uranium price reporting
- Sprott Asset Management uranium commentary

### Oil (Brent/WTI)

**Price drivers:**
1. OPEC+ production decisions — the single most important variable
2. US shale production (EIA weekly data — changes fast)
3. Global demand growth (IEA monthly data)
4. Chinese demand (customs import data monthly)
5. USD strength
6. Geopolitical premium (Middle East, Russia)
7. Strategic reserve policy (US SPR releases)

**Key data cadence:**
- Every Tuesday: API weekly crude inventory report
- Every Wednesday: EIA weekly petroleum report (market-moving)
- Monthly: IEA Oil Market Report, OPEC Monthly Oil Market Report, EIA Short-Term Energy Outlook
- OPEC+ meetings: ~every 2 months — major market event

**Valuation anchor:** OPEC's fiscal breakeven (Saudi Arabia ~US$80/bbl, UAE ~US$65/bbl). OPEC manages production to keep price above Saudi fiscal breakeven. This is the effective price floor.

### Iron Ore — Australia's Export Heartbeat

**Why it matters to Australian investors:**
- Iron ore is Australia's #1 export (~A$130bn/yr)
- BHP, RIO, FMG are the three largest ASX companies — tied to iron ore price
- AUD correlates ~0.6 with iron ore price

**Price drivers:**
1. Chinese steel production volume (monthly NBS data)
2. Chinese property market (floor space starts — iron ore demand proxy)
3. Chinese steel mill profitability (mills buy ore when margins positive)
4. Port inventory in China (45-day average = balanced; >50 days = bearish)
5. Seaborne supply from Australia and Brazil
6. Steel export competitiveness from China

**Key data:**
- Weekly: MySteel port inventory data, steel mill capacity utilisation
- Monthly: China NBS steel production, Customs ore import volumes
- Quarterly: BHP/RIO/FMG production reports (world's best real-time data)

**Valuation reference:**
- BHP's breakeven: ~US$30/t C1 cost at Pilbara
- RIO's breakeven: ~US$20/t C1
- FMG's breakeven: ~US$15/t C1
- Current price ~US$100–110/t → all three are highly profitable
- 62% Fe fines is the benchmark grade; Pilbara Blend is the most-traded grade

### Nitrogen (Urea/Ammonia) — The Food Security Commodity

**Why it matters:** ~50% of the world's food is produced using nitrogen fertilisers derived from natural gas. Urea is the most widely traded form.

**Price drivers:**
1. Natural gas prices (ammonia synthesis = ~70–80% of production cost)
2. Planting season demand (Northern hemisphere spring)
3. China export policy (China = ~30% of global urea exports; intermittently bans exports)
4. Indian government tender prices (India is the world's largest urea importer)
5. Russian/Middle Eastern production costs (low-cost gas)

**Key sources:**
- Fertilizer International magazine
- Green Markets (Bloomberg subsidiary): weekly fertiliser price reporting
- Argus Media: fertiliser price assessments
- IFA (International Fertilizer Association): ifa.asso.fr

## Australian Context — Commodities & the AUD

Australia is a commodity-producing nation. Understanding commodities is essential for understanding:

1. **AUD exchange rate** — AUD is a commodity currency. Iron ore and gold are primary drivers. Strong iron ore → strong AUD. Strong AUD → lower AUD-denominated commodity prices for local holders.

2. **ASX resources sector** — BHP + RIO + FMG are ~15% of ASX 200 market cap. Their share prices are essentially leveraged commodity plays.

3. **Physical gold at Perth Mint** — AUD gold price = USD gold / AUDUSD. You benefit when USD gold rises AND when AUD weakens (which tends to happen simultaneously in risk-off periods — natural hedge).

4. **ASX critical minerals** — Australia holds the world's largest lithium reserves (PLS, IGO, LTR), significant graphite (Syrah, EcoGraf), and rare earths (Lynas). This is an increasingly strategic geopolitical asset.

5. **Terms of trade** — When commodity prices rise, Australia's terms of trade improve, the AUD strengthens, and the RBA has less need to cut rates. Commodity watching is macro watching for Australian investors.
